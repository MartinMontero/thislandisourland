# Epstein file check separates solid facts from errors

The uploaded Epstein research file has a sound backbone and many errors in its detail. The backbone holds: the 2008 non-prosecution agreement (NPA) kept Epstein out of federal court, the Justice Department's ethics office (OPR) found "poor judgment" but no professional misconduct, the Inspector General documented serious failures at the Manhattan jail (MCC), the House Oversight report faulted banks and agencies, and Congress fought over file releases. Against that, the file contains at least a dozen errors a reader could act on. The most serious are three. It credits the 11-women and Blanche material to House Oversight Democrats when it comes from House Judiciary Democrats (report released Oct 26, 2026). It moves the 2025 discharge-petition story (Grijalva, Walkinshaw, Mayes, the Boebert meeting) into 2026 and attaches it to the second transparency bill (EFTA II). And it gives Todd Blanche the title of Attorney General for a July 2025 interview, when he was Deputy Attorney General. The "top vs bottom" framing has support in the record of cross-party votes and actions (the 427-to-1 House vote in 2025, a unanimous committee contempt vote), but no party-by-party polling figure supports it, because the researcher could not open the poll pages. Every party breakdown is therefore NOT VERIFIED. The Boebert/Laubacher result is an election fact (AP called Colorado's 4th District for Eileen Laubacher), and no source read gives a cause. All findings below are as of Nov 13, 2026.

**Method and limits.** This report rests only on the verification notes in the research folder. Verdict labels are the notes' own: CONFIRMED (primary source or two independent reputable outlets), REPORTED (one reputable outlet or an attributed allegation), CONTESTED (sources disagree or the subject denies it), NOT VERIFIED (no adequate source found, which is not the same as false), WRONG (correct fact given). Several primary documents could not be read by the researchers: the OPR report, the House Oversight report PDF, the Judiciary Democrats' PDF, the Guardian pieces the file cites, and every polling page. Where two notes disagree, both figures are given with their attribution. Quotes are kept short. Allegations are shown with the named person's response beside them. Where a death is mentioned, the report states what the official findings say and where reporting says no link to the case has been established.

## 3. What is solidly established about elite impunity

The record supports a limited, specific finding. Powerful or wealthy people and institutions repeatedly got outcomes ordinary defendants would not, and several official bodies have said so. **The 2008 NPA.** Epstein avoided federal charges and pleaded guilty in state court to soliciting prostitution and procuring a minor for prostitution. ## What the check changes

The file's thesis, that elite impunity is real and was documented by official bodies, survives. What fails is the connective tissue: dates, titles, committees, and a few causal links the sources do not make. The two mistakes most likely to damage the site are the Judiciary-versus-Oversight misattribution, which puts allegations against the President under the wrong committee, and the 2025-versus-2026 petition mix-up, which tells readers a House vote on EFTA II is compelled and under way when none has occurred. The "top vs bottom" slogan is best presented as the owner's argument with the evidence for and against beside it. The strongest support is the pattern of cross-party votes and a bipartisan contempt action. The weakest point is the absence of any read party breakdown. Until someone opens the Ipsos, Quinnipiac, PRRI and YouGov crosstabs, the section cannot say how Republicans and Democrats differ on who is to blame, and it should say so.
