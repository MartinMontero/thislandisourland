# Testing the AIPAC claim, sentence by sentence

**As of November 13, 2026.** The owner's position holds in part. AIPAC is a U.S. organization that is not registered under FARA, and its stated and documented agenda is single-issue: U.S. policy toward Israel. Three parts do not hold as written. "Sole goal ... on behalf of Israel" is CONTESTED, because no source in the research shows Israeli direction or funding, and AIPAC denies both. "No other nation has this set-up" fails as a general statement, because other countries and diaspora groups lobby in the U.S. and several foreign governments out-register Israel under FARA. It holds only in a narrower form about scale: AIPAC and its super PAC, United Democracy Project (UDP), dwarf every other country-focused committee on FEC data, and Israel is the top destination for privately sponsored congressional travel. "Foreign interference by another name" is a characterization. The documented Israeli-government influence work of 2024-26 runs through other channels, and no source links it to AIPAC. "Many say AIPAC should register" is accurate as a description of critics, and the Massie bill is the clearest legislative form of it. But practitioners say current law does not require registration, and no 2024-2026 poll asked the question. The narrower wording the evidence supports appears in the verdict section. Labels follow the research notes: CONFIRMED, REPORTED, CONTESTED, NOT VERIFIED.

## FARA turns on foreign direction, and the LDA exemption does not help a foreign-government agent

FARA reaches a person who acts "at the order, request, or under the direction or control" of a foreign principal while engaging in covered activity, including political activity in the U.S. (CONFIRMED, [DOJ text of the Act](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/fara-index-and-act)). The same clause reaches someone acting for an intermediary that a foreign principal "directly or indirectly" supervises, directs, controls, finances or subsidizes in whole or major part. Under § 611(b)(1) a foreign principal is a foreign government or political party. Under § 611(b)(2) a U.S.-organized entity with its principal place of business in the U.S. is excluded from the "person outside the United States" category. AIPAC is therefore not itself a foreign principal. Any obligation would have to come from showing that it acts for the Israeli government, directly or through a foreign-financed intermediary. The notes did not confirm the codified text on uscode.house.gov, so each quoted phrase needs a check against it before publication.

The § 613(h) exemption for lobbyists registered under the Lobbying Disclosure Act covers agents of foreign private persons and entities (§ 611(b)(2)-(3)), not agents of foreign governments (CONFIRMED, [DOJ text](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/fara-index-and-act)). DOJ's FAQ says the exemption "does not apply where a foreign government ... is the principal beneficiary" (CONFIRMED, [DOJ FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/frequently-asked-questions)). AIPAC's LDA registration would not shield it if an agency relationship with the Israeli government were shown. It would not by itself establish one either. The notes list as an open question whether informal coordination short of control can satisfy "request." No DOJ guidance found resolves it.

DOJ has said nothing public about AIPAC. Advisory opinions must concern "an actual event, not a hypothetical situation" and can be requested only by an actual party, so a third party cannot obtain one (CONFIRMED, [DOJ FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/frequently-asked-questions)). No advisory opinion on a comparable domestic diaspora group was located, and the published opinion index was not searched. A February 5, 2025 memo from Attorney General Bondi limits criminal FARA and § 951 charges to conduct "similar to more traditional espionage by foreign government actors" and points the FARA Unit to civil enforcement and guidance (REPORTED, [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/02/attorney-general-announces-shift-in-foreign-agents)). Former FARA Unit chief Brandon Van Grack and co-authors say civil enforcement remains (REPORTED, [Lawfare, Jan. 28, 2026](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-state-of-fara)). The memo is a statement of priorities, not a change in law. Whether it has been revised as of today was not checked. The only DOJ response to an AIPAC registration petition found is a May 18, 2011 letter, reported by the advocacy group IRmep, asking petitioners for "additional information" (REPORTED, [IRmep via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/americans-demand-justice-department-regulate-aipac-as-israeli-foreign-agent-video-documents-122867839.html)). No DOJ complaint or response from 2023-2026 was found.

Scholarship and practitioner commentary lean one way, with thin coverage. FARA lawyer Matthew Sanderson of Caplin & Drysdale, who is not AIPAC counsel, told the Forward that the law is clear that AIPAC need not register. He says regulators look at foreign funding, foreign-government contracts and foreign control of the board, and shared interests alone do not trigger registration (REPORTED, [Forward, June 2025](https://forward.com/news/730423/tucker-carlson-ted-cruz-aipac-foreign-agent/)). Lawfare's January 2026 overview by a former FARA Unit chief does not mention AIPAC (REPORTED, [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-state-of-fara)). The arguments for registration found in this research sit in non-peer-reviewed or student writing, none of it read: an [academia.edu paper](https://www.academia.edu/145727596/From_Foreign_Agent_to_Domestic_Lobby_A_Historical_Analysis_of_the_American_Zionist_Councils_Transformation_to_AIPAC_and_the_Implications_for_Foreign_Agent_Registration_Act_Compliance) and a [College Reporter piece, April 2026](https://www.the-college-reporter.com/aipac-needs-to-register-as-a-foreign-agent-under-federal-law/2026/04/) (both NOT VERIFIED). No Just Security piece or law review article specific to AIPAC was confirmed. Overall the question is CONTESTED in public debate, with practitioners leaning toward non-coverage.

### Calls for registration, 2024-2026

Rep. Thomas Massie's "Americans Insist on Political Agent Clarity Act" (the "AIPAC Act") was introduced May 14, 2026 and referred to House Judiciary (REPORTED, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIPAC_Act); [Military.com, May 18, 2026](https://www.military.com/massie-bill-targets-aipac-with-new-fara-standards-israel)). It is reported as H.R. 8809 (REPORTED, [Common Dreams, May 15, 2026](https://www.commondreams.org/news/thomas-massie-aipac-foreign-agents); not checked on congress.gov). It would let U.S.-organized entities qualify as foreign principals without direct foreign funding or instructions, by asking whether their lobbying "principally" advances a foreign state's interests. It lists factors including advocacy tracking a foreign government's diplomatic goals, coordination with foreign officials, and a name referencing a foreign nation, and it creates a citizen complaint process (REPORTED, [Military.com](https://www.military.com/massie-bill-targets-aipac-with-new-fara-standards-israel)). No cosponsors or Senate companion were reported at introduction. Massie lost his May 2026 primary to Ed Gallrein, and no later action on the bill was found. The bill's design suggests its sponsor thought current FARA text does not reach AIPAC. That is an inference, not a statement the notes confirm him making.

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene posted on August 7, 2025 that AIPAC "needs to register as a foreign lobbyist by U.S. law," after AIPAC criticized her remarks on Gaza. The post did not name FARA (REPORTED, [Just the News, Aug. 8, 2025](https://justthenews.com/government/congress/mtg-says-aipac-should-register-foreign-lobbyist-after-pushback-gaza-genocide)). On the other side, Sen. Ted Cruz told Tucker Carlson on June 18, 2025: "Does Israel direct AIPAC? No, they're not lobbying on behalf of them" (REPORTED, [Algemeiner](https://www.algemeiner.com/2025/06/18/ted-cruz-defends-aipac-foreign-influence-claims-accuses-tucker-carlson-antisemitism/)). Rep. Rashida Tlaib calling for registration is NOT VERIFIED and should not be attributed. Other members and candidates in 2023-2026 were not systematically identified.

## History: a 1962 request to AIPAC's predecessor, never enforced

Note on sources: the history rests partly on IRmep (israellobby.org), an advocacy group critical of AIPAC that hosts DOJ files released under FOIA in 2008. The research read IRmep's chronology, not the scans.

AIPAC descends from the American Zionist Council (AZC). The AZC was founded in 1949 as a tax-exempt umbrella for nine Zionist organizations. Isaiah (Si) Kenen organized a lobbying arm, the American Zionist Committee for Public Affairs, in 1951. The AZC divested it in 1954, reportedly so tax-exempt funds would not pay for lobbying, and it was renamed AIPAC in 1959 (REPORTED, [AZC entry, Wikipedia mirror](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/American_Zionist_Council), citing Nelson 1980 and Rossinow 2018). Historian Kobby Barda gives March 1954 for formation and 1958 for the rename, so the date is CONTESTED (Barda, [*Modern Judaism* 46(2), May 2026](https://academic.oup.com/mj/article/46/2/103/8707527)). IRmep says AIPAC was incorporated on January 2, 1963, the month Jewish Agency funding of the AZC stopped (REPORTED, [israellobby.org](https://israellobby.org/AZCDOJ)). The dates mark different events and are not strictly contradictory. The inference that AIPAC was set up to avoid FARA is IRmep's framing, not something a document establishes. Kenen worked for both bodies until July 1960 by his own account, so the personnel overlap is documented. A funding link to AIPAC is not.

The Fulbright hearings put Jewish Agency money on the record. At the August 1, 1963 hearing, Senator J. William Fulbright argued that groups funded by the Jewish Agency fell under FARA and that the Agency may have used the AZC and others as conduits (CONFIRMED as to date and testimony, [JTA, Aug. 2, 1963](https://www.jta.org/archive/senate-foreign-relations-committee-holds-hearing-on-jewish-agency)). JTA reports the Agency paid the AZC **$5,100,001.02** from 1955 to 1962, about 80% of the Council's funds (REPORTED). Barda gives **$1.5 million** for the same period, so the figure is CONTESTED. The two may measure different things, but that was not confirmed. Agency officials testified that they had complied with every Justice Department request, and counsel disputed that the Agency was an Israeli government agent (REPORTED, [JTA](https://www.jta.org/archive/senate-foreign-relations-committee-holds-hearing-on-jewish-agency)). Kenen's affidavit said AIPAC "had no connection" with the AZC or the Agency, and JTA noted these claims were unverified (REPORTED, [JTA, Aug. 6, 1963](https://www.jta.org/archive/fulbright-informed-kenen-quit-american-zionist-council-employ-in-1960)). The hearings established large Agency funding of the AZC, not Agency money reaching AIPAC. No committee report with formal findings was located, and the official transcript was not retrieved.

According to IRmep's reading of DOJ files, a November 21, 1962 DOJ order, approved by Robert Kennedy, said Jewish Agency funding made the Council an agent of a foreign principal and that its "registration is requested" (REPORTED, [israellobby.org](https://israellobby.org/AZCDOJ)). The AZC resisted. Counsel filed a legal memorandum, DOJ issued a 72-hour demand in October 1963, and the AZC refused on December 11, 1963 while offering voluntary disclosure. DOJ accepted an itemized statement for a sample period, and the case was closed on May 14, 1965 with no registration and no prosecution. Wikipedia summarizes the demand as "later withdrawn," and the AZC dissolved in 1966; both describe the same non-enforcement. The order applied to the AZC, not to AIPAC. Barda concludes AIPAC "continued to operate as a domestic lobbying organization without registering under FARA" (CONFIRMED as to non-registration). The exact wording of the order and the signer are NOT VERIFIED against the original scans.

Later episodes show alignment and access, not documented direction. In the Rosen/Weissman case, two AIPAC officials were accused of passing classified information from Pentagon analyst Larry Franklin to an Israeli diplomat, among others. Franklin pleaded guilty. AIPAC fired the two in 2005. On May 1, 2009 prosecutors moved to dismiss, citing Judge T.S. Ellis III's added intent requirement and the likelihood that classified information would be disclosed at trial (CONFIRMED, [Forward, May 1, 2009](https://forward.com/news/105479/prosecutors-ask-judge-to-throw-out-case-against-ai/); [Al Jazeera, May 2, 2009](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2009/5/2/us-to-drop-israel-lobbyist-spy-case)). No AIPAC employee was convicted, and no court or agency found AIPAC acted as an Israeli agent. Defenders read the dismissal as vindication and critics read the underlying facts as a conduit role. Indictment date, sentencing details and the diplomat's name were from background knowledge only (NOT VERIFIED).

In October 1992, AIPAC president David Steiner resigned after a secretly recorded call published by *The Washington Times*. He had claimed a deal with Secretary of State James Baker and negotiations with the Clinton campaign. Steiner said "There were no 'deals' with Secretary Baker," and AIPAC said it had no role in the aid talks (CONFIRMED, [JTA, Nov. 5, 1992](https://jta.org/archive/aipac-president-quits-after-boasting-of-influence-over-baker-and-clinton)). The tape shows a leader claiming influence, which both he and AIPAC retracted. It does not document Israeli direction.

In 2015, Ambassador Ron Dermer arranged Prime Minister Netanyahu's March 3 address to Congress with Speaker Boehner, reportedly without consulting AIPAC; the date coincided with AIPAC's policy conference (CONFIRMED as to dates, REPORTED as to AIPAC being bypassed, [JTA, Jan. 27, 2015](https://www.jta.org/2015/01/27/politics/netanyahus-planned-speech-roiling-jewish-lawmakers-pro-israel-community)). AIPAC then launched a separate 501(c)(4), Citizens for a Nuclear Free Iran, with ad plans of about $20M and expenses listed at $20-30M (REPORTED, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_for_a_Nuclear_Free_Iran)). The "$40 million" figure sometimes cited is unsupported there and CONTESTED. The campaign failed. The notes found no record of AIPAC staff coordinating the 2015 campaign with Israeli officials.

Al Jazeera's "The Lobby - USA," filmed in 2016 and never broadcast, was published by Electronic Intifada on November 2, 2018 (CONFIRMED, advocacy outlet, [Electronic Intifada](https://electronicintifada.net/content/watch-film-israel-lobby-didnt-want-you-see/25876)). It mainly shows the Israel on Campus Coalition describing coordination with Israel's Ministry of Strategic Affairs. AIPAC appears marginally, mainly through Eric Gallagher, then at The Israel Project, who says he formerly worked for AIPAC (REPORTED). Why it was withheld is CONTESTED: Al Jazeera cited legal issues, and Haaretz reported lobby sources saying Qatar had promised it would not air, which Qatar denied. The film's coordination evidence concerns the ICC and the ministry, not AIPAC. For 2020-2026 the notes found no court record, government finding or primary document showing AIPAC taking direction from Israeli officials. DAWN's May 20, 2026 count of 7 former AIPAC staffers now in Israeli government bodies and 6 current staffers who once worked for it rests on voluntary LinkedIn data and does not establish coordination (REPORTED, advocacy data, [DAWN](https://dawnmena.org/u-s-new-data-highlights-aipac-ties-to-the-u-s-israeli-governments/)).

## The "sole goal" test: single-issue scope holds, agency does not follow from it

AIPAC describes its aim as to "encourage and persuade the U.S. government to enact specific policies" supporting the U.S.-Israel relationship. It says it is "funded exclusively by Americans" and "not funded nor directed by the Israeli government" (CONFIRMED as self-description, [AIPAC FAQs, Jan. 1, 2026](https://www.aipac.org/memos/aipac-faqs); [AIPAC money page](https://aipac.org/aipac-money)). No audit confirms the funding claim, and Form 990s do not list donors (NOT VERIFIED independently). AIPAC is a 501(c)(4), EIN 53-0217164, and its revenue was $78.3M in FY2021, $156.5M in FY2024 and $122.5M in FY2025 (CONFIRMED, [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/530217164)). Spokesman Marshall Wittmann called it "a single-issue organization" in March 2022 (CONFIRMED, [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-700701)). The aipac.org/about page and the separate 990s for AIPAC PAC, UDP and the American Israel Education Foundation were not reviewed.

On legislation, every bill identified in AIPAC's disclosures and press output concerns Israel, Iran or Middle East security. In April 2024 AIPAC hailed Congress's $14.3B emergency supplemental as "the largest U.S. security aid package in Israel's history," though the supplemental also carried Ukraine and Indo-Pacific funding (CONFIRMED, [AIPAC, Apr. 23, 2024](https://aipac.org/press-release/america-stands-with-israel-congress-approves-historic-aid-package)). Its amended Q4 2025 LD-2 reports $973,910 spent, 10 in-house lobbyists, and bills including the Enhanced Iran Sanctions Act of 2025 (H.R.1422/S.556) and the FY2026 national security and State appropriations bill (REPORTED, [Legis1, Sept. 29, 2026](https://legis1.com/news/aipac-lobbying-iran-sanctions-lobbies-defense)). A Legis1 summary of its filings splits issue areas as Foreign Relations 26%, Budget/Appropriations 22%, Defense 19%, Energy/Nuclear 10%, Trade 9% and Homeland Security 7% (REPORTED, [Legis1, Oct. 20, 2025](https://legis1.com/news/aipac-lobbies-congress-middle-east-policy)). What the Trade and Homeland Security entries contain was not identified. The LD-2 filings themselves were not opened. AIPAC's annual lobbying spend is reported at $3.3M for 2024 and $3.1M for 2023 (REPORTED, [JNS, Jan. 22, 2025](https://www.jns.org/us-jewish-groups-spent-more-some-exponentially-so-lobbying-in-2024-than-prior-year/)). Lobbying on the Antisemitism Awareness Act is NOT VERIFIED; the one source attributes a lobbying day to the Jewish Federations of North America.

On military policy, AIPAC praised the February 28, 2026 U.S. strikes on Iran, calling Iran a "terror-supporting regime" (CONFIRMED, [AIPAC release](https://aipac.org/press-release/american-strikes-on-iran)). On Iraq in 2003 the record is CONTESTED. Wittmann told the New York Times in 2015 that AIPAC "took no position whatsoever on the Iraq war." LobeLog cites a January 2003 *New York Sun* quote from Howard Kohr that AIPAC "quietly" lobbied Congress for the use-of-force resolution, and Jeffrey Goldberg reporting in 2005 that it lobbied for the war though Iraq was not a chief priority (REPORTED, [LobeLog, Aug. 6, 2015](https://lobelog.com/aipac-bristles-at-obamas-reminder-of-iraq-war-lobbying/); the primary texts were not opened). Both can be true if "position" means formal policy.

AIPAC says it acts on Americans' views about U.S. interests, and nothing reviewed shows Israeli funding or direction.

## The "no other nation" test: scale and travel hold, uniqueness fails

Other diaspora lobbies exist, and none outside the pro-Israel space comes close in election money. FEC totals from the November 13 check show no country- or diaspora-focused committee outside the pro-Israel space spending more than about $0.5M in any cycle. Examples: Pakistani American Public Affairs Committee $0.44M (2026), Hindu American PAC $0.10M (2024), Iranian American PAC $0.09M, Turkish American Coalition PAC $0.08M (2026), U.S.-Cuba Democracy PAC about $0.06M (2022) and near zero by 2026. The Armenian and Greek PACs found are terminated or returned no 2022-2026 filings (CONFIRMED as FEC reports). One conflict: the separate Impact Fund super-PAC account (C00876391), which the Chrome table did not list, raised $3.38M and made $2.84M in independent expenditures in 2024 and, through October 14, 2026, raised $2.0M with $1.43M in IEs (CONFIRMED, [FEC C00876391 2024](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00876391/?cycle=2024)). So the "about $0.5M" ceiling holds only if that committee is excluded, and it is an Indian-American voter-mobilisation group, not a lobby on India's foreign policy. Other findings: American Priorities, a pro-Palestinian super PAC funded mostly by Arab American donors, had raised about $5.4M by June 2026 (REPORTED, [Jewish Insider, June 12, 2026](https://jewishinsider.com/2026/06/anti-israel-left-takes-a-page-from-aipac-in-spending-big-in-primaries/)); its FEC totals are NOT VERIFIED. No lobbying dollar figures were verified for any non-Israel group because the lobbying databases were unreachable, and FARA status for those groups is NOT VERIFIED, since the FARA database was not searched.

Within the pro-Israel space, UDP and AIPAC PAC ($85M and $56M disbursed in 2026) are several times larger than every other committee combined (CONFIRMED, FEC totals). The next largest are RJC Victory Fund ($16.7M disbursed in 2024; $6.6M in 2026), J Street PAC ($14.5M in 2024, mostly conduit) and DMFI PAC ($6.2M in 2024). J Street's Action Fund reported $3.4M raised in 2026, and J Street said it spent more than $3M against five AIPAC-backed candidates (REPORTED, self-reported, [J Street, Nov. 4, 2026](https://jstreet.org/press/midterm-results-are-resounding-victory-new-politics-israel-and-palestine)). The Intercept says new pro-Palestine groups' "pockets aren't nearly as deep" (REPORTED).

OpenSecrets counts lobbying, influence, tourism and trade promotion and notes a payment lag. The press figures differ. The Washington Examiner's OpenSecrets analysis gives 2024 as Japan $48.5M and China $32.9M, with Saudi Arabia, China and South Korea outspending Israel and Qatar ([Denver Gazette, July 28, 2025](https://www.denvergazette.com/2025/07/28/foreigners-spend-billions-to-influence-us-politics-heres-whos-cutting-the-biggest-checks-d8d5a134-bc03-513a-8fad-968feb201536/)). Everything-PR gives Israel $214.6M for 2016-2025 (REPORTED, [Everything-PR](https://everything-pr.com/foreign-influence-pr-study-2026)). Quincy's "Eighth Front" paper says Israeli clients have spent more than $100M under FARA since 2023 and that Israel is on track to lead in 2026, which is a projection (REPORTED, [Quincy, July 24, 2026](https://quincyinst.org/research/the-eighth-front-inside-israels-1-billion-influence-campaign/)). Quincy's separate "more than $1B" figure covers Israeli public-diplomacy spending since October 2023, not FARA, and Quincy calls it "likely" spending. These cannot be squared with OpenSecrets' $19.3M for 2025; the notes flag possible OpenSecrets lag or intermediary recording (such as Havas), unverified. On any of these sources, Israel's FARA total through 2025 is large but not first. FARA figures also miss unregistered actors.

On travel, Israel is the top destination for privately sponsored congressional trips. A Howard Center/POLITICO database found Israel No. 1, with more trips than the entire Western Hemisphere and Africa combined. About 75% of Israel trips were sponsored by AIPAC's affiliate, the American Israel Education Foundation (AIEF), and AIPAC-linked travel cost at least $10M for 2012-2023 per LegiStorm. J Street was second among Israel trip sponsors, and AIPAC's volume was nearly seven times larger (REPORTED, [POLITICO via Yahoo, Oct. 30, 2024](https://www.yahoo.com/news/aipac-paid-hundreds-lawmakers-visit-090000842.html)). In the first nine months of 2025 Israel accounted for 156 accepted House gift-travel invitations, over a quarter of the total, and the UK was second at under half Israel's count; AIEF share is about 60% in that source (REPORTED, [Mondoweiss, Dec. 9, 2025](https://mondoweiss.net/2025/12/inside-the-israel-lobbys-lesser-known-tool-for-influencing-congress-free-travel/)). An AIEF staff trip from May 24 to June 1, 2025 took 12 House staffers at $225,565 total (CONFIRMED, [LegiStorm](https://www.legistorm.com/trip/group/16168/sort/full_sponsor/type/asc.html)). Trip counts by sponsor for other countries' foundations were not retrieved.

The research supports this: the broad claim fails, and a scale claim holds. Defenders say AIPAC is a domestic citizen lobby like ANCA or the Cuban American National Foundation, only larger. Critics say scale and coordination with a foreign government's priorities make it different in kind. The notes conclude that the evidence supports "different in scale" and does not settle "different in kind." The academic literature (Mearsheimer and Walt, Paul and Anderson) was not read.

## Israeli government influence operations run through separate, mostly registered channels

The documented Israeli-government efforts are real, and none is linked to AIPAC in any source reviewed. STOIC, an Israeli commercial firm, ran a fake-account campaign aimed at U.S. audiences. OpenAI's May 30, 2024 report says it disrupted the activity and does not attribute it to the Israeli government; Meta also removed linked accounts (CONFIRMED by the platforms, [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-deceptive-uses-of-AI-by-covert-influence-operations/); [NPR, July 9, 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/07/09/nx-s1-4994027/israel-us-online-influence-campaign)). The New York Times reported on June 5, 2024 that Israel's Ministry of Diaspora Affairs paid STOIC about $2 million; Diaspora Minister Amichai Chikli denied the reports (REPORTED by NPR's account of the Times, with the ministry's denial, so CONTESTED). Lee Fang and Jack Poulson, with The Guardian, reported the ministry spent about NIS 32M (about $8.6M) from October 2023 to May 2024, funding U.S. groups including Christians United for Israel through Voices of Israel, none registered under FARA, with AIPAC not named (REPORTED, [Lee Fang, June 24, 2024](https://www.leefang.com/p/israeli-documents-show-expansive)).

Since 2025, Israel's Foreign Ministry has used Havas Media (Germany) to hire U.S. firms that registered under FARA. Clock Tower X (FARA #7649, Brad Parscale) is listed with the foreign principal "State of Israel via Havas Media Germany GmbH" (CONFIRMED, [FARA eFile index](https://efile.fara.gov/api/v1/RegDocs/html/7649); the per-document dates showed an anomaly and are NOT VERIFIED). It registered September 18, 2025 under a $6M contract aimed at Gen Z platforms (REPORTED, [Sludge, Oct. 6, 2025](https://readsludge.com/2025/10/06/israels-new-u-s-influence-campaigns-target-tiktok-churches-and-chatgpt/)). Sludge reported expansion to $9M in December, and a May 2026 supplemental reportedly shows $15M received (REPORTED, [Daily Caller, June 24, 2026](https://dailycaller.com/2026/06/24/brad-parscale-error-fara-filing/)). The amounts are CONTESTED across successive amendments and were not reconciled against filings. VP JD Vance said in July 2026 that an Israeli-government-contracted firm ran a "very discreet, extremely well-funded campaign" against Iran negotiations. Parscale replied that claims he attacked the administration are false and that his work is disclosed (CONTESTED, [Washington Examiner, July 16, 2026](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/?p=4651470)). Other contracts include Bridges Partners' influencer program, up to $900,000 (REPORTED, [Responsible Statecraft](https://responsiblestatecraft.org/israel-influencers)), an SKDK deal of $600,000 terminated August 29, 2025 (REPORTED, [Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2025/10/06/israels-new-u-s-influence-campaigns-target-tiktok-churches-and-chatgpt/)), and Show Faith by Works LLC, a church outreach contract of about $4M (REPORTED, [Dallas Express via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/federal-filing-details-israeli-contract-125045333.html)). Public Citizen filed a FARA complaint about influencers; its date and content are NOT VERIFIED.

These contracts are on the public FARA register. That fits the notes' inference that FARA catches government-directed work when the principal pays for it. Across NPR's STOIC coverage, the Lee Fang documents, Marc Owen Jones's roundup, the Daily Caller and Sludge, the notes found **no documented link to AIPAC, UDP or AIPAC-backed candidates** (REPORTED). Absence from these sources is not proof none exists. FARA "persons contacted" lists were not searched, and Drop Site, Zeteo and other outlets were not checked. Overlapping goals are not coordination, and neither the press nor the FARA record has shown coordination.

## Counterarguments: AIPAC's position, legal experts, dual-loyalty concerns and the "same structure" argument

AIPAC's position is that it is "an American organization" with American funding, American board members and no foreign direction (REPORTED, [AIPAC FAQs](https://aipac.org/memos/aipac-faqs); quoted by [DAWN](https://dawnmena.org/u-s-new-data-highlights-aipac-ties-to-the-u-s-israeli-governments/)). UDP spokesperson Patrick Dorton called UDP "an American organization made up of millions of pro-Israel American voters" ([NPR, Aug. 13, 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/13/nx-s1-5923565/aipac-israel-primary-elections)). The Forward reports AIPAC receives no Israeli government money and has no Israeli officials on its board. No formal AIPAC statement answering FARA demands was located. Sanderson calls the contrary reading a "bad faith interpretation" and says the foreign government must be "directing and controlling" the U.S. organization (REPORTED, [Forward](https://forward.com/news/730423/tucker-carlson-ted-cruz-aipac-foreign-agent/)). Cruz called Carlson's claim "not only not true," and the Forward records that critics' petitions have failed.

The historical counterweight is the 1962 AZC episode: foreign funding, not shared goals, triggered DOJ's request, and the American Council for Judaism says the AZC received "virtually all of its operating funds" from the Jewish Agency (REPORTED, [ACJ](https://acjna.org/articles/aipacs-new-political-role-raises-old-questions-about-foreign-agent-registration/)). Public Citizen's Craig Holman says that when a group lobbies at a foreign government's behest, "nearly all the exemptions are overridden," but he spoke about Israeli-funded groups in general, not AIPAC (REPORTED, [Lee Fang](https://www.leefang.com/p/israeli-documents-show-expansive)). The notes conclude the legal question is evidentiary: registration would require proof of Israeli direction, control or funding, and none has been produced publicly for AIPAC today.

On dual loyalty, the ADL (August 19, 2026, updated November 4, 2026) calls dual loyalty a "longstanding" antisemitic trope and lists a candidate's reference to "AIPAC-paid Israeli foreign agents" among antisemitic statements, while noting criticism of Israeli policy is not itself the problem ([ADL](https://www.adl.org/resources/article/antisemitic-themes-punctuate-2026-election-cycle-nationwide)). Van Jones said on NPR that criticizing AIPAC is fine but calling it a foreign front is "just not true" ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/13/nx-s1-5923565/aipac-israel-primary-elections)). Some Jewish voices argue the opposite. The American Council for Judaism argues AIPAC was created to take Israel advocacy "off the FARA register," and quotes M.J. Rosenberg that it should register "as what it is: a foreign agent" ([ACJ](https://acjna.org/articles/aipacs-new-political-role-raises-old-questions-about-foreign-agent-registration/); [Forward, 2018](https://forward.com/opinion/395676/its-time-for-aipac-to-register-as-a-foreign-agent)). A 2018 rebuttal said AIPAC members stand with Israel "as Americans, not foreign agents." IfNotNow campaigned against AIPAC endorsements, but a FARA-specific demand from IfNotNow or JVP is NOT VERIFIED, as is any Jewish Federations of North America statement and the contents of AJC's July 2026 candidate guide.

On "other lobbies use the same legal structure": Bethany Mandel wrote that "American donors giving money to an American organization is not foreign influence" (REPORTED, [Washington Examiner, Aug. 9, 2026](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/?p=4679655); paywalled). Brown's Dany Bahar says AIPAC's win rate is "pretty average," similar to the NRA and Planned Parenthood, and OpenSecrets' Brendan Glavin says local-sounding affiliated super PACs are common ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/13/nx-s1-5923565/aipac-israel-primary-elections)). The Forward notes other diaspora-linked groups are also unregistered. The FEC check supports the structural parallel in part: U.S.-organized diaspora groups (Turkish, Indian, Iranian, Hindu American and others) run PACs without a FARA registration found. But FARA status for these groups is NOT VERIFIED, and no sourced comparison of AIPAC's 501(c)(4)/PAC/super PAC form against ANCA, CANF or others was found. The parallel is plausible but not sourced as fact.

## Verdict, sentence by sentence

**Sentence 1: "AIPAC is a U.S. organization not registered as a foreign agent whose sole goal is to influence U.S. legislation, elections and military policy on behalf of Israel."** Partly supported. U.S. organization: supported (501(c)(4), EIN 53-0217164). Not registered as a foreign agent: supported by reporting and Barda's account; the FARA database was not searched directly, so label it REPORTED. "Sole goal": supported as to scope. AIPAC calls itself single-issue, and every identified bill, endorsement and statement concerns Israel, Iran or Middle East security, including election spending and praise for the February 2026 strikes on Iran. Two caveats: the content of the Trade and Homeland Security lobbying entries is unknown, and the October 21, 2026 change means AIPAC no longer endorses or gives PAC money directly. "On behalf of Israel": CONTESTED. AIPAC says it acts for the U.S.-Israel relationship as Americans, and no source shows Israeli direction or funding.

**Sentence 2: "No other nation has this set-up."** Not supported as written. Other diaspora groups have 501(c)(3)/(c)(4) bodies and PACs, and several governments register more FARA spending than Israel through 2025. Supported in narrower form: no other country-focused U.S. political operation matches the AIPAC/UDP election spending, and no other country draws comparable privately sponsored congressional travel. The pro-Israel space is the only one with committees above a few million dollars, with the Impact Fund as the one exception noted above.

**Sentence 3: "It is foreign interference by another name."** Not supported as a factual statement, and CONTESTED as a characterization. FARA requires agency, and no public evidence of Israeli order, direction, control or funding of AIPAC has been produced. The 1962 request applied to AIPAC's predecessor over documented Jewish Agency funding, not to AIPAC. Documented Israeli-government influence work (STOIC, Havas-routed contracts) has no reported link to AIPAC. The owner may argue that alignment of goals, personnel movement, access and scale amount to influence in practice, and the notes document alignment and personnel movement (DAWN, advocacy data). That is a values-based argument and should be labeled as the owner's view. The ADL and others also warn that "foreign agent" framing of a U.S. Jewish-led organization raises dual-loyalty concerns, while the ACJ and others reject that reading.

**Sentence 4: "Many say AIPAC and those it funds should be accountable under FARA."** Supported in part. Named voices say so: Rep. Greene (without naming FARA), Rep. Massie's bill, IRmep and the American Council for Judaism, M.J. Rosenberg and others. "Many" cannot be tied to current polling, since no 2024-2026 poll asked, and the only registration poll is from about 2004. "Those it funds" is not supported by any source in the notes: Massie's bill and the registration calls concern AIPAC itself, and the notes do not examine candidates AIPAC's super PAC supports under FARA.

### Narrower wording the evidence supports

"AIPAC is a U.S.-incorporated, American-funded (by its own account) advocacy organization that is not registered under FARA. Its stated and documented focus is U.S. policy toward Israel. With its super PAC, United Democracy Project, it has been the largest country-focused election spender on FEC data in the 2022, 2024 and 2026 cycles, and its affiliate sponsors most of the privately funded congressional trips to Israel. No public evidence shows Israel directs or funds AIPAC. Some members of Congress and advocacy groups argue it should register as a foreign agent, and a 2026 House bill would change the law to allow that. FARA lawyers cited in the press say current law does not require it."

## Conclusion

The record separates two questions the owner's wording blends: how big and how focused AIPAC is, and who directs it. The first has strong, partly conflicting numbers, now with a 2026 change in how the money flows. The second has no public evidence either way, only AIPAC's denial and the practitioner view that FARA turns on foreign control. Massie's bill, which would define the problem through "principally advances" and not through control, is itself a signal that the policy argument is for new law, not enforcement of the existing text. One FARA contrast is useful for the site: Israeli-government contracts since 2025 are on the public register, so the unregistered-influence concern attaches to AIPAC only if direction is shown.
