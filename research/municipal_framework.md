# US Municipal Government Framework: Fact-Check of Structural Statistics and 2026 Events

Fact-check date: November 8, 2026. Verdict key: CONFIRMED / CORRECTED / UNVERIFIED / FALSE. Access limits I hit: census.gov PDFs could only be read through a text-extraction fetch tool (direct download was blocked by the proxy). opensecrets.org was blocked outright. The Boulder County, Alaska Division of Elections, Alaska Beacon and ADN results pages either failed to load or had not posted 2026 results yet.

## A. Forms of government (ICMA)

### Takeaway
Several ICMA figures check out exactly: 27.2% women, 32.0% ward/district, 85.9% compensate, $61,723 / $9,286, and 10,969 governments. The "54% over 10,000 / 59% over 100,000" pair could not be traced to any ICMA source. The 38% mayor-council figure is real. The "nearly 10% more efficient" line comes from a 2011 IBM benchmarking study, not a peer-reviewed study. The bond-rating claim comes from an ICMA count of Moody's Aaa-rated cities, which shows correlation only.

### Cited Findings
- **Nearly 11,000 local governments of 2,500+: CONFIRMED.** The ICMA 2018-19 snapshot universe is 10,969 local governments with at least 2,500 residents. — [ICMA Survey Research Snapshot: MFOG](https://icma.org/sites/default/files/Survey%20Research%20Snapshot_MFOG.pdf)
- **Real shares, 2018 MFOG (universe of 10,969):** Council-manager 4,386 (40.0%), mayor-council 4,166 (38.0%), commission 1,302 (11.9%), town meeting 1,056 (9.6%), representative town meeting 59 (0.5%). — [ICMA Snapshot](https://icma.org/sites/default/files/Survey%20Research%20Snapshot_MFOG.pdf)
  - Caution on the snapshot: commission at 11.9% is far above the 3.2% that survey respondents reported (next bullet). The snapshot's universe may use a different classification. Cite both and label which is which.
- **2018 MFOG survey respondents (Q1, n=4,020):** Council-manager 48.2%, mayor-council 38.2%, town meeting 8.1%, commission 3.2%, representative town meeting 2.3%. Survey mailed to 12,761 municipalities, with 4,109 responses (32.2% response rate). — [ICMA 2018 MFOG Survey Report](https://icma.org/sites/default/files/2018%20Municipal%20Form%20of%20Government%20Survey%20Report.pdf)
- **Mayor-council 38%: CONFIRMED** (38.0% of the universe; 38.2% of respondents). — same sources.
- **Council-manager 54% (10,000+) and 59% (100,000+): UNVERIFIED.** No ICMA document I found gives these figures. Closest real data:
  - ICMA's 2012 Municipal Year Book table works out to about 57% council-manager among 10,000+ cities (1,883 of 3,311) and about 58% among 100,000+ cities (144 of 248). That is my arithmetic from ICMA's counts.
  - Breaking 100,000+ down by size: 66% at 100,000–249,999, 44% at 250,000–499,999, 30% at 500,000–1M, 33% above 1M. — [ICMA Form of Government Statistics, Cities (2012)](https://icma.org/sites/default/files/303819_Form%20of%20Government%20Statistics%20-%20Cities%20%282012%29%20-%20Council-Manager%20versus%20Mayor-Council%20%28Specific%20Population%20Ranges%29.pdf)
  - Ballotpedia cites ICMA's 2011 survey summary for "approximately 59 percent of cities" using council-manager. That figure covers all US cities, not cities over 100,000. — [Ballotpedia: Council-manager](https://ballotpedia.org/Council-manager)
  - Recommendation: drop 54%/59% or replace them with the sourced 2012 figures, labelled "2012 data."
- **"Nearly 10% more efficient": CORRECTED (source attribution).** ICMA cites "Smarter, Faster, Cheaper," a 2011 benchmarking study by IBM Global Business Services. It used public data on 100 of the largest US cities and found council-manager cities "nearly 10 percent more efficient" than mayor-council cities. It is a vendor benchmarking report, not peer-reviewed research. — [ICMA blog: Professional management drives efficiency](https://icma.org/blog-posts/professional-management-drives-localgov-efficiency-effectiveness)
- **"Higher bond ratings": CORRECTED (overstated).** ICMA's August 4, 2016 review found that 66% of cities with Moody's Aaa rating employ a professional manager, up from 61% in a 2009 review. A second ICMA post puts it as more than 66% of 179 Aaa-rated municipalities. This shows correlation, not that the council-manager form causes higher ratings. — [ICMA: Bond ratings better in cities where life is well run (2016)](https://icma.org/blog-posts/bond-ratings-better-cities-where-life-well-run)
- **About 32% elect some or all of the council by ward or district: CONFIRMED.**
  - Report breakdown (Q26, n=3,855): all at-large 68.0%, all by ward/district 18.4%, a mix 13.6%. The last two sum to 32.0%. — [ICMA 2018 Report](https://icma.org/sites/default/files/2018%20Municipal%20Form%20of%20Government%20Survey%20Report.pdf)
  - The snapshot states 32.0% directly. — [ICMA Snapshot](https://icma.org/sites/default/files/Survey%20Research%20Snapshot_MFOG.pdf)
- **Women hold 27.2% of council seats: CONFIRMED** (2018 data; 22,509 council members in 3,878 governments). — [ICMA 2018 Report](https://icma.org/sites/default/files/2018%20Municipal%20Form%20of%20Government%20Survey%20Report.pdf); [ICMA blog, July 29, 2019](https://icma.org/blog-posts/municipal-form-government-numbers)
- **85.9% compensate elected officials: CONFIRMED** (snapshot figure: salary, per diem or other). — [ICMA Snapshot](https://icma.org/sites/default/files/Survey%20Research%20Snapshot_MFOG.pdf)
  - The full report gives two separate figures: 86.0% for the chief elected official and 83.7% for council members. — [ICMA 2018 Report](https://icma.org/sites/default/files/2018%20Municipal%20Form%20of%20Government%20Survey%20Report.pdf)
- **Full-time mayors $61,723, part-time $9,286: CONFIRMED** (2018 survey averages). — [ICMA "Municipal Form of Government by the Numbers," Rebecca DeSantis, July 29, 2019](https://icma.org/blog-posts/municipal-form-government-numbers)
  - These are means. The snapshot reports medians, for example $69,322 for full-time mayors in mayor-council cities. Do not mix the two.

### Inferences
- All ICMA structural numbers are from the 2018 survey. ICMA publishes MFOG surveys roughly every five years. A 2023/24 edition may exist, but I did not find one, so label every figure "2018."

### Gaps
- I found no source for 54%/59%.
- The snapshot's 11.9% commission share conflicts with the survey's 3.2%. I could not resolve which classification the snapshot uses.

## C. Missoula County, February 2026 cancellations

### Takeaway
Confirmed event, but the prototype mixes two groups. Missoula Irrigation District (Division 2) candidates were elected by acclamation. Missoula Rural Fire District and Turah Meadows Sewer and Water District also had elections canceled, but because no candidates filed. Those seats were left vacant for appointment, not filled by acclamation.

### Cited Findings

- **Statutory authority cited:** MCA 7-5-2101, MCA 85-7-1702(3) (irrigation districts) and **MCA 13-1-304**. 13-1-304 is CONFIRMED as one of the cited authorities, but it is not the only one. — same source.

  - East Missoula Rural Fire District
  - Elk Meadows Ranchettes County Water District
  - Frenchtown Irrigation District
  - Frenchtown Rural Fire District
  - Greenough-Potomac Rural Fire Service Area
  - **Missoula Irrigation District (Division 2):** Shawn F. Clouse and Robert J. Cole, 3-year terms
  - Seeley Lake Rural Fire District
  - Seeley-Swan Hospital District
  - Seeley Lake Water District
  - Shelby Addition Water District
  - Sunny Meadows Water and Sewer District
  - — same source.

## E. Jail voting

### Takeaway
The "about 80% unconvicted" figure is close. BJS-based PPI 2026 data give about 426,000 pretrial out of about 546,000, roughly 78% (2024 midyear data). "Unconvicted" does not mean "eligible": some unconvicted people are noncitizens, and some are barred by prior felony convictions in some states. No national jail-voting turnout figure exists. "Under 2% nationwide" is UNVERIFIED. The best real data point is from Massachusetts: 1% in the 2024 primary and 5% in the 2024 general. The Harris County jail polling place was first used November 2, 2021.

### Cited Findings
- **Jail population:** About 426,000 people held pretrial in local jails and about 120,000 convicted. Source data: BJS Jails Report Series, 2024 Preliminary Data Release (midyear 2024). — [Prison Policy Initiative, Mass Incarceration: The Whole Pie 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/pie2026.html)
  - 426k / (426k + 120k) ≈ 78% is my calculation. The page as fetched did not state a percentage.
- **Pretrial growth:** "Since 1997, all of the net growth in jails" came from the pretrial population. — [PPI graph, 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/graphs/pie2026_jail_growth.html)
- **"~80% eligible/unconvicted": CORRECTED** to "about 78% have not been convicted (2024)." Do not equate unconvicted with eligible.
- **"Turnout under 2% nationwide": UNVERIFIED.** I found no national dataset. Real figure from a court-ordered Massachusetts report released June 8, 2026:
  - 2024 state primary: 58 of 5,590 eligible incarcerated voters cast a counted ballot (1%).
  - 2024 general: 230 of 4,647 (5%).
  - — [Campaign Legal Center press release](https://campaignlegal.org/index%2ephp/press-releases/court-ordered-report-reveals-low-voting-rates-jails-highlights-need-more-voter); see also [Lawyers for Civil Rights](https://lawyersforcivilrights.org/our-impact/voting-rights/low-voting-rates-in-jails/)
- **Harris County jail polling place: CONFIRMED, first used November 2, 2021.** It was the first such polling place in Texas. Cook County Jail opened one in 2020. About 100 eligible people were expected to vote; the pilot covered people arrested on or after October 22. — [Texas Public Radio, Nov 2, 2021](https://www.tpr.org/government-politics/2021-11-02/the-harris-county-jail-was-used-as-a-polling-place-for-eligible-incarcerated-voters-on-tuesday)

### Gaps
- I found no national count of eligible voters in jail or of jail turnout.
- I did not read the Texas statutory basis (SB 1, 2021 or similar).

## F. Election Assistance Commission (EAC)

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED with one correction. On July 9, 2026, Trump fired Hicks and Hovland, and McCormick was allowed to resign. Palmer had already resigned effective April 30, 2026. The EAC has had zero commissioners and no quorum since. I found no evidence of new nominations as of the CRS update of September 1, 2026, or in later searches.

### Cited Findings
- **July 9, 2026:**
  - Thomas Hicks (D, chair, serving since 2014) and Benjamin Hovland (D, since 2019) were fired by email from the Executive Office of the President.
  - Christy McCormick (R) was permitted to resign, according to three agency sources.
  - The commission was left with no commissioners and no quorum.
  - The White House cited *Trump v. Slaughter*.
  - — [Votebeat, July 9, 2026](https://www.votebeat.org/national/2026/07/09/trump-fires-election-assistance-commission-members-hicks-hovland-mccormick/)
- **Palmer and quorum history:** Donald Palmer resigned effective April 30, 2026. HAVA requires 3 commissioners for most actions. This is the EAC's third loss of quorum: the earlier ones ran December 2010–January 2015 and March 2018–February 2019. Under the EAC's August 2026 overview, staff can carry on delegated tasks, but VVSG changes and changes to the federal registration form require commissioners. No nominations are mentioned. — [CRS Insight IN12713, updated Sept 1, 2026](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-09-01_IN12713_db98774ac4ecb16d53aa96049141208febe648cd.html)
- **Verdict on "fired all three": CORRECTED.** Two were fired and one was allowed to resign. The result, zero commissioners, is accurate.
- **VVSG:**
  - VVSG 2.0 was adopted February 10, 2021, by a 4-0 vote.
  - VVSG 1.0 and 1.1 were deprecated November 16, 2023. No new systems are tested against them, but previously certified systems stay certified and may still be bought and used.
  - The first test lab was accredited for VVSG 2.0 on November 15, 2022.
  - — [Ballotpedia: Deprecation of VVSG 1.0 and 1.1](https://ballotpedia.org/Deprecation_of_Voluntary_Voting_Systems_Guidelines_1.0_and_1.1,_November_2023)
- **Barcode/QR issue:**
  - Trump's March 2025 elections executive order directs the EAC to amend the VVSG so systems are "not allowed to use QR codes or barcodes in the counting of votes," except for disability accommodation.
  - VVSG 2.0 already allows barcodes only alongside human-readable text of the voter's choices.
  - — [Votebeat, Mar 28, 2025](https://www.votebeat.org/2025/03/28/trump-executive-order-elections-bans-barcodes-qr-codes-explained)
  - A June 13, 2025 preliminary injunction (D. Mass., Judge Casper) blocked five provisions, including proof of citizenship on the federal form. It did not rule on the VVSG/barcode provision. — [States United, June 30, 2025](https://statesunited.org/resources/court-rejects-election-order/)
  - CRS notes that VVSG changes require commissioners, so the barcode directive cannot be adopted while the EAC has no quorum. — [CRS IN12713](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-09-01_IN12713_db98774ac4ecb16d53aa96049141208febe648cd.html)

### Inferences
- With no quorum through the November 3, 2026 midterms, no VVSG barcode ban could have been formally adopted before the election.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm quorum status for October–November 2026 from an EAC primary source. My searches turned up no nominations, but absence from search results is not proof that none were made.
- I did not determine whether any system has been certified to VVSG 2.0.
