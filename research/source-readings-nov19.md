# Source readings, November 19, 2026: what was read, what was not, and what changed

This note records a second pass on sources that earlier notes had only partly read. "Read in full" means the page text was extracted from start to finish and the sections that bear on our claims were read. For long documents, the note says which parts were read.

## Read in full (or in full for the parts we use)

- **Stahl, "Home Rule and State Preemption of Local Land Use Control," Urban Lawyer 50(2), 2020** (American Bar Association). Full text extracted (about 10,300 words); read from the introduction through the conclusion.
  - Findings: the conclusion says "home rule does not protect local land-use decisions against preemption." It is the author's argument. The author is a Chapman University land-use law professor who also advises developers (author bio). Most examples are from California (Big Creek, CalFed, Tahoe, City of Vista). The article argues against a proposed local-control ballot measure.
  - Changes made: the lever guide now says the argument is mostly about California, that the author advises developers, and that he argues for state override of local housing rules.
- **National Governors Association, Governors' Powers and Authority**. Read in full except for the first qualification and tenure sections.
  - Findings: "a number of states" have line-item (reduction) vetoes. Our page said "many." Corrected.
  - Confirmed: governors generally can veto; legislatures can usually override by supermajority; many appointments are subject to confirmation ("frequently," per NGA); a majority of governors can appoint state judges.
- **Arkansas Public Service Commission, "The Rate Case Process"** (8,700 characters). Read in full.
  - New fact added: if the commission does not act within 30 days after filing, the proposed increase takes effect automatically. The commission may suspend it for up to nine months. A decision is due within 10 months.
  - Participation options confirmed: letter, petition, testimony at the hearing, and intervenor status.
- **Ballotpedia, "Public service commission elections, 2026"** (about 15,100 characters). Read in full, except for the state-by-state detail tables.
  - Confirmed: nine states held commission elections in 2026 (Alabama, Arizona, Georgia, Louisiana, Montana, Nebraska, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota). Mississippi also elects its commission but had no election in 2026. Democrats won a majority on one commission (Georgia).
- **Ballotpedia, "State legislative elections, 2026"** (summary read in full; table partly read). The count is now dated "as of 12:00 PM EST on Nov. 19, 2026": 46 Democratic, 39 Republican, 1 coalition or tie, 2 uncalled, 12 changed (all to Democrats). Same numbers as before; the date on the site is updated.
- **Movement Voter Project, "Evidence of electoral impact in 2024"** (full analysis, movement.vote/2024-evidence/). Methods section read in full.
  - Finding: the 220,635 figure is a model. MVP multiplies outreach counts (doors, calls, texts, registrations) by average turnout effects taken from earlier experiments, with lower effects in battleground states. MVP says controlled trials would be needed to measure its impact. The site now says so.
  - An external 2025 study of 26 independent political organizations is cited by MVP; we did not open that study.
- **OpenSecrets, Movement Voter Project: summary and recipients (2024 cycle)**.
  - Finding: the recipient breakdown covers about $14.7 million, not the full $19.1 million. Within that, 99.85% went to outside groups and 0.10% to candidates. The site said 99.85% of the $19.1 million. Corrected.
  - Page 1 of the recipient list read; other pages not read.
- **FEC committee overview, End Citizens United (C00573261)**. Financial summary read.
  - Finding: receipts for January 2025 to October 14, 2026 were $23.1 million, including $6.5 million in transfers from affiliated committees. Not used on the site.
- **End Citizens United, endorsements page**. Full list read.
  - Finding: the page lists federal and state candidates and does not state their parties. Our directory line said "Democratic candidates," which was an inference. Corrected. (Dan Osborn, listed for Nebraska, is not identified as a Democrat on the page.)
- **Public Citizen, About**. Full text read.
  - Confirmed: "with rare exceptions" no partisan activity or candidate endorsements; "we take no government or corporate money" (its own statement).
- **Texas open-government outline (Reporters Committee)**. Read in part. The outline covers notice for regular meetings, special meetings and closed sessions. Notice rule read: posted at least 72 hours before the meeting, in a place accessible to the public. The site now uses this as an example.
- **Reporters Committee, Introduction to the Open Government Guide**. Read in full.
  - Confirmed: the guide covers all 50 states and D.C., with a standard outline for each.
- **U.S. Customs and Border Protection, Jeffrey Epstein records**. Page read. A records library; the page lists records but we did not open the individual files.
- **FBI Vault, Jeffrey Epstein**. Page read. The FBI notes that some older files may be unreliable because of how they were processed.

- **epstein-data.com**. Home page read. Says it is "free, open-source, and independently run," uses AI-powered research tools, and requires visitors to confirm they are 18 or older. Not listed on the site.
