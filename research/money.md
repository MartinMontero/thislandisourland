# Money: the national picture, 2026 cycle

Checked: November 10, 2026. Verdict key: CONFIRMED (read on the source page) / CONFIRMED, SECONDARY (the figure is attributed to another body and was read in a report of it) / DISAGREE (sources conflict; the page shows the range) / NOT USED.

Access limits: opensecrets.org cannot be read by the fetch tool, so OpenSecrets figures are quoted from IBTimes (Nov 4, 2026), which attributes them to OpenSecrets. The Axios page on Senate costs returned HTTP 403. New York Times figures are quoted as reported by Political Wire and the Brennan Center.

## How much

- **Total federal election spending: $11.9 billion projected, against $8.9 billion in 2022 (about $10.2 billion adjusted for inflation).** Outside groups $3.9 billion, up 85% from four years earlier. As of Oct 30, conservative groups about $2.03 billion and liberal groups about $1.67 billion. CONFIRMED, SECONDARY (OpenSecrets, via IBTimes, Nov 4, 2026). It is a projection until all reports are filed. — [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/2026-midterm-election-spending-record-11-9-billion-3809414)
- **Dark money: at least $1.47 billion to outside spending groups, against $1.35 billion across 2024.** OpenSecrets calls it a lower bound. CONFIRMED, SECONDARY (same article).
- **Corporate political spending: $737.3 million through Oct 23.** 60% above the 2024 presidential cycle ($461 million) and more than four times 2022 ($184.1 million); over 40% of the $1.8 billion corporations have spent since Citizens United. Cryptocurrency $233 million; online betting $85.2 million; Big Tech, AI and data centers $73.1 million (together $391.3 million, 53%); fossil fuel companies $51.4 million; Koch Inc. $36.8 million; tobacco companies $34.8 million. CONFIRMED (Public Citizen, Oct 29, 2026). Public Citizen says the totals likely undercount corporate spending. Its Key Findings give Andreessen Horowitz as $72 million and Table 2 gives $76.2 million; the page does not explain the difference. — [Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/?p=128196)
- **515 donors who gave at least $1 million gave $4.2 billion to super PACs, more than 70% of the money super PACs raised this cycle so far** (a graphic on the page says 73%). Elon Musk's $445 million is 10% of the total; without him it is $3.8 billion from 514 donors. CONFIRMED (Public Citizen, Oct 27, 2026; no stated cutoff). — [Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/article/millionaires-and-billionaires-fuel-2026-super-pac-spending/)
- **The 20 biggest donors gave roughly $2 billion to federal races, through the mid-October filing deadline.** CONFIRMED, SECONDARY (New York Times ranking, as reported by Political Wire, Nov 1, 2026). — [Political Wire](https://politicalwire.com/2026/11/01/2-billion-from-20-top-donors-fuels-the-midterms/)

## Secret money

- **Issue One, Jul 22, 2026.** Between January 2025 and June 2026 the four main leadership super PACs raised $714.6 million, of which $197.7 million (about 28%, our division) came from their affiliated dark money groups. Senate Leadership Fund $70.74 million from One Nation (about 28% of its receipts); Senate Majority PAC $61 million from Majority Forward (about 35%); Congressional Leadership Fund $44.2 million from American Action Network (about 27%); House Majority PAC $21.7 million from House Majority Forward (about 18%). Republican-aligned super PACs account for 58% of this dark money and Democratic-aligned for 42%. CONFIRMED. — [Issue One](https://issueone.org/press/new-data-illuminates-how-secret-money-fuels-2026-midterms/)

CONFIRMED. — [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/01/texas-senate-gop-outside-spending-ken-paxton-thune-trump-talarico/)

CONFIRMED. — [CBS Austin](https://cbsaustin.com/news/texas-politics/paxton-vs-talarico-the-competitive-costly-senate-race-comes-to-a-close)

CONFIRMED. — [Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2026/08/03/aipac-tops-100-million-in-election-spending-for-second-straight-cycle/)
  - Al Jazeera, Aug 2, 2026: UDP $30,638,639.63 (FEC); more than $60 million in all to support Stevens and oppose El-Sayed, including more than $54 million from PACs and about $6.5 million from the Center for Democratic Priorities, which had not reported to the FEC (figure from local media); about $3.5 million of independent expenditures for El-Sayed. CONFIRMED. — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/2/how-much-has-aipac-spent-against-michigans-abdul-el-sayed-and-why)

## Since Citizens United (added Nov 10, 2026, from the seven sources the site's editor supplied)

Read in full: Roosevelt Institute report (PDF, 30 pages, text extracted), Roosevelt fact sheet, Roosevelt report landing page, Common Cause "8 charts", Brennan Center dark money topic page, Fair Observer. Not readable: the OpenSecrets "By the numbers: 15 years of Citizens United" page (opensecrets.org is blocked for the fetch tool); its figures were confirmed instead from the OpenSecrets newsletter of the same date (below).

- **Outside spending by presidential-election cycle: 2008 $574 million; 2012 nearly $1.3 billion; 2020 $3.3 billion; 2024 "close to $4.5 billion."** Super PACs: $62.6 million in 2010; super PACs and hybrid PACs $622.7 million in 2012; more than $4.1 billion in 2024; "most of that money was spent by super PACs." Top 100 individual donors: $80.9 million in 2008, 1.5% of the $5.3 billion spent on federal elections; their share "climbed sharply through 2016" and "has since settled in the 14% to 16% range." CONFIRMED (OpenSecrets newsletter, Jan 24, 2025, read in the Archive of Political Emails). — [OpenSecrets email](https://politicalemails.org/messages/1706920)
- **Billionaires, 2024:** billionaire donors and their families spent over $2.6 billion, nearly 20% of total federal election spending that cycle; over 80% of it through channels prohibited before Citizens United; overall billionaire election spending multiplied by a factor of 163 since the ruling. CONFIRMED, SECONDARY (Roosevelt Institute, Oct 2, 2025, citing Tashman and Rice 2025 / Americans for Tax Fairness; the original was not read). The report is by a think tank that argues for reversing the ruling; the page attributes the numbers to it. One billionaire gave over $290 million to outside spending groups in 2024, about the combined gifts of 3 million small donors (fact sheet, citing OpenSecrets; the billionaire is not named). CONFIRMED, SECONDARY. — [Roosevelt Institute report](https://rooseveltinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/RI_Citizens-United-15-Years-Later_Report_202510.pdf); [fact sheet](https://rooseveltinstitute.org/publications/15-years-after-citizens-united-fact-sheet/)
- **Dark money: more than $1.9 billion in the 2024 federal cycle, about $1 billion in 2020, at least $4.3 billion since 2010.** Counts four channels: contributions to super PACs (about $1.3 billion), direct spending reported to the FEC (about $43 million), TV ads (about $242 million) and online ads. The author says it "necessarily, and perhaps substantially, underestimates" the real total. CONFIRMED (Brennan Center, Anna Massoglia, May 7, 2025). **Reconciles with the OpenSecrets $1.35 billion for 2024** used on the page: that figure counts only dark-money gifts to outside groups, which matches the Brennan "about $1.3 billion" super PAC channel. — [Brennan Center](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/dark-money-hit-record-high-19-billion-2024-federal-races)
- **Early dark money: social-welfare nonprofit (501(c)(4)) spending on federal elections rose from $1.2 million in 2006 to $257 million in 2012.** CONFIRMED (Common Cause, Jan 21, 2015, citing OpenSecrets). Old, and a different measure from the Brennan total; the page says so. — [Common Cause](https://www.commoncause.org/articles/8-charts-that-show-how-political-spending-has-exploded-since-citizens-united/)
- **Public opinion (Pew Research Center, report dated Sept 19, 2023; surveys of July 10–16, 2023, 8,480 adults, and June 5–11, 2023, 5,115 adults):** 80% say big campaign donors have too much influence on decisions by members of Congress; 70% say people in members' own districts have too little influence; 72% support limits on how much individuals and organizations can spend on campaigns. The page does not say which survey each number comes from. CONFIRMED. The Roosevelt fact sheet dates the 80% / 70% figures to Oct 23, 2023; the Pew page itself says Sept 19, 2023, which the site uses. — [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2023/09/19/americans-dismal-views-of-the-nations-politics/)
- **Montana Initiative 194 ("the Montana Plan").** Approved Nov 3, 2026 with about 65% (Daily Montanan, Nov 4, 2026, early returns with nearly 57% of ballots counted; NBC Montana, Nov 6, 2026). Bars corporations, nonprofits and other organizations from spending to support or oppose candidates, parties or ballot measures; individuals acting on their own and political committees are exempt. Takes effect Jan 1, 2027 unless a court blocks it. A federal lawsuit was filed at 11:15 p.m. on election night by the Frontier Institute, the Montana Republican State Central Committee and other groups, arguing it violates their First Amendment rights and seeking an injunction; the court is not named on the page. CONFIRMED. Every number in it is cited to another outlet (Politico, Al Jazeera, Brennan Center, Forbes and others) or to nothing (Musk "nearly half a billion dollars"). ## Brennan Center and NBER, round 3 (Nov 10, 2026)

- **The top 40 donors, then and now** — Brennan Center, Ian Vandewalker and Alex Brunet, "Big Changes to Makeup of the Biggest Midterm Donors," published Oct 27, 2026, updated Oct 29. FEC data through Aug 31; Sept 1 to Oct 14 from raw OpenFEC data processed by the authors. The 40 biggest donors gave $2.4 billion through mid-October, a record for that point in a midterm since 2010; eight times the 2014 figure after inflation; more than 50% above the $1.6 billion from all of 2022. The 40th donor gave $25 million, against almost $2.3 million (2026 dollars) in 2014. In 2014 the top 40 favored Democrats two to one; donors who give only to Republicans gave $1.5 billion this cycle, more than three times loyal Democratic donors. Bipartisan giving more than 100 times the last two midterms (crypto $304 million). Dark money groups in the top 40: $699 million, 13 times 2014, 29% of top-donor giving (17% in 2014); more than half from four groups tied to congressional party leadership. Shares of top-40 giving, 2014 to 2026: labor 14% to off the list; traditional finance almost half to 15%; real estate 10% to 1%; crypto now 13%. Musk and his company United States of America Inc.: $483 million, all for Republicans, more than any donor in any cycle. Uihleins $147 million; Adelson $67 million; Soros $102 million (company and nonprofit); Bloomberg $61 million through Oct 14. AI-tied donors (including Musk, Bloomberg, Andreessen, Horowitz, Hoffman) more than $650 million together. CONFIRMED. — [Brennan Center](https://www.brennancenter.org/node/12567)
  - **Musk's total now has four counts:** $366 million (NYT via Brennan, Oct 13), $445 million (Public Citizen, Oct 27), $446 million (Al Jazeera, Oct 27), $483 million (Brennan, Oct 29, including his company, data through Oct 14). DISAGREE; the page shows the range.
- **Governors' races** — Brennan Center, Eric Petry, "Who's Trying to Buy the Governor's Mansion?", Oct 27, 2026. Figures come from news reports and campaign filings and were not independently verified by Brennan; no stated cutoff (Wisconsin: end-of-September filings; latest cited items Oct 13 and Oct 20). Georgia: Rick Jackson (R) self-funded over $100 million and outspent Keisha Lance Bottoms (D) "five to one." Ohio: Vivek Ramaswamy (R) loaned his campaign at least $36 million; Jeff Yass gave at least $30 million to V-PAC; Ramaswamy and allies spent over $120 million on ads, "more than triple" Amy Acton's (D) side. Wisconsin: Diane Hendricks and Richard and Liz Uihlein gave nearly $60 million to the state Republican Party; three megadonors were 92% of Tom Tiffany's fundraising in the prior three-month period; labor union PACs limited by state law to $86,000 to David Crowley (D). Not used: Nevada (no gap stated). CONFIRMED. - **Reforms** — Brennan Center, "Nine Solutions for Political Corruption," Jan 20, 2026 (Daniel Weiner, Joanna Zdanys, Eric Petry). Used: the four campaign-money proposals (constitutional amendment to restore limits; ban big donations from government contractors; end dark money by requiring disclosure of major donors; public financing), New York's matching of small contributions at up to $12 to $1, and "legislators or voters in 23 states" have passed resolutions for a constitutional amendment on campaign finance. These are proposals by a think tank. Not used: the five other solutions (ethics, emoluments, pardons, stock trading) and the page's statistics about the President. The Brennan topic page lists the same paper as an Oct 13, 2026 item; the paper's own page says Jan 20, 2026, which is used. CONFIRMED. — [Brennan Center](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/policy-solutions/nine-solutions-political-corruption)
- **NBER Working Paper 31481, "The Impact of Money in Politics on Labor and Capital: Evidence from Citizens United v. FEC"** — Pat Akey (Toronto), Tania Babina (Columbia), Greg Buchak (Stanford), Ana-Maria Tenekedjieva (Federal Reserve Board; the paper says its views are not the Fed's). July 2023. **A working paper circulated for comment; not peer-reviewed.** Method: difference-in-differences between the 23 states that had banned independent political spending before Citizens United and states that had not. Findings: total income (state GDP or adjusted gross income) about 2% higher in affected states; labor income up to 3% higher, for up to six years; capital income generally positive but often not statistically significant; contributions rose among a broad set of donors, including small donors; political turnover among governors and legislators rose in both parties; legislatures less polarized; fewer state enforcement actions against labor and consumer-protection violations; a composite regulatory burden fell; no change found in adverse worker health outcomes. The authors read this as more political competition. Also cites OpenSecrets: federal election spending $3.1 billion in the 2000 cycle and $14.4 billion in 2020 (not used on the page). CONFIRMED (read in full). It points the other way from the Roosevelt Institute's summary of state-level studies (corporate tax rates down 4 to 8 percent; Republican success up 4 to 11 points), which the page reports as "cited by" the Institute, because those studies were not read. The page states that researchers disagree. — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31481/w31481.pdf)
- **Brennan Center, "Influence of Big Money" topic page.** An index page. Summary line: "A handful of wealthy donors dominate electoral giving and spending in the United States." No statistics. Its dated 2026 items were read individually where they carry data (above); "Ads Focusing on Corruption Surge in Midterms" (Oct 30), "Voter Anger at Corruption Shapes 2026 Midterms" (Oct 28) and "Voters Want Action" (Nov 10) were not read.
