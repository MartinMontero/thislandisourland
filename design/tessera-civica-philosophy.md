# Tessera Civica

A tessera is the single cut stone of a mosaic. Alone it is a fleck of colour; set beside thousands of others, held in a bed of mortar, it becomes a face, a map, a record. Tessera Civica treats every visual decision as the placing of one stone. Nothing is ornamental. Every tile carries weight, and the picture only resolves when the viewer steps back far enough to see the whole field.

Space is organised on a strict modular grid, the kind a mosaicist snaps in chalk before a single stone is laid. Tiles are square or near-square, separated by a constant hairline of grout that never varies in width. The grout is the quiet hero: a warm, unbleached paper tone that holds the composition together and gives the eye somewhere to rest. Large fields of that ground are left deliberately empty, so that the tiles which do appear read as decisions, not decoration. Asymmetry is permitted only when it is earned; the default is calm, measured repetition that rewards patient looking. The whole should feel meticulously crafted, as if each cell had been weighed and set by hand over countless hours.

Colour comes from the mineral pigments of old floors and frescoes: lapis for depth, verdigris for the living, ochre for emphasis, terracotta for warning, and a deep iron-gall ink for structure. These are never saturated to the point of shouting, and no single hue dominates the field. Colour encodes category, never allegiance; political colour coding is avoided entirely, because the movement's purpose is to make the viewer look at structure rather than at sides. Each pigment is calibrated against the paper ground with painstaking care, so that the palette reads as one material family rather than a set of digital swatches.

Scale moves in a deliberate rhythm: a few monumental forms, a middle register of repeated units, and a fine layer of almost microscopic marks and reference numerals that only reveal themselves up close. Typography follows the same law. A high-contrast serif carries the rare large word; a restrained grotesque handles labels; a monospaced face supplies index marks, coordinates, and counts, as though the composition were a plate from a survey of an imaginary discipline. Text is sparse and placed as stone is placed: aligned to the grid, never crowding a neighbour, never falling off the edge.

Hierarchy is built from accumulation rather than volume. Importance is signalled by position, by isolation, and by the faint gold rule that marks a cell as singular, not by size or glare. The finished work should look like the product of deep expertise and master-level execution: every margin checked, every alignment refined until it disappears, every mark earning its place. When the viewer steps back, the individual stones dissolve into a single legible figure, and that moment of resolution is the entire point.
