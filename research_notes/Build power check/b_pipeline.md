# Build power check: Pillar II (candidate pipeline) fact-check

Read/verified: 2026-11-17. Source under test: AI-written research report, Pillar II (lines 21-39 of the attachment). The report was treated as a claims list only, never as evidence.

Method note: pages were read through a fetch tool that returns a model-written summary of each page, not raw HTML. Figures below are the ones that summary reported; quote-level wording should be re-checked on the live page before it is printed on the site. ## A. Claim-by-claim verdicts

### 1. "In 2020 approximately seventy percent of elected seats lacked competitive opposition" - CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTION (wrong year; "seats" wrong unit)

- Ballotpedia's own series (started 2018) counts elections where the sole candidate on the ballot was virtually guaranteed to win. Covered elections only, not "all seats nationwide."
- 2020: 65% of covered elections uncontested (Ballotpedia 2024 analysis page); 64% in Ballotpedia's Dec 2024 news post. Both say "second-highest on record" at the time.
- 70% is the 2024 figure: 53,485 of 76,902 elections (70%), highest since 2018. https://ballotpedia.org/Analysis_of_uncontested_elections,_2024 (read 2026-11-17).
- Best current number: 2025, 68% (26,318 of 38,609 elections in 49 states), second-highest on record. https://ballotpedia.org/Analysis_of_uncontested_elections,_2025 (read 2026-11-17). 2018-2025 average 64%; 2021 low 49%.
- Scope caveat from Ballotpedia: all congressional, state executive, state judicial and state legislative races, plus local races only in the 100 largest cities, every state capital and 475 school districts (full local coverage in 26 states). Ballotpedia says this likely undercounts uncontested races (cancelled elections, no-filer seats).
- Other cuts: 2024 state legislative general elections, 2,227 seats (38%) with no major-party competition (PR Newswire/Ballotpedia release, read 2026-11-17: https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-report-from-ballotpedia-shows-2-227-uncontested-state-legislative-seats-nationwide-302266317.html). Nov 5, 2024 election day: 65% of 40,646 elections uncontested; congressional/state 31%; school boards 45%. https://news.ballotpedia.org/2024/12/04/65-of-general-elections-held-on-nov-5-were-uncontested/
- BallotReady (CivicEngine) says about 60% of down-ballot local offices go unopposed each year (as cited on its case-study page, https://organizations.ballotready.org/case-studies/run-for-somethings-numbers-speak-for-themselves). Secondary; the 60% is its figure, not independently checked.
- Suggested site wording: "In 2025, 68% of the elections Ballotpedia tracked had only one candidate on the ballot (70% in 2024, 65% in 2020)." Do not say "seats," do not say "nationwide."
- Also: "lacked competitive opposition" is a looser claim than "uncontested". Keep Ballotpedia's term.

### 2. "Over 500,000 elected officials; 96% at the local level" - CONFIRMED (500,000+), 96% is an approximation with no current official count

- Ballotpedia says "More than 500,000 officials are elected to office in the United States," with no as-of date. https://ballotpedia.org/Ballotpedia:Scope (page updated May 16, 2024; read 2026-11-17).
- Ballotpedia also describes "the more than 500,000 local elected offices nationwide" on its election-coverage page (https://ballotpedia.org/Election_coverage_approach_for_Ballotpedia). The two Ballotpedia statements are not the same claim: one is all officials, one is local offices only. Ballotpedia does not reconcile them.
- Older estimate: 519,000 elected officials (Scott Rasmussen "Number of the Day," Ballotpedia, 2017/2019). https://ballotpedia.org/Scott_Rasmussen's_Number_of_the_Day_for_December_4,_2019
- A second estimate: Jennifer Lawless, Becoming a Candidate (2012), "over half a million", as reported by a Substack explainer (https://socdoneleft.substack.com/p/how-many-elected-offices-does-the). That Substack tallies about 536 federal seats, about 7,631 state seats and a local count built on the 1992 Census of Governments, and gets roughly 96% local. The Substack says its own figures do not reconcile exactly and that no official count has been updated since 1992. Secondary source.
- Census of Governments 2022: 90,837 local governments (3,031 counties; 19,491 municipal; 16,214 township; 12,546 independent school districts; 39,555 special districts). The Census publication contains no count of elected officials. https://cdn.www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/publications/2025/econ/govtorg2225.pdf
- I did not find "96%" in Ballotpedia, Census, or Run for Something material. NOT VERIFIED as a published statistic; it can be derived (federal ~536 + state ~7,600 against 500,000+ is under 2% non-local, so "about 98% local" by that arithmetic, or "about 96%" on the Substack's smaller base). Safe wording: "More than 500,000 people hold elected office in the US; the overwhelming majority are in local government. No agency keeps a current official count."

### 4. Amy Gooch critique - CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTION (not a journal paper)

- Source: Amy Gooch, "'Women in the House Y'all:' She Should Run, Post-Feminism, and Women's Representation in Politics." A Cultural Studies Capstone Paper (BA), Columbia College Chicago, Spring 2014, hosted on Digital Commons. https://digitalcommons.colum.edu/cultural_studies/8 (read 2026-11-17).
- Abstract: argues SSR addresses women's underrepresentation but relies on essentialist framing and post-feminist ideas, and that this reinforces fixed ideas of women's identities.
- Corrections to the report: it is an undergraduate capstone from 2014, not a journal article; it analyses SSR as it was in 2014. I read only the abstract, so the report's specific phrase "depoliticizing metonymy of the word 'woman'" is NOT VERIFIED (it may be in the full text).
- How to record it: "One scholar's argument (an undergraduate capstone, 2014)." Do not write "academic analyses" in the plural.
