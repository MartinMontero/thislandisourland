### Co-governance: CORRECTION
Engler & Engler, "A Guide to Co-Governance: Can Movements and Politicians Work Together?" (2022; hosted by Democracy Uprising). Contents: Part I rise of co-governance; Part II "Five Practices and Five Pitfalls"; Part III Brazil (landless workers' movement). So the attachment's "four practices" is wrong: five practices and five pitfalls. Definition (In These Times): practices through which movements and elected officials coordinate; also called inside-outside politics. A second, different use exists: New America (Gilman and Schmitt) uses co-governance for community and local government sharing decision-making (participatory budgeting, citizen assemblies, community land trusts). Say both, label as two meanings. Race Forward "Co-Governing Toward Multiracial Democracy" exists (Roosevelt Institute reference). The five practices themselves: titles not retrieved; do not list.

### Hahrie Han: CONFIRMED (partial)
How Organizations Develop Activists (Oxford University Press, 2014). Argument: high-engagement associations use organizing to build depth, which enables breadth through mobilizing; "transformational" vs "transactional" (Commons Library summary; Civic Tech Field Guide). Han's APSR 2016 field experiments: creating a relational context makes people more likely to sign petitions, recruit others, attend meetings. The attachment's three-part scheme stays NOT VERIFIED; use only the depth/breadth finding.
