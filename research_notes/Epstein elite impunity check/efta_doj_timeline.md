## Key question 2: DOJ compliance — deadline, releases, "less than 1%," redactions, lawsuit, OIG audit

### Takeaway
DOJ missed the Dec 19, 2025 deadline, released tens of thousands of pages in December, told a court on Jan 5, 2026 that about 125,575 pages had been released with 2 million+ documents still under review (the "less than 1%" figure is the press's arithmetic, not a DOJ statement), then released 3+ million pages on Jan 30, 2026. The "over 3 million pages withheld" figure is Massie's later claim, not an early-2026 court finding. Survivors filed a class action on March 26, 2026, and the DOJ inspector general announced an EFTA compliance audit on April 23, 2026.

### Cited Findings
- DOJ missed the Dec 19 congressional deadline; just before Christmas it released tens of thousands of pages, many already public or heavily redacted. — [AP via CFJC Today, Jan 30, 2026](https://cfjctoday.com/?p=1061904)
- In a letter to Judge Paul Engelmayer (Maxwell case) filed late Monday [Jan 5, 2026], DOJ said it had released about 12,285 documents (~125,575 pages) and that 2 million+ documents were "in various phases of review"; more than 400 DOJ lawyers involved; DOJ said many unreviewed documents are likely duplicates. "Less than 1%" is WTOP/AP's characterization, not DOJ wording. — [WTOP/AP, Jan 2026](https://wtop.com/government/2026/01/doj-says-it-has-reviewed-less-than-1-of-epstein-files-so-far/); headline corroboration [NBC Palm Springs, Jan 6, 2026](https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2026/01/06/doj-says-less-than-1-of-epstein-files-released-as-review-of-millions-of-documents-continues)
- Jan 30, 2026: DOJ posted 3 million+ pages, 2,000+ videos, 180,000 images; documents under review grew to ~6 million including duplicates. Deputy AG Blanche said it "marks the end of a very comprehensive document identification and review process." — [AP via CFJC Today, Jan 30, 2026](https://cfjctoday.com/?p=1061904)
- The Jan 30 release is described as a 3.5-million-page catalogue. — [Al Jazeera, Apr 23, 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/23/us-department-of-justice-watchdog-to-probe-release-of-epstein-files)
- Massie claims DOJ is withholding more than 3 million files and keeping heavy redactions. — [Roll Call, Sept 3, 2026](https://rollcall.com/2026-09-03/second-epstein-petition-looks-for-republican-support)
- Survivors' class action filed in federal court in the Northern District of California (Thursday; article dated Mar 27, 2026 → Mar 26, 2026) against DOJ and Google, alleging releases in late 2025/early 2026 exposed about 100 survivors; DOJ said 0.1% of released pages had unredacted victim information and that flagged documents were removed. — [CNN via KRDO, Mar 27, 2026](https://krdo.com/news/2026/03/27/epstein-survivors-sue-justice-department-and-google-over-release-of-private-information/)
- DOJ OIG announced on Thursday April 23, 2026 an audit of DOJ processes for identifying, redacting, and releasing records under EFTA. — [Al Jazeera, Apr 23, 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/23/us-department-of-justice-watchdog-to-probe-release-of-epstein-files); OIG letter listed at [Blumenthal.senate.gov, Apr 23, 2026](https://www.blumenthal.senate.gov/download/2026-4-23_doj_oig_letter_re_efta_audit_04232026) (not fetched)
- Massie told Blanche he had "30 days to release the rest of the files." — [Al Jazeera, Apr 23, 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/23/us-department-of-justice-watchdog-to-probe-release-of-epstein-files)

### Inferences
- The file's sentence "By early 2026, court filings revealed the DOJ had released less than 1% … unlawfully withholding over 3 million pages" merges two separate data points: the Jan 5, 2026 filing (<1% by press arithmetic, 2M+ documents under review) and Massie's post–Jan 30 claim (3M+ withheld, compared with the ~6M identified and ~3.5M released). "Unlawfully" is an allegation, not a finding by any court found in this search.
- The file cites a Dec 19, 2025 Guardian article (ref 35) for a claim about "early 2026 court filings" — a date mismatch in its own sourcing.
- "Blanche saying no further major releases": closest verified statement is the Jan 30, 2026 "marks the end" remark. The wording "no further major releases" is not verified as a quote.

### Gaps
- No primary DOJ figure for total pages withheld after Jan 30, 2026 was found.
- WTOP summary referenced a "Dec. 12 deadline"; statute's 30 days from Nov 19 gives Dec 19, which AP (CFJC) uses. Treat Dec 19 as correct.

## Key question 4: EFTA II — bill, provisions, 2026 discharge petition, quotes

### Takeaway
EFTA II is real: H.R. 9694, introduced July 15, 2026, with the provisions the file lists. Its discharge petition opened Aug 31, 2026 (not Sept 2) and reached 218 on Nov 11, 2026 with Rep. John Garamendi as the 218th signer. Republican signers were Massie, Mace, Norman, and Fitzpatrick — not Greene or Boebert. No floor vote has occurred; earliest eligibility reported is about Dec 2–3, 2026. "Hostile act," "superfluous," Walkinshaw, Grijalva, Mayes, and the Boebert meeting all belong to 2025, not EFTA II.

### Cited Findings
- July 15, 2026: Massie, Khanna, Leger Fernández (House) and Merkley, Luján (Senate) introduced EFTA II, H.R. 9694.
