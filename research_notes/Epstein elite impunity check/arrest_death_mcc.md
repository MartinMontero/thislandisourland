## 1. Arrest, indictment, bail, Acosta resignation, death, medical examiner ruling

### Takeaway
The file's dates are correct. The file says the indictment was obtained July 2; it was returned July 2 and unsealed July 8. The death on Aug 10 and the suicide ruling (announced Aug 16, 2019) are confirmed. The file's causal language ("directly forced," "forced to resign," "disastrous") is characterization, not fact.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED: Grand jury returned the indictment July 2, 2019. DOJ filing: "On July 2, 2019, a grand jury ... returned an indictment" — [DOJ motion to unseal grand jury material, filed July 18, 2025 (PDF mirror)](https://c1.legalinsurrection.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/DOJ-Asks-Court-to-Unseal-Epstein-Grand-Jury.pdf)

Announced by U.S. Attorney Geoffrey Berman — [SDNY, Berman prepared remarks, July 8, 2019](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/page/file/1181906/dl)
- CONFIRMED: Arrested Saturday, July 6, 2019, at Teterboro Airport (NJ) after landing from Paris, then taken to MCC New York — [SDNY, July 8, 2019](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/page/file/1181906/dl); [NPR, July 18, 2019](https://www.npr.org/2019/07/18/741802612/jeffrey-epstein-is-denied-bail-in-sex-trafficking-case)

The NYC medical examiner announced on Aug 16, 2019 that the death was a suicide by hanging — [CBS News, Aug 16, 2019](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/jeffrey-epstein-death-ruled-suicide-by-hanging-medical-examiner-today-2019-08-16). - The file's statement that the reporting "directly forced the DOJ to reopen the investigation" is a causal claim. The Herald series is widely credited, but this check found no SDNY statement attributing the case to it. Treat it as characterization unless separately sourced.

## 2. DOJ Inspector General report (June 2023, Report 23-085)

### Takeaway
The OIG documented serious failures. It found no evidence contradicting the FBI's conclusion that no crime was involved. The file's "nearly eight hours" unmonitored is consistent with the report. "At least 12 rounds and three counts" does not match: the OIG gives no figure of 12 missed rounds, and at least four required counts were missed (10 p.m., 12, 3 and 5 a.m.).

### Cited Findings
All from [DOJ OIG Report 23-085, "Investigation and Review of the BOP's Custody, Care, and Supervision of Jeffrey Epstein at MCC New York," June 2023](https://oig.justice.gov:443/sites/default/files/reports/23-085.pdf), unless noted.
- **Rounds:** No 30-minute rounds were done after about 10:40 p.m. Aug 9 (p. 62). Officer Noel signed "more than 75 separate 30-minute entries" claiming rounds (p. 69). The report gives no total count of missed rounds. **File's "at least 12 required rounds": NOT VERIFIED / not an OIG figure.** At 30-minute intervals, about 16 rounds were due between 10:40 p.m. and 6:30 a.m. (my arithmetic, not an OIG number).
- **Counts:** No required SHU counts were conducted from 4 p.m. Aug 9 until about 6:30 a.m. Aug 10 (p. 62). The 10 p.m. count slip read "73 + 1" though no count was done (p. 64). Noel and Thomas admitted falsifying the 12, 3 and 5 a.m. slips (p. 67). **File's "three mandatory inmate counts": WRONG (understated).** At least four required counts were missed (10 p.m., 12 a.m., 3 a.m., 5 a.m.). The executive summary also lists the 4 p.m. count among those not done.
- **Unmonitored period:** Video shows no one entered the SHU tiers from about 10:40 p.m. Aug 9 to 6:30 a.m. Aug 10 (p. 86). **File's "nearly eight hours": CONFIRMED** (about 7 hours 50 minutes). He was locked in his cell from about 8 p.m.
- **July 23 incident and suicide watch:** About 1:27 a.m. July 23, 2019, staff found him with an orange cloth around his neck. He was placed on suicide watch, removed July 24 at about 8:45 a.m., and kept on psychological observation until July 30. A staff psychologist noted it was "unclear" whether he or someone else placed it (p. 31). A BOP internal review (SIS, Aug 2) found "insufficient evidence" either way (p. 32). **File's "previous suicide attempt just weeks earlier": REPORTED.** The OIG does not settle whether it was an attempt. **"Taken off suicide watch": CONFIRMED**, but that was July 24, about 17 days before his death, not the night of it.
- **Cellmate failure:** On July 30, psychology emailed over 70 staff that Epstein "needs to be housed with an appropriate cellmate" (p. 33). His cellmate was transferred out about 8:30 a.m. Aug 9. No replacement was assigned, "as required by the Psychology Department" (exec. summary; pp. 52–57). **File's "failed to follow mandatory psychological protocols ... left alone": CONFIRMED in substance.**
- **Cameras:** DVR 2 disk failures on July 29, 2019 stopped recording on about half of roughly 150 cameras (p. 83). BOP learned of it Aug 8, and the technician postponed the repair (p. 85). The L-tier camera was live but not recording (p. 82). The FBI found "catastrophic disk failures" (p. 94). Two nearby cameras did record, including the SHU common area (p. 86).
- **Staffing/overtime:** Noel worked a 16-hour double shift, 4 p.m. Aug 9 to 8 a.m. Aug 10 (p. 58), after five days of overtime (p. 72). Thomas had worked 22 consecutive hours by the 10 p.m. count (p. 64). The OIG calls shortages "a persistent issue for the BOP" (p. 95). No headcount figures appear. **File's "severe understaffing": CONFIRMED in substance.**
- **Other failures:** Excess linens were in the cell, some "ripped into thin strips" and tied as nooses (p. 78). He was authorized two sheets and one blanket (p. 79). Only one cell search was logged Aug 9 (p. 78). He made an unmonitored phone call about 7 p.m. Aug 9, contrary to policy (exec. summary).
- **Guard prosecutions:** Tova Noel and Michael Thomas were charged with falsifying records. Both entered deferred prosecution agreements with SDNY, and "the court dismissed all charges" after they met the terms. Prosecution of other employees was declined (p. 95). **CONFIRMED.** The OIG report does not give charge or dismissal dates (charged Nov 2019, dismissed early 2022, from general knowledge; not verified in this pass).
- **Foul play:** The OIG "did not uncover evidence contradicting the FBI's determination" that no crime was involved (exec. summary; p. 94).

### Inferences
- The file's phrase "dysfunction ... that facilitated Epstein's death" is close to the OIG's view that the failures gave him the chance to take his own life. The file cites the House Oversight report (ref 7) for these numbers, not the OIG. The OIG is the primary source and should be cited instead.
- The file's closing line ("or for a third party to intervene") raises a possibility the OIG says it found no evidence for. Under the brief's constraint, this needs the OIG finding placed beside it.

### Gaps
- Charge and dismissal dates for Noel and Thomas were not confirmed from a fetched source.
- The "handwritten note from Epstein complaining about his treatment" was NOT VERIFIED. I did not locate it in the report sections read or in fetched news. The file cites Britannica (ref 19), which was not checked.

## 3. "Confusion over which noose" and "three bones in his neck"

### Takeaway
Both claims trace to real reporting, but neither is stated accurately. The noose question is REPORTED: the OIG documents the ligature being torn away and linens recycled, and Baden disputes that the noose in the autopsy file matches the neck mark. "Three bones" is WRONG in detail: the fractures reported were one bone (left hyoid) and two in the thyroid cartilage.

### Cited Findings
- REPORTED (noose): Officer Thomas "immediately ripped the orange sheet or shirt away" (p. 71). BOP policy puts lifesaving ahead of crime-scene preservation (p. 76, n.45). The FBI took one torn sheet. BOP then recycled the cell's linens (p. 77). A noose in an evidence photo "is not the ligature Epstein used" (p. 78, n.47). Multiple nooses were in the cell — [DOJ OIG 23-085](https://oig.justice.gov:443/sites/default/files/reports/23-085.pdf)
- REPORTED (noose): 60 Minutes reviewed photos showing multiple nooses. Baden said the noose sketched in the autopsy report "doesn't match the ligature furrow mark" — [CBS 60 Minutes, Jan 5, 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/did-jeffrey-epstein-kill-himself-60-minutes-investigates-2020-01-05/)
- WRONG in detail (bones): Baden described "fractures of the left, the right thyroid cartilage and the left hyoid bone." That is three fractures, one of them in bone — [CBS 60 Minutes, Jan 5, 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/did-jeffrey-epstein-kill-himself-60-minutes-investigates-2020-01-05/). Correct wording: "three fractures in the neck (hyoid bone and thyroid cartilage)."
- REPORTED: Brown herself used the "three neck bones" framing on her Substack, as reported in 2026 — [Raw Story, May 13, 2026](https://www.rawstory.com/famed-reporter-flags-suspicious/)
- NOT VERIFIED: The file says "forensic experts" found it "nearly impossible" to determine manner of death from the physical evidence alone. No source found with that wording. The closest is Baden's "I hesitate ... to make a final opinion until all the evidence is in" — [CBS, Jan 5, 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/did-jeffrey-epstein-kill-himself-60-minutes-investigates-2020-01-05/). The file cites Britannica (ref 19), which was not checked.

### Inferences
- "Official confusion over which specific noose" is a fair label only if tied to the OIG footnote and the 60 Minutes photos. Whether the ligature was recovered is documented as a handling failure, not as evidence of homicide.

### Gaps
- Britannica (ref 19) was not fetched, so the file's own sourcing for these sentences is unchecked.

## 4. Baden (family-retained) vs. the medical examiner — CONTESTED

### Takeaway
CONTESTED. The NYC medical examiner ruled suicide by hanging and stands by it. Michael Baden, paid by Epstein's brother Mark, said the evidence pointed more to homicidal strangulation, but declined to give a final opinion. The OIG found no evidence contradicting the FBI's no-crime finding.

### Cited Findings
- Medical examiner's side: Ruled suicide by hanging, announced Aug 16, 2019 — [CBS, Aug 16, 2019](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/jeffrey-epstein-death-ruled-suicide-by-hanging-medical-examiner-today-2019-08-16). Sampson: "We stand by that determination" — [NPR, Oct 30, 2019](https://www.npr.org/2019/10/30/774838950/jeffrey-epstein-case-expert-hired-by-his-family-suggests-doubt-on-suicide-findin). The office said these fractures occur in both suicides and homicides — [CBS, Jan 5, 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/did-jeffrey-epstein-kill-himself-60-minutes-investigates-2020-01-05/). No defensive wounds and no struggle (OIG p. 80) — [OIG 23-085](https://oig.justice.gov:443/sites/default/files/reports/23-085.pdf)
- Baden's side: The injuries were more consistent with "homicidal strangulation"; he was retained by Mark Epstein — [NPR, Oct 30, 2019](https://www.npr.org/2019/10/30/774838950/jeffrey-epstein-case-expert-hired-by-his-family-suggests-doubt-on-suicide-findin). "I have never seen three fractures like this in a suicidal hanging." The evidence "point[s] much more to murder." He also said he would not give a final opinion yet, and acknowledged the appearance of bias because Mark pays him — [CBS 60 Minutes, Jan 5, 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/did-jeffrey-epstein-kill-himself-60-minutes-investigates-2020-01-05/)

### Inferences
- The file does not mention Baden by name. If it presents doubts about the manner of death, it should name the competing positions and who retained Baden.

### Gaps
- None material to the verdict.

## 6. July 2025 DOJ/FBI memo and the jail video ("missing minute")

### Takeaway
On July 7, 2025, an unsigned two-page DOJ/FBI memo concluded suicide, no "client list," no credible blackmail evidence, and no basis to charge third parties. It also released jail video. A minute after about 11:59 p.m. was missing from that release. Bondi attributed the gap to a nightly reset. A fuller version released by Congress on Sept 2, 2025 includes the minute, and CBS reports nothing unusual in it.

### Cited Findings
- REPORTED (memo): Unsigned, two-page memo, July 7, 2025. Found no "client list" and said no "further disclosure would be appropriate or warranted" — [AP via Live 5 News, July 7, 2025](https://www.live5news.com/2025/07/07/epstein-client-list-doesnt-exist-justice-department-says-walking-back-theory-bondi-had-promoted/). The memo says the video "supports a medical examiner's finding" of suicide. It cites "no credible evidence" of blackmail and nothing that could predicate an investigation of uncharged third parties. Raw and "enhanced" (contrast-increased) footage was released — [Axios, July 7, 2025](https://www.axios.com/2025/07/07/jeffrey-epstein-suicide-client-list-trump-administration)
- REPORTED (missing minute): The FBI release ran about 7:40 p.m. to 6:40 a.m. A minute just after 11:59 p.m. was missing. Bondi said "every night should have that same missing minute." The fuller Sept 2, 2025 congressional release (6 p.m.–7 a.m.) includes the minute and shows nothing apparently unusual; CBS says this contradicts the reset explanation. Experts told CBS the FBI file looked like a screen recording rather than raw footage. The congressional files lack confirming metadata — [CBS News, Sept 3, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/jeffrey-epstein-missing-minute-jail-video-shows)
- CONFIRMED: DOJ's July 18, 2025 court filing states Epstein "committed suicide in his cell" — [DOJ motion, July 18, 2025](https://c1.legalinsurrection.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/DOJ-Asks-Court-to-Unseal-Epstein-Grand-Jury.pdf)

### Inferences
- The file's section 5 omits the July 2025 memo and the video release entirely. For a complete record, add: (a) the 2025 memo's suicide conclusion; (b) the missing-minute controversy; (c) the fact that the gap was later filled and showed nothing unusual, while questions about video provenance remain.

### Gaps
- CBS's earlier (July 2025) metadata analysis of the FBI file, reporting that the file was assembled from clips, was not fetched. The Sept 3 piece does not state that finding.
