Checked on Nov 13, 2026. Verdict labels: CONFIRMED (primary source or two independent reputable outlets), REPORTED (one reputable outlet), CONTESTED (sources disagree, or the subject denies it), NOT VERIFIED (no adequate source found), WRONG (correct fact given).

**How this was done, and what limited it.** Web searches returned titles and links but no text, so every verdict below rests on pages that were actually fetched and read. Five pages could not be read:

- An AP story from Dec 23, 2025 on the 2007 draft indictment, in the Detroit News (blocked by robots.txt).

Without the OPR report, several exact dates (July 31, 2007; Sept 24, 2007; June 30, 2008) could not be confirmed from a primary source here, and they are labelled accordingly. ## Q1. Palm Beach police, 2001–2005 (section 2)

### Takeaway
The broad outline holds up. The department had an earlier look at Epstein and Maxwell (a 2001 report, trash pulls, closed in April 2002), and in 2005 it opened the case that led to the federal investigation. Several specifics are wrong or unsupported:
- The 2001 report is dated December, not March.
- The 14-year-old's family first reported by phone, and the police report gives $300, not $200.
- The 82-page prosecution memo and the 53-page indictment were drafted by federal prosecutors, not local police.
- The Reddit-sourced details (Sgt. Frick, the Oct 2003 burglary, the Nov 2004 driveway incident, the $90,000 check, Capt. Gudger, the "police officers" quote) found no reputable replacement source.

The section's framing of the department as a place of "systemic paralysis" sits uneasily with the record that Chief Reiter pushed the case forward and went to the FBI.

## Q2. The 2007 Non-Prosecution Agreement and the CVRA litigation (section 3.1)

### Takeaway
The substance is confirmed: no federal charges; a state guilty plea to two charges (one of them procuring a *minor*); under 13 months served on generous work release; immunity for "any potential co-conspirators"; victims kept in the dark. Exact dates (July 31, 2007; Sept 24, 2007; June 30, 2008) could not be read from a primary source here.

The file never mentions the outcome of the victims' lawsuit under the Crime Victims' Rights Act (CVRA): the trial court found the government broke the law in Feb 2019, and the full 11th Circuit (en banc) ruled against the victims on April 15, 2021.

  - Holding: the CVRA gives victims no private right of action to enforce their rights in court when no criminal case has been filed. The court said it "needn't decide" whether CVRA rights apply before charges — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca11/19-13843/19-13843-2021-04-15.html).

## Q3. OPR report, Nov 2020 (section 3.2)

### Takeaway
The core findings are confirmed: "poor judgment," no professional misconduct, victims left "confused and ill-treated," and a "misimpression" of collusion.

Three things in the file are wrong or overstated:
- "Decades later" is wrong. OPR opened its review in early 2019, about 11 years after the deal.
- The "clear and unambiguous" test was OPR's standard for misconduct, applied to questions such as a duty to notify victims. It was not about a rule against "deferring a case to state authorities via a secret agreement."
- Acosta's resignation (announced July 12, 2019) did come before the report (Nov 12, 2020), so that timing is right.

### Cited Findings
- **"OPR launched an investigation amid public backlash" and "decades later."** The first part is CONFIRMED; "decades" is WRONG. Sen. Ben Sasse pressed DOJ to open the inquiry in Feb 2019, after the Herald series, and the probe ran 21 months — [Fox News, Nov 12, 2020](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/doj-no-alexander-acosta-jeffrey-epstein-sex-trafficking-case). The NPA was signed in 2007.
- **"Report finalized after Acosta resigned as Labor Secretary in July 2019."** CONFIRMED. Acosta announced his resignation on July 12, 2019, effective seven days later — [PBS/AP, July 12, 2019](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/labor-secretary-acosta-resigns-amid-epstein-scandal). The OPR executive summary was released Nov 12, 2020 — [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/doj-no-alexander-acosta-jeffrey-epstein-sex-trafficking-case); [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/acosta-used-poor-judgment-in-epstein-plea-deal-doj-finds/).
  - "Forced to resign" is a characterization. AP reports a resignation amid scrutiny, after a news conference defending the deal on Wednesday, July 10, 2019.
- **"'Poor judgment' but not professional misconduct."** CONFIRMED — [Courthouse News, Nov 12, 2020](https://www.courthousenews.com/acosta-used-poor-judgment-in-epstein-plea-deal-doj-finds/); [Fox News, Nov 12, 2020](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/doj-no-alexander-acosta-jeffrey-epstein-sex-trafficking-case)
- **"'No clear and unambiguous standard' prohibited deferring to the state via a secret agreement."** Partly WRONG as paraphrased. OPR found no "clear and unambiguous duty" to inform victims before the deal — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/acosta-used-poor-judgment-in-epstein-plea-deal-doj-finds/). OPR's misconduct test requires breaking a clear and unambiguous obligation. The file's wording about deferral "via a secret agreement" is not supported by what was read.
- **"Victims left 'confused and ill-treated'; 'misimpression that the government had colluded.'"** CONFIRMED — [Fox News, Nov 12, 2020](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/doj-no-alexander-acosta-jeffrey-epstein-sex-trafficking-case); [Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/119137/timeline-jeffrey-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell/). OPR also found victims were not treated with the "forthrightness and sensitivity expected" — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/acosta-used-poor-judgment-in-epstein-plea-deal-doj-finds/).
- **"Severely undercut public confidence."** NOT VERIFIED as worded. The OPR text was not read, so the adverb "severely" may be the file's own.
- **Reactions (context; these are opinions).** Victims' lawyer Adam Horowitz: the mistakes were "not just poor judgment. It was reckless." Sen. Tim Kaine asked how this was anything "but gross professional misconduct" — [Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/119137/timeline-jeffrey-epstein-ghislaine-maxwell/). No statement from Acosta was found in the coverage read.
- **"Third-order insight: frameworks tilted toward discretionary power."** Commentary, not a fact.

### Inferences
- The timing sentence should read: OPR opened its review in early 2019, Acosta resigned in July 2019, and the report came out Nov 12, 2020.

### Gaps
- The exact OPR quotation and page numbers for the "clear and unambiguous" standard and for "undercut public confidence" need the report itself (justice.gov/opr). It could not be read in this session.
