## Claim 1: Notification of emergency arms sales (May 24, 2019)

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED (primary). The CRS Insight and the State OIG report both support May 24, 2019 and "more than $8 billion". The OIG gives about $8.1 billion. One nuance: the formal emergency determination was made after close of business May 23.

### Cited Findings
- CRS Insight IN11127 dates the notice May 24, 2019 and says "with a possible value of more than $8 billion". It names Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Jordan (Jordan via a UAE gift transfer of precision-guided munitions). — [CRS IN11127 (everycrsreport copy)](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20190531_IN11127_aa0147522ff9d7a686caebb2b0758e2c1d8f1724.html)
- OIG ISP-I-20-19: "a total value of approximately $8.1 billion". It was "notified to Congress and announced publicly the following day, on May 24". The Secretary's determination came after close of business May 23. — [OIG ISP-I-20-19 (oversight.gov copy)](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2020-08/ISP-I-20-19.pdf)
- Verdict: CONFIRMED (primary).

### Inferences
- Block wording is accurate. Optional precision: "notified Congress on May 24, 2019 (determination made May 23) of emergency arms sales valued at about $8.1 billion".

### Gaps
- DSCA/Federal Register notices were not opened. The CRS PDF at sgp.fas.org returned empty, so the everycrsreport HTML copy was used.

## Claim 2: Vetoes (April 16 and July 24, 2019) and no override

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED. April 16 veto of S.J.Res. 7 is a primary read. The Senate override attempt failed May 2, 2019 (53-45). The July 24 veto and the July 29 failed overrides are CONFIRMED-SECONDARY.

### Cited Findings
- Veto message, S.J.Res. 7, dated April 16, 2019. Quote: "returning herewith without my approval S.J. Res. 7" — [White House archive](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/presidential-actions/presidential-veto-message-senate-accompany-s-j-res-7/) (primary, CONFIRMED).
- Senate override vote on S.J.Res. 7, May 2, 2019: "Not passed over the President's veto: 53-45" (Senate Democrats' roll-call relay, not senate.gov) — [Senate Dems](https://www.democrats.senate.gov/2019/05/02/roll-call-vote-on-veto-override-re-sjres7-yemen) (CONFIRMED-SECONDARY). Two-thirds was needed. A 53-45 count short of 67 is consistent with this.
- Three resolutions (S.J.Res. 36, 37, 38, Menendez) vetoed July 24, 2019. — [Ballotpedia](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2019/07/26/president-trump-vetoes-three-resolutions-related-to-arms-sales-bringing-his-veto-total-to-five/); [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/congress/2019/07/29/us-senate-allows-arms-sales-to-saudi-arabia-sustaining-trump-vetoes/) (CONFIRMED-SECONDARY).
- Senate override votes July 29, 2019 failed, counts 45-40, 45-39 and 46-41; 67 needed. — Defense News (same URL). The S.J.Res. 36 vote is independently shown as "failed, 45-40" at [Senate Dems](https://www.democrats.senate.gov/2019/07/29/roll-call-vote-on-veto-message-with-sjres36-saudi-arms-sales). Defense News does not map counts to S.J.Res. 37/38.

### Inferences
- "The Senate did not override either" is right. Suggested precision: "The Senate failed to override the Yemen veto (53-45, May 2) and the three arms-sale vetoes (July 29, votes of 45-40, 45-39, 46-41)."

### Gaps
- Not opened: congress.gov/senate.gov roll calls, July veto messages on whitehouse.gov or federalregister.gov. The S.J.Res. 37 and 38 votes are known only from the Defense News tally.

## Claim 3: State OIG finding (Aug 2020)

### Takeaway
PARTLY OVERSTATED. The OIG found the Secretary's emergency certification was executed in accordance with the AECA. It did not find that "the sales were carried out in accordance" with the Act. The civilian-casualty criticism is accurate.

### Cited Findings
- Quote: "executed in accordance with the requirements of the AECA" (it refers to the emergency certification). — [OIG ISP-I-20-19](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2020-08/ISP-I-20-19.pdf)
- Quote: "did not fully assess risks and implement mitigation measures" for civilian casualties. Detail is in a classified annex. Same URL.
- The transmittal memo is dated Aug 10, 2020. — Same URL.
- Verdict: certification point CONFIRMED (primary, via the oversight.gov copy; state.gov/oig.state.gov not opened). Block wording "the sales were carried out in accordance with the AECA" is CONTRADICTED in scope. The finding covers the certification, not the sales.

### Inferences
- Corrected wording: "The State Department inspector general found in August 2020 that the Secretary's emergency certification met the Arms Export Control Act's requirements, but that the Department did not fully assess risks to civilians or put mitigation measures in place."

### Gaps
- oig.state.gov original page and the classified annex not opened.

## Claim 4: Linick firing

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED-SECONDARY as a two-sided dispute. The "management and ethics" gloss on the State position is loose. The rationales State officials gave were failure to perform, management shortcomings, leaks and the handling of the leak inquiry. "Ethics" has only thin support.

### Cited Findings
- Democrats' side: Rep. Engel said the arms-sale probe "may be another reason" for the firing. Engel also said the committee lacked the full picture. — [Defense One](https://defenseone.com/policy/2020/05/fired-state-ig-was-investigating-saudi-arms-sale/165469); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2020/05/18/state-watchdog-investigating-trump-saudi-arm-sale).
- Trump's letter to Pelosi said only that he "lost confidence in Linick". — Defense One (same URL).
- Pompeo to the Washington Post said Linick "wasn't performing a function" as asked. Bulatao pointed to leaks and Linick's lack of support for an ethics statement. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloombergquint.com/politics/trump-ouster-of-state-official-stirs-fear-of-war-on-watchdogs). Paraphrase from a fetch summary, not read in full.
- Bulatao (Sept 2020 testimony) cited a late FY2019 audit, low morale, unfilled deputy IG and GC posts, a leaked report, and mishandling of the leak inquiry. He said removal "was not about retaliation on any specific report". — [GovExec](https://govexec.com/management/2020/09/high-ranking-state-dept-official-testifies-ig-was-not-ousted-political-retaliation/168532). That summary does not use "ethics".
- Pompeo denied retaliation and gave no specifics. He said "I couldn't possibly have retaliated". — [VOA via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2020/05/mil-200520-voa03.htm).
- Linick said no valid reason was given. — [Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/people/2020/06/fired-state-department-ig-cites-bullying-from-senior-officials/257758).
- Timing: the Washington Times (via the fetch tool) and Defense One put the firing in mid-May 2020. Defense One shows a USA Today story dated May 16. "Mid-May 2020" is safe.

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "Democrats tie the May 2020 firing to Linick's review of the arms sales. The State Department denies retaliation and cites management shortcomings, handling of a leak, and failure to perform."

### Gaps
- No State press release or on-record "management and ethics" statement found. The Bloomberg "ethics statement" detail rests on one secondary source. The Pompeo Washington Post interview original was not opened.

## Claim 6: March-April 2018 sales vs Crown Prince visit

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED-SECONDARY with one wording fix. "March 22 and 23" is loose. Notification to Congress was March 22, and reporting appeared March 23.

### Cited Findings
- Crown Prince met Trump on March 20, 2018. The anti-tank missile notification came "two days after the Oval Office meeting", about March 22. The notification covered more than 6,500 anti-tank missiles, valued over $1 billion. — [Arms Control Association](https://armscontrol.org/act/2018-04/news/trump-touts-saudi-arms-sales) (summary of a fetch; the page did not name the missile type in my read).
- White House notified Congress "Thursday, March 22, 2018". The article is dated March 23. — [Military.com](https://military.com/daily-news/2018/03/23/trump-administration-approves-1-billion-arms-sale-saudi-arabia.html).
- $1.3 billion artillery sale: dated April 5, 2018, with a DSCA statement that day. — [Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2018/apr/5/us-signs-billion-dollar-arms-deal-saudi-arabia/). This is a DSCA announcement date, not read from a DSCA primary.

### Inferences
- Suggested wording: "about $1 billion in anti-tank missiles was notified March 22, two days after the March 20 visit, and the $1.3 billion artillery sale was announced April 5".

### Gaps
- DSCA original notices not opened. Whether the formal notification of the artillery sale equals the announcement date is unverified. Press accounts only.
