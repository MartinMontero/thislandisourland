# Saudi nuclear (IP3 / Section 123) claims: source check as of Nov 14, 2026

Method note: pages were read with WebFetch, which returns a model summary of each page, not raw text. Quotes below are only those the tool returned in quotation marks. "Primary" means a government, committee or party-staff document, or an official release. Congress.gov refused automated access, so bill facts rest on govinfo.gov bill text and a bill tracker.

## Claim 1: Signed July 22, 2026

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED (primary release plus secondary). One nuance: Arms Control Association says Energy Secretary Wright signed July 20 and the Saudi minister signed July 22, so July 22 is the date signing was completed.

### Cited Findings
- Energy.gov release dated July 22, 2026: the two ministers "signed a peaceful nuclear cooperation agreement, commonly known as a 123 agreement", plus a bilateral safeguards agreement; signers are Energy Secretary Chris Wright and Saudi Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman. The page gives its own date as July 22 but does not state a signing date in the body. — [Energy.gov](https://www.energy.gov/node/4859590)
- Al-Monitor: signed "Wednesday, July 22, 2026" (as summarised by the fetch tool). — [Al-Monitor](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/07/us-saudi-arabia-sign-landmark-nuclear-power-agreement)
- ANS: Wright "said Wednesday" they had signed (article dated July 23). — [ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-07-23/article-8238/saudi-arabia-us-sign-civil-nuclear-energy-deal/)
- Arms Control Association: Trump signed the determination July 16; Wright signed July 20; Abdulaziz signed July 22. — [ACA](https://www.armscontrol.org/node/15446)

### Inferences
- "Signed on July 22" is accurate for completion of signature. Safe wording: "signed July 22, 2026 (Energy Secretary Wright's signature reportedly dated July 20)".

### Gaps
- whitehouse.gov and state.gov releases not opened.

## Claim 2: Sent to Congress Sept 14, 90-day review ends about Dec 13; House resolution Sept 17; Senate resolution Oct 19

### Takeaway
CONTRADICTED on the transmittal date and the Senate date; PARTLY SUPPORTED on the House date and the Dec 13 end. The White House message to Congress is dated August 24, 2026, not September 14. The Senate resolution was introduced October 15, not October 19. The House resolution was introduced September 16 per the bill text and tracker (press reported it Sept 17).

### Cited Findings
- Presidential message transmitting the agreement is dated August 24, 2026 and was "Referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs" on August 27, 2026 (House Document 119-191). — [GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CDOC-119hdoc191/html/CDOC-119hdoc191.htm)
- Arms Control Association: agreement and nonproliferation assessment submitted Aug. 24, starting a 90-day review. — [ACA](https://www.armscontrol.org/node/15446)
- Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists: submitted August 24; Congress was in recess; the statute counts only "continuous session" days, so the clock "will likely stay stopped until November". The same piece says this Congress ends before 90 days expire and that continuous session does not carry into the next Congress. — [Bulletin](https://thebulletin.org/2026/10/congress-has-the-power-to-fix-the-trump-saudi-nuclear-deal/)
- AP (via KSAT) on Aug 26: submitted to Capitol Hill "on Monday" (Aug 24); anonymous sources. — [KSAT/AP](https://www.ksat.com/news/politics/2026/08/26/trump-has-submitted-civil-nuclear-agreement-with-saudi-arabia-to-congress-for-review-ap-sources-say/)
- Where "September 14" comes from: the House resolution's own title reads "submitted to the Congress on September 14, 2026". H.J.Res. 217 bill text: introduced Sept 16, 2026, sponsor Meeks with Sherman, Garamendi, Beyer; referred to Foreign Affairs. — [GovInfo bill text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hjres217ih/html/BILLS-119hjres217ih.htm); [FastDemocracy tracker](https://fastdemocracy.com/bill-search/us/119/bills/USB00108166/)
- AP (via GV Wire, Oct 2): senators have a "90-day window from its printing in the Senate record on September 14", with a deadline of December 13, 2026; senators "expect it before December 13". So Sept 14 and Dec 13 are the Senate-record counting, not the date the White House sent it. — [GV Wire/AP](https://gvwire.com/2026/10/02/us-senators-expect-vote-after-midterms-on-trumps-nuclear-power-pact-with-saudi-arabia/)
- House resolution reported as introduced "on Thursday" in an article dated Sept 17 (Sept 17 is a Thursday; the bill text says Sept 16, a Wednesday). — [Jewish Insider](https://jewishinsider.com/2026/09/house-democrats-resolution-block-saudi-nuclear-deal/)
- Senate resolution is S.J.Res. 221, introduced Oct 15, 2026, referred to Foreign Relations the same day (Markey listed; Merkley and Markey announced it). — [FastDemocracy tracker](https://fastdemocracy.com/bill-search/us/119/bills/USB00109065/); [Jewish Insider](https://jewishinsider.com/2026/10/senate-vote-saudi-nuclear-deal-end-of-year/) ("on Thursday", Oct 15)
- Oct 19 is the date of the ANS article, which itself says the resolution was announced Oct 15. — [ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-10-19/article-8500/merkley-and-markey-introduce-resolution-to-block-ussaudi-deal/)
- Senate vote: Merkley and Markey say the resolution "will be voted on after November 29"; Jewish Insider says "Nov. 29 at the earliest". — [ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-10-19/article-8500/merkley-and-markey-introduce-resolution-to-block-ussaudi-deal/); [Jewish Insider](https://jewishinsider.com/2026/10/senate-vote-saudi-nuclear-deal-end-of-year/)
- No news found of any floor vote or committee action through Nov 14, 2026. The FastDemocracy House page showed no action beyond referral (page capture date unknown).

### Inferences
- The block conflates two dates. Aug 24 is the transmittal; Sept 14 appears to be the date used by the Senate record and by the House resolution title. Dec 13 is an AP-reported deadline under the Senate counting, but the Bulletin argues the clock started Aug 24 and pauses in recess, which would give a different, later end. Treat "ends about December 13" as one reported reading, not settled.
- Under ACA's simple arithmetic (Aug 24 plus 90 days) the end is about Nov 22; recess days do not count under the statute, so that is not reliable either.

### Gaps
- Could not open congress.gov (blocked) for H.J.Res. 217 or S.J.Res. 221 actions, nor the Congressional Record page showing the Sept 14 entry. Could not open the FDD pieces (Aug 28, Oct 30). Federal Register item 2026-15273 and whitehouse.gov not opened. Why a Sept 14 date exists at all is not explained in any source I read.

### Proposed wording
"The agreement was signed July 22, 2026. The White House sent it to Congress on August 24; the Senate record and the House resolution date its submission September 14. House Democrats introduced H.J.Res. 217 on September 16; Senators Merkley and Markey introduced S.J.Res. 221 on October 15. Reports put the end of the 90-day review at about December 13, though the count depends on days in session."

## Claim 3: House Democratic staff reports (Feb 19, July 2019), citing whistleblowers, alleged IP3 pushed a plan that skipped normal review

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED-SECONDARY in substance, with two wording problems: IP3 is described in the reports as a private company that assembled a consortium (led by retired generals), and the "skipped review" allegation is about White House/NSC conduct, not IP3 itself. Also Democrats were the committee majority in 2019, so these are majority staff reports.

### Cited Findings
- First interim staff report, Feb 2019 (prepared for Chairman Cummings): multiple White House and NSC staff came forward; report says the Atomic Energy Act requires a Section 123 agreement and congressional approval before transfer; alleges work continued after NSC legal advisor Eisenberg ordered a stop on Jan 30, 2017; cites possible "potential violation" of the Act. IP3 is a private company with a consortium; leaders include retired generals Keith Alexander, Jack Keane, Michael Hewitt and Bud McFarlane. — [Oversight Democrats PDF, Feb 19, 2019](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-oversight.house.gov/files/Trump%20Saudi%20Nuclear%20Report%20-%202-19-2019.pdf)
- Second interim staff report, July 2019 (press release dated July 29, 2019): relies on documents from IP3 and others and on whistleblower accounts; alleges IP3 pressed the administration to drop the "Gold Standard", and describes legal and ethical concerns, including a canceled Bolton meeting. Only the first 100,000 of 125,359 characters of this PDF were returned to me. — [Oversight Democrats PDF, July 2019](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-oversight.house.gov/files/Trump%20Saudi%20Nuclear%20Report%20July%202019.pdf); [press release](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/new-documents-show-corporate-and-foreign-interests-seek-to-influence-us-nuclear)
- The February date: the July report itself refers to the "February 19, 2019 first interim report". — same July PDF

### Inferences
- The block's phrase "a group of retired generals and executives (IP3)" is loose; "IP3, a private company led by retired generals" is closer.
- "Skipped normal review" is a fair gloss of the allegation that officials kept working after lawyers said the Section 123 and congressional-approval requirements could not be bypassed, but it was alleged conduct by officials, framed as a potential violation.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full text of either PDF line by line; I read summaries. The July report's second half (past character 100,000) was not returned.

### Proposed wording
"House Oversight Committee Democrats, then the majority, issued staff reports on Feb 19 and July 29, 2019. Citing whistleblowers and company documents, they alleged that IP3, a private company led by retired generals, lobbied for a plan to build Saudi reactors and that White House staff kept advancing it after National Security Council lawyers said the legal process could not be bypassed."

### Takeaway
CONFIRMED (primary read). The report is dated July 22, 2019. One sequencing note: it came a week before the second Democratic report (July 29), so it answered the February report and the ongoing probe, not the July report.

### Cited Findings

- Committee Republicans' release, July 22, 2019, via Ranking Member Jordan: headline "Committee Investigation Shows No Impropriety in Nuclear Energy Transfers to Saudi Arabia". — [Oversight Republicans release](https://oversight.house.gov/release/committee-investigation-shows-no-impropriety-in-nuclear-energy-transfers-to-saudi-arabia)

### Inferences
- "Answered in July 2019" is acceptable; "answered both reports" would not be.

### Gaps
- Read via the summarising fetch tool, not a raw PDF extraction. The quote was returned in quotation marks and the date matches the file name.

## Claim 5: "Barrack was never made envoy and no reactor or nuclear material was transferred"

### Takeaway
Barrack half: CONTRADICTED as worded. He was named US Special Envoy for Syria in May 2025 (while Ambassador to Turkey). It is true only that he did not get the posts he reportedly sought in 2017. Transfer half: NOT VERIFIED as an affirmative statement; sources show Part 810 authorizations covered discussions and marketing, and say nothing about a transfer.

### Cited Findings
- Barrack was announced May 23, 2025 as State Department special envoy for Syria while remaining US Ambassador to Turkey; he said he was "proud to assume the role of the US Special Envoy for Syria." — [Al-Monitor](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2025/05/trump-appoints-turkey-ambassador-tom-barrack-envoy-syria-what-know)
- A 2026 headline reports Trump naming Barrack envoy to Syria and Iraq (page not opened; headline from search results only). — [Enab Baladi](https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2026/05/trump-names-thomas-barrack-envoy-to-syria-and-iraq/amp/)
- July 2019 Democratic report: Barrack "sought the Special Envoy to the Middle East and UAE Ambassador posts" while promoting the nuclear plan (fetch-tool summary, not verbatim). — [Oversight Democrats PDF, July 2019](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-oversight.house.gov/files/Trump%20Saudi%20Nuclear%20Report%20July%202019.pdf)
- CRS: DOE granted seven Part 810 authorizations in 2017 to 2019 allowing US companies to hold civil nuclear discussions, including marketing, with Saudi Arabia; these do not require a 123 agreement. CRS says nothing about any reactor or material transferred. (Page is the May 26, 2026 version, before signing.) — [CRS IF10799](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF10799.html)
- The 123 agreement was only transmitted in August 2026 and is under review, so no transfer could lawfully occur under it yet. That follows from the review process in claim 2 and is not a quoted statement. — [ACA](https://www.armscontrol.org/node/15446)

### Inferences
- Part 810 authorizations after 2019, and any under the 2026 agreement, were not found. Their absence from my searches is not evidence that none exist.
- The Part 810 category (technical assistance and marketing) differs from a physical transfer of a reactor or nuclear material, which needs a 123 agreement and export licenses; the block's wording does not make that distinction.

### Gaps
- No source states "no reactor or nuclear material has been transferred". Could not open Stimson, NRC, DOE Part 810 reports, or the Barrack verdict. Barrack's envoy status as of Nov 2026 not independently checked beyond the 2025 appointment.

### Proposed wording
"In 2017 Barrack reportedly sought, but did not receive, the Middle East envoy and UAE ambassador posts; he later became ambassador to Turkey and special envoy for Syria (2025). The Department of Energy authorized seven Part 810 permissions in 2017 to 2019 for US companies to discuss and market nuclear technology to Saudi Arabia; we found no report of a reactor or nuclear material being transferred, and the 2026 agreement has not taken effect."
