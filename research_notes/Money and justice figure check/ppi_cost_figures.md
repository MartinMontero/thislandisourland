# PPI incarceration-cost figures: $445 billion vs. $54.3 billion / 13%

Checked as of Nov 15, 2026. All pages were opened with WebFetch. Quotes are kept under 15 words.

## Does "The Whole Pie 2026" say "at least $445 billion a year," and where does that figure come from?

### Takeaway
Yes. Whole Pie 2026 gives "at least $445 billion" as the system-wide yearly cost. Its footnote 2 sources that figure to PPI's *Following the Money of Mass Incarceration 2026* (Kang-Brown and Wagner, Feb 11, 2026). In that report, $445B is the sum of two numbers: $417.1B in 2025 government spending (policing, corrections, immigration policing and detention, courts) and $27.7B a year paid by system-involved people and their families.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED: Whole Pie 2026 says "at a system-wide cost of at least $445 billion each year." The sentence follows a list of confinement systems (prisons, jails, youth facilities, immigration detention, and others). [Whole Pie 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/pie2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: Footnote 2 of Whole Pie 2026 points to the 2026 *Following the Money* report for the $445B figure. It calls the figure "undoubtedly a low estimate." [Whole Pie 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/pie2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: *Following the Money 2026* says the system costs "the government and families of system-involved people at least $445 billion every year." Published Feb 11, 2026, by Jacob Kang-Brown and Peter Wagner. [Following the Money 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/money2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: Table 1 of that report gives 2025 government spending of $417.1B: policing $203.2B, corrections $115.8B, immigration policing and detention $54.3B, judicial and legal $43.8B. [Following the Money 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/money2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: The same report says individuals and their families "pay over $27.7 billion a year." This covers fines and fees, bail premiums, civil asset forfeiture, phone calls, and commissary. [Following the Money 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/money2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: Data years. Federal figures are FY2025 OMB budget data plus annualized P.L. 119-21 funding. State and local figures come from the Census Bureau's 2023 Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances and March 2024 payroll data. Some components are older: health care uses 2015 data, food and utilities 2001. [Following the Money 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/money2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: Courts are counted at roughly half of total court spending (about $43.8B of $87.6B), on the assumption that 50% of court spending relates to criminal law. Private prisons (about $5.5B in 2025 revenue) are treated as part of public spending, not added on top. Lost income from incarceration is excluded. Costs under $1B are left out. [Following the Money 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/money2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: PPI's graph page describes "the $445 billion system of mass incarceration." [PPI graph page](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/graphs/money2026.html)
- REPORTED: Secondary coverage reused the figure, for example a Davis Vanguard headline: "$445 Billion Spent on Policing, Courts, and Corrections Annually." Seen only as a search result title; the page was not opened. [Davis Vanguard](https://davisvanguard.org/2026/02/mass-incarceration-system-costs/)

### Inferences
- $417.1B + $27.7B = $444.8B, which rounds to $445B. The report does not spell out that addition in one sentence (NOT VERIFIED as an explicit statement). However, its headline wording ("government and families") and its two component figures match exactly.
- So $445B is not a government-only number. It includes about $27.7B in costs paid by individuals and families. It also includes policing and courts, not only incarceration.
- The figure mixes data years: mainly FY2025 federal budgets and 2023–2024 state and local data, plus a few older components. It is best described as a current yearly estimate, not a single-year 2025 actual.

### Gaps
- The report has no single sentence that says "$417.1B + $27.7B = $445B." The breakdown above is derived arithmetic that matches PPI's stated components and headline wording.

## Where do "$54.3 billion in 2025, up from $20 billion in 2017" and "13% of government criminal legal system spending" come from?

### Takeaway
Both figures come from *Following the Money 2026*, and Whole Pie 2026 repeats them. The 13% has a government-only denominator: $54.3B ÷ $417.1B = 13.0%. It is not 13% of $445B, which would be 12.2%. The $20B figure for 2017 is in 2017 dollars (unadjusted). In 2025 dollars it is $26.3B.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED: Whole Pie 2026 says immigration-related policing and detention "totaled $54.3 billion in 2025 (up from $20 billion in 2017)" (footnote 11). It also says this "makes up a staggering 13% of all government spending" on the criminal legal system. No separate footnote is attached to the 13% sentence. [Whole Pie 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/pie2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: *Following the Money 2026* says immigration spending "now makes up 13% of all government spending" on the criminal legal system. It also says the budget is "2.7 times larger than in 2017." [Following the Money 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/money2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: Table 1 gives immigration policing and detention as $20.0B in 2017 (unadjusted), $26.3B in 2017 adjusted to 2025 dollars, and $54.3B in 2025. Government totals are $288.9B in 2017 (unadjusted), $379.8B in 2017 (adjusted), and $417.1B in 2025. [Following the Money 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/money2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: PPI calls the immigration figure "a lower bound." It includes annualized multi-year funding from P.L. 119-21 (the 2025 reconciliation law), alongside ICE budget justifications and a CBO estimate for National Guard deployments. [Following the Money 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/money2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: A separate share appears in the same report: federal policing and deportation-related spending together make up 25% of government criminal legal spending. [Following the Money 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/money2026.html)

### Inferences
- $54.3B ÷ $417.1B = 13.02%, so the 13% is consistent with the government-only total. $54.3B ÷ $445B = 12.2%, which would not round to 13%. The denominator is therefore $417.1B, not $445B.
- "$54.3B, up from $20B" sets a 2025-dollar figure beside a 2017-dollar figure. "2.7 times larger" is $54.3B ÷ $20.0B, also nominal. In constant 2025 dollars the rise is $26.3B to $54.3B, about 2.1 times. The site's wording reproduces PPI's own wording, so it is not wrong. It does overstate real growth unless a reader understands that $20B is unadjusted.
- PPI says most of the growth happened in 2025. Part of the $54.3B is multi-year reconciliation money spread across years. This is a budgeted and appropriated figure, not audited 2025 outlays.

### Gaps
- PPI's year-by-year immigration spending for 2018–2024 was not checked. Only the 2017 and 2025 endpoints were verified.
- The underlying DHS/ICE/CBP budget documents were not opened. The $54.3B is verified only as PPI's own compilation.

## Do the figures contradict each other, and what is the correct pairing?

### Takeaway
They do not contradict each other. Both come from the same report (*Following the Money 2026*) and the same yearly estimate, but they use different totals. $445B covers government spending plus costs paid by families. The 13% share is measured against the government-only total of $417.1B. Nothing is wrong. The only risk is a reader assuming that 13% is a share of $445B.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED: $445B is described as the cost to "the government and families of system-involved people." [Following the Money 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/money2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: The 13% is described as a share of "all government spending" on the criminal legal system. Table 1 puts that total at $417.1B. [Following the Money 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/money2026.html); [Whole Pie 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/pie2026.html)
- CONFIRMED: Whole Pie 2026 has a separate, unrelated "13%": at least 1 in 8 jail bookings is tied to a missed court appearance (footnote 38). Do not confuse it with the immigration 13%. [Whole Pie 2026](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/pie2026.html)

### Inferences
- Correct pairing:
  - $445B = $417.1B in government spending + about $27.7B paid by individuals and families (policing, corrections, immigration enforcement and detention, criminal courts).
  - $54.3B = 13% of the $417.1B in government spending, in 2025.
- Suggested wording for the site:
  - "The Prison Policy Initiative estimates that mass incarceration costs governments and families at least $445 billion a year. That total counts policing, courts, prisons, jails, and immigration detention, plus about $28 billion paid by people in the system and their families."
  - "Federal spending on immigration policing and detention reached $54.3 billion in 2025, up from $20 billion in 2017 (about $26 billion in today's dollars). That is 13% of the $417 billion that governments spend on the criminal legal system."
- If space is tight, the smallest change is to keep "13% of government spending" and add "($417 billion)." Do not describe the 13% as a share of the $445B.
- Cite *Following the Money of Mass Incarceration 2026* (PPI, Feb 11, 2026) as the primary source for both figures. Whole Pie 2026 repeats them.

### Gaps
- None material. Every claim above was checked against PPI's own pages. The only derived points are the arithmetic ($417.1B + $27.7B ≈ $445B, and 54.3 ÷ 417.1 ≈ 13%).
