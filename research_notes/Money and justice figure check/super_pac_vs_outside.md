# Public Citizen's $4.2B super PAC figure and OpenSecrets' $3.9B outside-spending figure: do they contradict each other?

Status as of Nov 15, 2026. Every source URL listed was opened with WebFetch. opensecrets.org returned "SITE_BLOCKED", and so did its web.archive.org and archive.ph copies. That means no OpenSecrets page was read directly, and everything attributed to OpenSecrets below comes secondhand.

Labels used:

- **REPORTED** means read in a secondary outlet that attributes the figure to the original source.
- **NOT VERIFIED** means it could not be checked.

## Q1. What does Public Citizen's "515 donors / more than 70% / $4.2 billion" count?

### Takeaway
$4.2 billion is the amount the 515 donors of $1 million or more gave to super PACs. It is not total super PAC money. It counts **receipts (money raised)**, not spending. It covers the 2026 cycle "thus far," uses FEC filings, and includes hybrid PACs. Transfers between super PACs are excluded. The report never states the total that the "more than 70%" is a share of.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.** Title: "Millionaires and Billionaires Fuel 2026 Super PAC Spending," by Mike Tanglis, Public Citizen. The web article is dated Oct 27, 2026, and the page was modified Oct 29. The PDF is headed "OCTOBER 2026" with no specific day. Sources: [web article](https://www.citizen.org/article/millionaires-and-billionaires-fuel-2026-super-pac-spending/), [PDF](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/1-Million-Super-PAC-Donors-1.pdf)
- **CONFIRMED.** It measures receipts. Key wording: super PACs "reported receiving $4.2 billion from 515 donors." These donors account for "more than 70 percent of the money raised by super PACs." Source: [PDF](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/1-Million-Super-PAC-Donors-1.pdf)
- **CONFIRMED.** The infographic gives the share as "73% of super PAC contributions." The subtitle says "more than 500" donors. Excluding Elon Musk, the figure is $3.8B from 514 donors. Source: [web article](https://www.citizen.org/article/millionaires-and-billionaires-fuel-2026-super-pac-spending/)
- **CONFIRMED.** The total super PAC receipts figure behind the percentage is never stated. Source: [PDF](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/1-Million-Super-PAC-Donors-1.pdf)
- **CONFIRMED.** Scope and method, from the footnotes. Source: [PDF](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/1-Million-Super-PAC-Donors-1.pdf)
  - The data is "Public Citizen's analysis of FEC data from the 2026 election cycle," calculated from FEC bulk data files.
  - "We also included Hybrid PACs."
  - A donor's gifts are combined across super PACs, so someone giving $500K to each of two super PACs counts as a $1M+ donor.
  - "Transfers from other super PACs were excluded to avoid double counting."
- **CONFIRMED.** No exact data cutoff date is given. The text says "thus far," and the latest dated source cited is Oct 23, 2026. Source: [PDF](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/1-Million-Super-PAC-Donors-1.pdf)
- **CONFIRMED.** The report does not say "federal" in so many words. Its scope is FEC filings, which by definition are federal committees. Source: [PDF](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/1-Million-Super-PAC-Donors-1.pdf)
- **CONFIRMED.** Table 1 lists each committee's "2026 receipts thus far," excluding transfers from super PACs. Source: [PDF](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/1-Million-Super-PAC-Donors-1.pdf)
  - Senate Leadership Fund: $526M
  - MAGA Inc.: $449M
  - Senate Majority PAC: $305M
  - House Majority PAC: $294M
  - Congressional Leadership Fund: $260M

### Inferences
- Dividing $4.2B by 73% gives total super PAC and hybrid PAC receipts of roughly $5.75–5.8B, excluding transfers between super PACs. This is my arithmetic, not a published figure, and should not be shown as Public Citizen's number.
- The word "Spending" in the report's title is loose. Every figure in the report measures contributions received.

### Gaps
- The exact date the FEC data was extracted. The report does not give it.
- Whether committees regulated only at the state level are excluded. FEC data implies they are, but the report never says so.

## Q2. What does OpenSecrets' "$3.9B outside spending, up 85%" and "$11.9B total" count?

### Takeaway
Both figures are **projections of spending** for the 2026 federal elections, not money raised. $11.9B is projected total federal election spending, made "once all campaign finance reports are filed." $3.9B is the projected spending by "outside political groups," up 85% from four years earlier (2022). The exact OpenSecrets definition and methodology page could not be opened.

### Cited Findings
- **REPORTED.** IBTimes, Nov 4, 2026, gives the following wording. Source: [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/2026-midterm-election-spending-record-11-9-billion-3809414)
  - "Total federal election spending is projected to reach $11.9 billion once all campaign finance reports are filed."
  - The figures come from "an OpenSecrets analysis of Federal Election Commission data."
  - Outside groups' "spending is expected to reach $3.9 billion, up 85% from four years earlier."
  - The candidate figures exclude "independent expenditures by super PACs and other outside organizations."
  - The 2022 comparison: $8.9B, or about $10.2B adjusted for inflation.
  - "Some totals may change when additional disclosures are filed."
- **REPORTED.** UA.News, republishing BBC, Nov 2, 2026, says spending "could reach a record $11.9 billion," about 30% above 2022, per OpenSecrets. The $3.9B figure is not on that page. Source: [UA.News](https://ua.news/en/world/opensecrets-prognozuie-11-9-mlrd-vitrat-na-vibori-u-ssha-bbc-news)
- **REPORTED.** Al Jazeera, Nov 3, 2026, attributes a "close to a record $12bn" total by candidates and outside groups to OpenSecrets. It does not contain the $3.9B figure. Source: [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/3/how-us-midterms-have-cost-a-record-12bn-as-cost-of-living-soars)
- **REPORTED.** How OpenSecrets has historically defined outside spending, per Ballotpedia. Source: [Ballotpedia fact check](https://ballotpedia.org/Fact_check/Is_hard_money_a_larger_share_of_political_spending_than_outside_money)
  - It covers independent expenditures, electioneering communications and communication costs.
  - Its headline outside-spending series is published "excluding party committees."
- **CONFIRMED.** The old OpenSecrets glossary, 2014 archive copy, defines independent expenditures as ads that "expressly advocate the election or defeat" of candidates. It describes super PACs as committees that raise and spend unlimited sums "for the sole purpose of making independent expenditures." Source: [UNT web archive](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/dentonfracking/20141113211728mp_/http://www.opensecrets.org/resources/learn/glossary.php)
- **NOT VERIFIED.** The OpenSecrets cost-of-election page and outside-spending page, including their methodology text. Every route was blocked:
  - https://www.opensecrets.org/elections-overview/cost-of-election
  - its web.archive.org copy
  - the archive.ph copy of opensecrets.org/outside-spending

### Inferences
- If OpenSecrets used its usual definition, the $3.9B is outside spending by groups other than candidates and party committees. That covers super PACs, hybrid PACs, dark-money nonprofits and others. It counts independent expenditures and similar communications, not money raised, cash on hand, or transfers between committees. This is inferred from OpenSecrets' historical practice, not from the 2026 methodology.
- 85% growth implies a 2022 base of about $2.1B. This is my arithmetic, not checked against OpenSecrets.

### Gaps
- OpenSecrets' own 2026 release and its methodology wording. The site was blocked from this environment.
- Whether the $3.9B includes party independent expenditures, and exactly how OpenSecrets builds its projection.

## Q3. Can super PAC receipts exceed outside spending?

### Takeaway
Yes, and by a lot. FEC figures through June 30, 2026 show super PACs and hybrid PACs raised about $6.7B but had disbursed about $5.2B. They held about $2.3B in cash. All filers together had reported only $643M in independent expenditures over the same period. Money raised and money spent on election ads are different measures.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.** FEC statistical summary, 18 months (Jan 1, 2025 – Jun 30, 2026), released Oct 1, 2026. Sources: [FEC update](https://www.fec.gov/updates/statistical-summary-of-18-month-campaign-activity-of-the-2025-2026-election-cycle/), [FEC table PAC1](https://www.fec.gov/resources/campaign-finance-statistics/2026/tables/pac/PAC1_2026_18m.pdf)

  | Committee type | Committees | Receipts | Disbursements | Cash on hand |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Independent expenditure-only committees (super PACs) | 2,419 | $2,491.7M | $1,648.9M | $1,280.3M |
  | Committees with non-contribution accounts (hybrid PACs) | 1,049 | $4,229.7M | $3,501.7M | $1,021.4M |

- **CONFIRMED.** Independent expenditures reported for the same period totalled $643.4M across all filers. Super PACs accounted for $529.6M of that, and hybrid PACs for $97.3M. Source: [FEC update](https://www.fec.gov/updates/statistical-summary-of-18-month-campaign-activity-of-the-2025-2026-election-cycle/)
- **CONFIRMED.** Public Citizen's Table 1 shows receipts for single committees through October (Senate Leadership Fund, $526M). Source: [Public Citizen PDF](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/1-Million-Super-PAC-Donors-1.pdf)
- **REPORTED.** Al Jazeera reports Senate Leadership Fund spending of $489M. It does not attribute that figure to a source, and the date and definition are unclear. Source: [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/3/how-us-midterms-have-cost-a-record-12bn-as-cost-of-living-soars)

### Inferences
- **Gap between receipts and disbursements.** About $6.72B raised against $5.15B disbursed leaves about $2.3B in cash on hand at June 30. This is my sum of the two FEC rows.
- **Gap between disbursements and independent expenditures.** Disbursements are far larger than reported independent expenditures. Likely reasons:
  - transfers to other committees
  - hybrid PACs' contributions to candidates from their limited accounts
  - overhead and fundraising costs
  - most ad spending landing after June, in the general-election stretch
- **Caveat on the FEC totals.** FEC gross receipts include transfers between committees, and hybrid PAC rows include contribution-limited accounts. They are not directly comparable to Public Citizen's transfer-excluded total.
- **Super PAC money that is not outside spending.** Super PAC receipts can exceed outside spending through:
  - money raised but not yet spent
  - transfers to other super PACs or party-linked PACs
  - operating costs
  - money still on hand after the election
- **No mismatch implied.** It is therefore not inconsistent for super PACs to have raised roughly $5.8B (implied by Public Citizen) while OpenSecrets projects $3.9B in outside spending. The two measure different flows, over different windows (raised through late October versus projected full-cycle spending), and different sets of groups (super PACs and hybrid PACs versus all outside groups).

### Gaps
- No full-cycle (post-October) FEC total for super PAC receipts or independent expenditures was found. FEC pre-general and post-general summaries were not located.

## Q4. A plain-English way to present both numbers together

### Takeaway
The numbers measure different things, so they do not contradict each other.
- **Public Citizen:** money *given to* super PACs, through late October.
- **OpenSecrets:** money *projected to be spent* by all outside groups on the elections.

Show both, and say which is money raised and which is money spent.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.** Public Citizen's figure is contributions received, with no stated denominator. Source: [Public Citizen PDF](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/1-Million-Super-PAC-Donors-1.pdf)
- **REPORTED.** OpenSecrets' figure is projected spending by outside groups, against $11.9B in projected total federal election spending. Source: [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/2026-midterm-election-spending-record-11-9-billion-3809414)

### Inferences
Recommended single sentence:

> "By late October, 515 donors who each gave at least $1 million had put $4.2 billion into super PACs — more than 70% of all the money super PACs had raised this cycle (Public Citizen); separately, OpenSecrets projects that outside groups will *spend* $3.9 billion of the $11.9 billion total on the 2026 federal elections."

Optional note: "Money raised is not the same as money spent: super PACs also pass money to other groups and keep cash in reserve."

Avoid any wording that:
- calls $4.2B "70% of the $3.9B"
- calls $4.2B "total super PAC money"
- describes the Public Citizen figure as spending

### Gaps
- If the site cites OpenSecrets directly, the OpenSecrets release should be opened from a network that can reach opensecrets.org before publishing. Until then the $3.9B / 85% figure is REPORTED via IBTimes only.
