# AIPAC compared with other country-focused and diaspora lobbies, and with foreign governments' FARA-registered lobbying (as of November 13, 2026)

Labels: CONFIRMED = read in a primary or official record this pass. REPORTED = read in news or analysis this pass and attributed. CONTESTED = sources conflict. NOT VERIFIED = from background knowledge or a search title only; not read this pass. Do not publish as fact.

## 1. Structure, PACs, election spending, lobbying and FARA status of AIPAC and the comparison groups

### Takeaway
AIPAC's political arm is far larger than any other diaspora or country-focused group found this pass. In 2024 AIPAC's PAC reported about $53.7M in direct contributions and its super PAC about $41.4M in independent spending. In 2026 the reported figures are about $53.3M and $66.7M. On October 21, 2026, AIPAC said it would stop endorsing candidates, stop direct PAC contributions and stop earmarking. Its super PAC, United Democracy Project (UDP), continues. No dollar figures were verified this pass for any comparison group.

### Cited Findings

**AIPAC / AIPAC PAC / United Democracy Project (UDP)**
- REPORTED. 2024 cycle: AIPAC direct contributions to candidates were $53.7M. UDP independent expenditures were $41.4M. — [The Intercept, Oct 29, 2026](https://theintercept.com/2026/10/29/aipac-israel-midterms-spending-elections-congress/)
- REPORTED. 2026 cycle: AIPAC direct contributions were $53.3M. UDP independent expenditures were $66.7M. UDP routed $13.1M through other committees in the 2026 primaries, against $3.5M in 2024. — [The Intercept, Oct 29, 2026](https://theintercept.com/2026/10/29/aipac-israel-midterms-spending-elections-congress/)
- CONTESTED. The same Intercept article also gives $53.6M for UDP's direct race spending this cycle and does not reconcile that with $66.7M. NPR, citing FEC filings through Sept 30, 2026, reports UDP independent expenditures of $53.5M, plus $13M given to candidates and committees. It reports AIPAC contributions of "nearly $25 million" to candidates and committees. That is far below the Intercept's $53.3M. The likely cause is earmarked or bundled individual gifts counted by one source and not the other. This is inferred, not confirmed. — [NPR via OPB, Oct 21–22, 2026](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/21/aipac-won-t-give-candidates-direct-contributions-anymore/); [The Intercept, Oct 29, 2026](https://theintercept.com/2026/10/29/aipac-israel-midterms-spending-elections-congress/)
- REPORTED. An AIPAC spokesperson said members gave "more than $90 million directly to candidates this cycle." This is an AIPAC claim and was not checked against FEC data. — [NPR via OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/21/aipac-won-t-give-candidates-direct-contributions-anymore/)
- REPORTED. Policy change on Oct 21, 2026: AIPAC will no longer endorse candidates, make direct PAC contributions or earmark funds. It will instead prioritize "direct, organized contributions from individual Americans." UDP will continue and can raise unlimited sums. Its recent spending targets Michigan Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed. — [NPR via OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/21/aipac-won-t-give-candidates-direct-contributions-anymore/)
- REPORTED. AIPAC or UDP spent on 333 of the 473 seats up in 2026. UDP spent more than $30M in the Michigan Democratic Senate primary. — [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2026/10/29/aipac-israel-midterms-spending-elections-congress/)
- REPORTED. UDP funded short-lived "pop-up PACs," which delayed donor disclosure until after the primaries. Examples: about $2.8M to Bold America; $1.65M to SD PAC. — [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2026/10/29/aipac-israel-midterms-spending-elections-congress/)
- REPORTED. A Quincy Institute paper says AIPAC and CUFI are funded by American donors. It keeps them separate from Israeli government spending. — [Quincy Institute, "The Eighth Front," Jul 24, 2026](https://quincyinst.org/research/the-eighth-front-inside-israels-1-billion-influence-campaign/)
- NOT VERIFIED (background). AIPAC is a 501(c)(4) and is not registered under FARA. Its charitable affiliate, the American Israel Education Foundation (AIEF), is a 501(c)(3). AIPAC launched its PAC and UDP in late 2021 / early 2022. NPR's "about five years" framing is consistent with this. — [NPR via OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/21/aipac-won-t-give-candidates-direct-contributions-anymore/) for the timing only.

## 2. Foreign governments' FARA-registered spending by country, and Israel's rank

### Takeaway
On FARA-registered spending, Israel has not been the top country in recent years. It ranks about 10th cumulatively for 2016–2025 at $214.6M. In 2024 it was outspent by at least Japan, Saudi Arabia, China and South Korea. Quincy reports its FARA spending rising steeply and projects it may lead all countries in 2026. That 2026 claim is a projection.

### Cited Findings
- REPORTED. 2024: Japan $48.5M, the most of any country. China $32.9M, down from a peak of $85.4M in 2023. Saudi Arabia, China and South Korea outspent Israel and Qatar in 2024. Israel was the top spender in 2018. China led from 2019 to 2022. Source: OpenSecrets data analyzed by the Washington Examiner. — [Denver Gazette / Washington Examiner, Jul 28, 2025](https://www.denvergazette.com/2025/07/28/foreigners-spend-billions-to-influence-us-politics-heres-whos-cutting-the-biggest-checks-d8d5a134-bc03-513a-8fad-968feb201536/)
- REPORTED. Israel's cumulative 2016–2025 FARA total is $214.6M, 10th in the article's table, per OpenSecrets Foreign Lobby Watch retrieved Oct 8, 2026. This is a secondary source; the OpenSecrets page itself was not read. — [Everything-PR, Apr 21, 2026, updated Nov 1, 2026](https://everything-pr.com/foreign-influence-pr-study-2026)
- REPORTED. Quincy Institute findings:
  - Israeli clients have spent more than $100M under FARA since 2023.
  - Israel's spending is on track to more than triple from 2024 to 2026, and it is on track to be the largest FARA spender in 2026.
  - Qatar, the UAE and Saudi Arabia outspent Israel over the past 10 years.
  - Israel spent $185M from 2016 to 2023, per OpenSecrets.
  - Seventeen new firms registered for Israeli interests in 2024–25.
  - Quincy's separate figure of more than $1B covers Israeli government public-diplomacy spending since Oct 7, 2023. It is not FARA spending. Quincy itself calls it "likely" spending, and it includes a proposed 2026 allocation.
  — [Quincy Institute, Jul 24, 2026](https://quincyinst.org/research/the-eighth-front-inside-israels-1-billion-influence-campaign/)
- CONTESTED (minor). The cumulative figures differ between sources: $185M for 2016–2023 (Quincy) against $214.6M for 2016–2025 (Everything-PR). These are compatible only if 2024–25 spending was about $30M. That conflicts with Quincy's claim of more than $100M since 2023. The difference likely comes from OpenSecrets data lag or from how "since 2023" is counted. Unresolved.
- REPORTED. FARA figures leave out unregistered actors and understate total influence spending. — [Denver Gazette](https://www.denvergazette.com/2025/07/28/foreigners-spend-billions-to-influence-us-politics-heres-whos-cutting-the-biggest-checks-d8d5a134-bc03-513a-8fad-968feb201536/); [Quincy](https://quincyinst.org/research/the-eighth-front-inside-israels-1-billion-influence-campaign/)

### Inferences
- On registered foreign-government lobbying, Israel is a large but not exceptional spender. Several governments, including Japan, China, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the UAE and South Korea, have matched or exceeded it in most years. The "no other nation" claim fails on the FARA measure, at least through 2025.
- AIPAC's domestic election spending (about $95–120M per cycle) exceeds Israel's annual FARA spending and exceeds any single country's reported annual FARA total in 2024. AIPAC is a U.S. organization funded by U.S. donors, so the two figures are different categories.

### Gaps
- Annual 2023, 2024 and 2025 FARA figures for Israel and a full country ranking were not read directly from OpenSecrets. The fetch was blocked.
- 2025 annual totals by country were not found.

## 3. AIEF congressional trips: counts and costs, and comparison with other countries' sponsored travel

### Takeaway
Israel is the top destination for privately sponsored congressional foreign travel. AIEF sponsors most of those trips. Since 2012, more than a quarter of roughly 4,100 privately sponsored foreign House trips went to Israel, about 75% of them AIEF-sponsored. Israel trips outnumber trips to the entire Western Hemisphere and Africa combined. The UK is second, with under half of Israel's count in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- REPORTED. Howard Center (University of Maryland) and POLITICO built a database of about 17,000 House trips. Findings:
  - Israel was the No. 1 destination for privately sponsored foreign House travel, with more trips than the entire Western Hemisphere and Africa combined.
  - About 75% of Israel trips were AIEF-sponsored.
  - AIPAC-linked travel cost at least $10M for 2012–2023, per LegiStorm.
  - J Street was the second-largest Israel trip sponsor, but AIPAC's volume was nearly seven times larger.
  - After a pause following Oct 7, 2023, there were eight trips with 71 House members and staff from March to September 2024.
  — [POLITICO/Howard Center via Yahoo, Oct 30, 2024](https://www.yahoo.com/news/aipac-paid-hundreds-lawmakers-visit-090000842.html)
- REPORTED. In the first nine months of 2025, Israel accounted for 156 accepted House gift-travel invitations, against 117 in all of 2024. That is over a quarter of 2025 House international gift travel. The UK was second, with less than half of Israel's count. Israel was the destination for almost a fifth of privately sponsored Senate international travel over three years. AIEF provides about 60% of gift travel to Israel. Example: Rep. Hakeem Jeffries's 2023 AIEF trip cost about $20,000. — [Mondoweiss, Dec 9, 2025](https://mondoweiss.net/2025/12/inside-the-israel-lobbys-lesser-known-tool-for-influencing-congress-free-travel/). The AIEF share differs between sources: about 60% here against about 75% in POLITICO (different periods and methods).
- CONFIRMED (LegiStorm disclosure listing). An AIEF staff trip to Israel, May 24–June 1, 2025: 12 House staffers, all approved by Republican members. Costs were $17,691–$21,713 per person, $225,565 in total. — [LegiStorm trip group 16168](https://www.legistorm.com/trip/group/16168/sort/full_sponsor/type/asc.html)
- CONFIRMED (existence, from LegiStorm search titles). Members' trips to Israel are listed for Feb 13, 2025 (Reps. Luna, Mace, Ogles) and Aug 3, 2025 (Reps. McGuire, Hamadeh, Stutzman, Downing, Gill, all first-term in 2025). The sponsor and cost on each page were not read. — [LegiStorm 61102](https://www.legistorm.com/trip/61102.html); [61084](https://www.legistorm.com/trip/61084.html); [61160](https://www.legistorm.com/trip/61160.html); [61195](https://www.legistorm.com/trip/61195.html); [61087](https://www.legistorm.com/trip/61087.html); [59996](https://www.legistorm.com/trip/59996.html); [58971](https://www.legistorm.com/trip/58971.html); [59997](https://www.legistorm.com/trip/59997.html)

### Inferences
- Congressional travel is the strongest measure on which the "no other nation" framing is close to supportable. No other country receives a comparable volume of privately sponsored congressional trips, and no single sponsor tied to another country approaches AIEF's volume.
- August of election-off years appears to be the main freshman trip window. The Aug 3, 2025 trip date fits the pattern, but the full 2025 freshman headcount was not verified.

### Gaps
- Full head counts and total costs for the 2023 and 2025 AIEF freshman trips (House Clerk/Ethics filings) were not retrieved.
- Trip counts by sponsor for other countries' foundations (Taiwan, Turkey, Armenia, India and others) were not retrieved. Only the destination comparison (UK second) was found.

## 4. Conclusion: which version of the "no other nation" claim holds

### Takeaway
The broad claim does not hold. Many countries register lobbyists under FARA, and several outspend Israel there. Many diaspora groups also have nonprofits, PACs and congressional access. A narrower claim is supported by the evidence found: no other country-focused U.S. lobby operates a super PAC and donor network at AIPAC/UDP's scale. AIPAC plus UDP reported about $95M in 2024 and $120M in 2026, with spending in 333 of 473 races in 2026. No other country is the destination for anything close to Israel's volume of privately sponsored congressional travel. Two qualifiers apply. First, the comparison-group figures were not verified this pass. Second, AIPAC ended direct PAC giving and endorsements on Oct 21, 2026.

### Cited Findings
- AIPAC/UDP scale — [The Intercept, Oct 29, 2026](https://theintercept.com/2026/10/29/aipac-israel-midterms-spending-elections-congress/); policy change — [NPR via OPB, Oct 21, 2026](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/21/aipac-won-t-give-candidates-direct-contributions-anymore/)
- FARA ranking (Israel not first) — [Denver Gazette/Washington Examiner](https://www.denvergazette.com/2025/07/28/foreigners-spend-billions-to-influence-us-politics-heres-whos-cutting-the-biggest-checks-d8d5a134-bc03-513a-8fad-968feb201536/); [Everything-PR](https://everything-pr.com/foreign-influence-pr-study-2026); [Quincy](https://quincyinst.org/research/the-eighth-front-inside-israels-1-billion-influence-campaign/)
- Travel lead — [POLITICO/Howard Center](https://www.yahoo.com/news/aipac-paid-hundreds-lawmakers-visit-090000842.html); [Mondoweiss](https://mondoweiss.net/2025/12/inside-the-israel-lobbys-lesser-known-tool-for-influencing-congress-free-travel/)

### Inferences
- Defensible wording: "No other country's American supporters run an election operation or congressional travel program anywhere near this size." This holds if the FEC check of the comparison groups confirms the gap.
- Not defensible: claims that Israel is unique in having registered lobbyists, a diaspora lobby, or a "set-up" in general. Also not defensible: claims that AIPAC is a foreign agent. It is not FARA-registered and is funded by U.S. donors, though critics dispute whether it should register.
- Even-handedness: defenders argue AIPAC is a domestic citizen lobby like ANCA or CANF, only larger. Critics argue its scale and coordination with a foreign government's priorities make it different in kind. The evidence here supports "different in scale" and does not settle "different in kind."

### Gaps
- Academic framing (Mearsheimer & Walt and their critics; Paul & Anderson on ethnic lobbies) was not read this pass. The Rienner excerpt of Paul & Anderson, "Ethnic Lobbies and US Foreign Policy," was found but not fetched: https://www.rienner.com/uploads/48e3ce9d83653.pdf
- Verified FEC figures for every comparison group are the key outstanding check before the narrower claim can be stated as CONFIRMED.
