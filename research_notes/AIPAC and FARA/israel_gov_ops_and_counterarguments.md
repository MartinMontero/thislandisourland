# Israeli Government Influence Operations in the U.S., Links to AIPAC, and Counterarguments to the "AIPAC Should Register Under FARA" Claim (as of Nov 13, 2026)

Labels: CONFIRMED = primary record or the platform's/government's own statement; REPORTED = credible press reporting, not independently checked against a primary record; CONTESTED = sources conflict or a party denies; NOT VERIFIED = could not check. Quotes kept under 15 words.

## A1. 2023–24 Ministry of Diaspora Affairs / STOIC campaign

### Takeaway
An Israeli firm, STOIC, ran a fake-account campaign aimed at U.S. audiences and Democratic lawmakers during the Gaza war; Meta and OpenAI confirmed taking it down (May 2024). The ~$2M Diaspora Ministry funding is REPORTED by the NYT and denied by the ministry; neither platform attributed it to the Israeli government. No source connects it to AIPAC.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED (platform): OpenAI's May 30, 2024 report says it disrupted an operation by a commercial company in Israel, STOIC, nicknamed "Zero Zeno"; it generated articles and comments posted to Instagram, Facebook, X and websites. OpenAI says it "disrupted the activity, not the company" and does **not** attribute it to the Israeli government. Rated no higher than 2 on the Brookings Breakout Scale (no breakout into real communities). — [OpenAI, "Disrupting deceptive uses of AI by covert influence operations," May 30, 2024](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-deceptive-uses-of-AI-by-covert-influence-operations/)
- CONFIRMED (platform), via press: Meta said it removed Facebook and Instagram accounts linked to STOIC before they gained traction with real people (Meta's Q1 2024 Adversarial Threat Report, released late May 2024). — [NPR, July 9, 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/07/09/nx-s1-4994027/israel-us-online-influence-campaign); [NBC, Meta/OpenAI disrupted Israeli company's campaign](https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/national-international/meta-openai-say-disrupted-israeli-companys-influence-campaign/3556045/). Meta's own report PDF was not opened directly (see Gaps).
- REPORTED: The NYT (June 5, 2024) reported that Israel's Ministry of Diaspora Affairs paid STOIC about $2 million, with the aim of getting Democratic members of Congress to "maintain support for Israel." No individual lawmakers are named in NPR's account; Israeli watchdog FakeReporter separately found a network targeting Black Democratic members of Congress, not explicitly tied to STOIC. — [NPR, July 9, 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/07/09/nx-s1-4994027/israel-us-online-influence-campaign); [Times of Israel/ynet summary](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/r1puqhpv0)
- REPORTED: Target audience was mainly younger, progressive Americans; one site rated universities "safe" or "unsafe" for Jewish students. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2024/07/09/nx-s1-4994027/israel-us-online-influence-campaign)
- CONTESTED: Diaspora Minister Amichai Chikli denied the reports and accused FakeReporter of "slander against IDF soldiers and the State of Israel." Former ambassador Michael Oren called for an investigation, calling it "inappropriate interference" in an ally's politics. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2024/07/09/nx-s1-4994027/israel-us-online-influence-campaign); [Jewish Chronicle](https://www.thejc.com/news/israel/israeli-minister-denies-reports-the-government-ran-covert-influence-campaign-on-us-politicians-uy5gj4fq)
- REPORTED (related, broader ministry program): Lee Fang and Jack Poulson (with The Guardian), June 24, 2024, reported the Diaspora Ministry spent about NIS 32M (~$8.6M) on advocacy Oct 2023–May 2024; Voices of Israel (formerly Concert, chartered via the Ministry of Strategic Affairs) funded U.S. groups including Christians United for Israel, Proclaiming Justice to the Nations and the Israel Allies Foundation; none had registered under FARA. AIPAC is not named. — [Lee Fang, "Israeli Documents Show Expansive Covert U.S. Influence Campaign," June 24, 2024](https://www.leefang.com/p/israeli-documents-show-expansive)

### Inferences
- The operation is confirmed; Israeli-government direction rests on NYT sourcing plus the ministry's denial. "Israeli government-funded (reported, denied)" is the accurate wording, not "confirmed."
- It had little measurable reach according to both platforms.

### Gaps
- Meta Q1 2024 Adversarial Threat Report PDF not opened directly; the exact Meta wording on STOIC and account counts not verified from the primary document.
- NYT original (June 5, 2024) not fetched directly; ~$2M figure taken from NPR's account of it.

## A2. FARA registrations 2024–2026 with Israel as foreign principal

### Takeaway
Since 2025, Israel's Foreign Ministry has used Havas Media (Germany) as an intermediary to hire several U.S. firms that registered under FARA. Clock Tower X (#7649, Parscale) is the largest. Its reported totals rose from a $6M contract (Sept 2025) to $9M (Dec 2025) to $15M received by May 2026. These are disclosed, legal FARA registrations, not covert operations. None of the sources links them to AIPAC.

### Cited Findings
**Clock Tower X LLC (FARA #7649; Brad Parscale)**
- CONFIRMED (eFile index): The FARA eFile index for #7649 lists registrant Clock Tower X LLC, foreign principal "State of Israel via Havas Media Germany GmbH," and 311 documents, mostly "Informational Materials" plus supplemental statements. — [efile.fara.gov RegDocs 7649](https://efile.fara.gov/api/v1/RegDocs/html/7649); example exhibit: [7649-Exhibit-AB-20260607](https://efile.fara.gov/docs/7649-Exhibit-AB-20260607-4.pdf). Caution: the fetched index showed document dates of 10/23–11/26/2026, and 11/26/2026 is after today's date. This is probably a parsing or year error (the dates may be 2025), so the per-document dates are NOT VERIFIED.
- REPORTED: Registered Sept 18, 2025. Contract was $6M from Havas ($1.5M/month for four months) through Dec 31, 2025, plus matching funds from Parscale's firm. Scope: a campaign to "combat antisemitism," with 100+ root creative assets/month, 80% aimed at Gen Z platforms (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube), 50M paid impressions/month, and integration into Salem Media Group properties. — [Sludge, Oct 6, 2025](https://readsludge.com/2025/10/06/israels-new-u-s-influence-campaigns-target-tiktok-churches-and-chatgpt/)
- REPORTED: The contract was expanded from $6M to $9M in a December (2025) update. Starting late February 2026, a network of sites published "AI-optimized content" on the U.S.–Israel campaign against Iran. Sludge says the principal chain is Israel's Foreign Ministry via Havas. — [Sludge, Apr 3, 2026](https://readsludge.com/2026/04/03/how-trumps-former-digital-guru-is-shaping-ai-narratives-about-iran-for-israel/)
- REPORTED (from filings): The original registration named the State of Israel directly. A later supplemental filing called this "in error" (no receipts under it). An Oct 2025 amendment says the principal was "incorrectly named"; the corrected principal is the State of Israel via Havas Media Germany GmbH. A May 2026 supplemental reports $15M received from Havas Media Network and ~$13M disbursed over six months, including >$6M to SparkFire Technologies for "Direct Engagement." Sites distributed by Clock Tower X: Paxpoint, Compassion Pulse, FactSignal, Cultravia, Justorium, Allyvia. Paid via Havas Media Germany for the Government of Israel, with a $199,985 advance for "campaign expenses." Up to $900,000 total. 14–18 influencers were to post 25–30 times/month, July–Nov 2025. ## B2. Legal experts on whether FARA applies

### Takeaway
The main legal counterargument is that FARA turns on a foreign principal's "order, request, … direction or control." Shared interests are not enough, and no public evidence shows Israel directs AIPAC. The historical counterpoint is that DOJ did order AIPAC's predecessor, the AZC, to register in 1962, because of foreign funding.

### Cited Findings
- FARA lawyer Matthew Sanderson (Caplin & Drysdale): AIPAC does not have to register. The foreign government must be "directing and controlling the U.S. organization," and shared interests are not enough. He calls the contrary reading a "bad faith interpretation." — [Forward](https://forward.com/news/730423/tucker-carlson-ted-cruz-aipac-foreign-agent/)
- FARA dates from 1938. It covers persons acting "at the order, request, or under the direction or control" of a foreign principal. AIPAC-critical petitions to force registration have periodically failed. — [Forward](https://forward.com/news/730423/tucker-carlson-ted-cruz-aipac-foreign-agent/)
- Sen. Ted Cruz rejected Tucker Carlson's claim that AIPAC lobbies for a foreign government: "That is not only not true — that is false." — [Forward](https://forward.com/news/730423/tucker-carlson-ted-cruz-aipac-foreign-agent/)
- Counterpoint (history): In Nov 1962, DOJ under Robert Kennedy ordered the American Zionist Council to register, alleging funding by the Jewish Agency for Israel; the demand was later withdrawn. AZC deposition: it received "virtually all of its operating funds" from the Jewish Agency. — [American Council for Judaism (undated; footer 2026)](https://acjna.org/articles/aipacs-new-political-role-raises-old-questions-about-foreign-agent-registration/)
- Counterpoint (exemptions): Public Citizen's Craig Holman says that when a group lobbies at a foreign government's behest, "nearly all the exemptions are overridden." This was said about Israeli-funded groups in general, not AIPAC. — [Lee Fang](https://www.leefang.com/p/israeli-documents-show-expansive)
- DOJ's own framing: FARA protects "the integrity of American democracy" through disclosure. — [Dallas Express via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/federal-filing-details-israeli-contract-125045333.html)

### Inferences
- The legal question is evidentiary: registration would require proof of Israeli direction or control, or of foreign funding as in the AZC 1962 case. None has been produced publicly for AIPAC today.

- No former DOJ FARA Unit official was found commenting specifically on AIPAC. Lawfare and Just Security pieces specific to AIPAC were not located in this pass.
- An academic article surfaced in searches (Modern Judaism 46(2), [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/mj/article/46/2/103/8707527)) appears relevant but was not read.

## B3. Antisemitism / dual-loyalty concerns vs. Jewish groups supporting scrutiny

### Takeaway
ADL classes "foreign agent" and "Israel-first" attacks on AIPAC as dual-loyalty antisemitism in specific 2026 cases, while noting that criticism of Israeli policy is not in itself the problem. Some Jewish groups argue the opposite. The American Council for Judaism explicitly backs FARA registration, and IfNotNow and JVP campaign against AIPAC; their FARA-specific positions were not verified.

### Cited Findings
- ADL (Aug 19, 2026; updated Nov 4, 2026) calls dual loyalty a "longstanding" antisemitic trope. It lists a candidate's reference to "AIPAC-paid Israeli foreign agents" among antisemitic statements, calls an "Israel-first" mailer "antisemitic dual loyalty rhetoric," and says Zohran Mamdani "invoked antisemitic themes" by calling AIPAC "monsters" and citing "dark money." — [ADL, "Antisemitic Themes Punctuate 2026 Election Cycle Nationwide"](https://www.adl.org/resources/article/antisemitic-themes-punctuate-2026-election-cycle-nationwide)
- ADL warned that a dual-citizenship bill risks reviving the "dual loyalty" narrative (Dec 2025). — [Jewish Insider](https://jewishinsider.com/2025/12/adl-bernie-moreno-dual-citizenship-bill-dual-loyalty-antisemitic-trope/)
- AJC published a July 2026 "Candidate Guide to Antisemitism in American Politics." — [AJC PDF](https://www.ajc.org/sites/default/files/pdf/2026-07/DPPA-Candidate-Guide-to-Antisemitism-in-American-Politics_%20GRFX-10409_v3.pdf). Its contents on FARA/AIPAC are NOT VERIFIED (not opened).
- Van Jones (NPR): criticism of AIPAC is fine, but calling it a foreign front is "just not true." He likened that framing to an old "dual-loyalty" stereotype. — [NPR, Aug 13, 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/13/nx-s1-5923565/aipac-israel-primary-elections)
- Other side: The American Council for Judaism argues AIPAC was created to take Israel-advocacy work "off the FARA register." It quotes M.J. Rosenberg: AIPAC should register "as what it is: a foreign agent." — [ACJ](https://acjna.org/articles/aipacs-new-political-role-raises-old-questions-about-foreign-agent-registration/); Rosenberg's 2018 op-ed: [Forward, "It's Time For AIPAC To Register As A Foreign Agent"](https://forward.com/opinion/395676/its-time-for-aipac-to-register-as-a-foreign-agent)
- 2018 rebuttal letter: "AIPAC members stand with Israel as Americans, not foreign agents." — [Forward letters](https://forward.com/opinion/letters/395768/aipac-members-stand-with-israel-as-americans-not-foreign-agents)
- IfNotNow pressured Democrats to reject AIPAC endorsements (an anti-AIPAC campaign, not a confirmed FARA demand). — [Forward](https://forward.com/news/556450/progressive-jewish-democrats-aipac-ifnotnow-israel/?amp=1)

## B4. "Other diaspora lobbies use the same legal structure"

### Takeaway
This argument is commonly made, but no source reviewed here makes it in sourced detail. Partial support: NPR's expert compares AIPAC's electoral record to other U.S. single-issue groups, and op-eds argue that American donors to an American group is not foreign influence.

### Cited Findings
- Bethany Mandel (Washington Examiner, Aug 9, 2026): "American donors giving money to an American organization is not foreign influence." The full text is paywalled. — [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/?p=4679655)
- Brown's Dany Bahar: AIPAC's win rate is "pretty average," comparable to the NRA and Planned Parenthood. OpenSecrets' Brendan Glavin says using affiliated super PACs with local-sounding names is common among national super PACs. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/13/nx-s1-5923565/aipac-israel-primary-elections)

### Inferences
- The structural parallel (U.S.-incorporated, U.S.-funded advocacy groups for a homeland cause, e.g., Armenian, Indian, Cuban or Greek-American groups, none FARA-registered) is plausible. It is not sourced here, so it should not be stated as fact without a citation.

### Gaps
- No sourced comparison of AIPAC's legal form (501(c)(4) + PAC + super PAC) with ANCA, Hindu American Foundation, Cuban American National Foundation, etc. was found within this research budget.
