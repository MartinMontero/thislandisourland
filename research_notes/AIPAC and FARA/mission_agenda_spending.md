# AIPAC: Stated Mission, Legislative Agenda, Election Role and Positions on U.S. Military Action (through Nov 13, 2026)

Claim being tested: AIPAC's "sole goal is to influence U.S. legislation, elections and military policy on behalf of a foreign nation (Israel)."

Labels: CONFIRMED = primary source or multiple independent reliable sources; REPORTED = single credible secondary source; CONTESTED = credible sources disagree, or AIPAC disputes it; NOT VERIFIED = seen only in a search-result title or snippet, or the primary record was not opened.

Method note: aipac.org/about could not be fetched in this session because a permission prompt went unanswered. AIPAC's self-description below comes from its FAQ memo and its "AIPAC money" page. The LD-2 filings at lda.senate.gov were not opened directly; the lobbying-disclosure detail comes from Legis1 summaries of those filings.

## 1. Mission, structure and funding

### Takeaway
AIPAC describes itself as a single-issue organization whose job is to persuade the U.S. government to adopt policies that strengthen U.S.-Israel ties. It says it is funded only by Americans and is not directed by Israel. It is a 501(c)(4), and its revenue roughly doubled in FY2024.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED (self-description). AIPAC's FAQ, dated Jan 1, 2026, says its aim is to "encourage and persuade the U.S. government to enact specific policies" that support the U.S.-Israel relationship. — [AIPAC FAQs](https://www.aipac.org/memos/aipac-faqs)
- CONFIRMED (self-description). The same page says AIPAC is "funded exclusively by Americans" and "not funded nor directed by the Israeli government." It claims "more than 6.5 million members" and says it supports pro-Israel candidates of both parties. — [AIPAC FAQs](https://www.aipac.org/memos/aipac-faqs)
- CONFIRMED (self-description). A second AIPAC page says its money "comes from American citizens" and that AIPAC is "not directed or funded by the Israeli government or any foreign source." It gives no donor count or percentage breakdown. — [AIPAC, "The Truth Behind AIPAC Money"](https://aipac.org/aipac-money)
- NOT VERIFIED independently. No public audit was found that confirms the "exclusively American" funding claim. Form 990s do not publicly list donors. — basis: [ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, EIN 53-0217164](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/530217164)
- CONFIRMED. AIPAC (EIN 53-0217164) is a 501(c)(4), and donations to it are not tax-deductible. Its NTEE category is "International, Foreign Affairs and National Security." — [ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/530217164)
- CONFIRMED. Form 990 figures by fiscal year (years end in September), from ProPublica:
  - FY2021: revenue $78.26M; expenses $63.52M; net assets $124.40M
  - FY2022: revenue $73.51M; expenses $79.10M; net assets $113.44M
  - FY2023: revenue $79.40M; expenses $83.08M; net assets $111.12M
  - FY2024: revenue $156.45M; expenses $100.36M; net assets $171.57M
  - FY2025: revenue $122.54M; expenses $118.29M; net assets $177.59M
  - CEO Howard Kohr's compensation was $1.49M in FY2025. The FY2025 filing lists him as CEO through Dec 31, 2024.
  - Source: [ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/530217164)
- CONFIRMED. In March 2022 AIPAC spokesman Marshall Wittmann called it "a single-issue organization." — [Jerusalem Post, Mar 8, 2022](https://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-700701)
- Context for FARA. AIPAC's public pages state that it is not directed by a foreign government. That claim is central to whether FARA registration would be required. Others argue it should register, for example [Forward on Tucker Carlson and Ted Cruz](https://forward.com/news/730423/tucker-carlson-ted-cruz-aipac-foreign-agent/), which was not opened. FARA analysis is outside this file's scope.

### Inferences
- On its own account, AIPAC is single-issue, and that issue is U.S. policy toward Israel. That matches part of the claim. The claim's phrase "on behalf of a foreign nation" implies agency or direction from Israel. AIPAC denies this, and nothing reviewed here establishes it. That part of the claim is CONTESTED.
- The jump in FY2024 revenue (+97%) lines up with the Oct 2023 war and the 2024 election push. That timing is an inference; the 990 itself does not explain the increase.

### Gaps
- The 990 Part III mission text was not extracted.
- The verbatim aipac.org/about statement was not retrieved (the fetch needed approval).
- 990s for the separately incorporated AIPAC PAC, UDP and the American Israel Education Foundation were not reviewed.

## 2. Legislative agenda and lobbying disclosures

### Takeaway
AIPAC's documented legislative work centers on security aid to Israel, Iran sanctions, appropriations and defense bills, and anti-boycott measures. Lobbying spending runs about $0.9–1.0M per quarter. One secondary summary lists "Trade Policy" and "Homeland Security" among its issue areas, but the bills behind those categories were not identified.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED. On Apr 23, 2024, AIPAC hailed Congress's $14.3B emergency supplemental for Israel as "the largest U.S. security aid package in Israel's history." — [AIPAC press release, Apr 23, 2024](https://aipac.org/press-release/america-stands-with-israel-congress-approves-historic-aid-package)
- REPORTED. Q3 2025 lobbying spending was $940,000. Cumulative spending is about $57.4M across 78 disclosures since Aug 2003. Issue areas by share: Foreign Relations 26%, Budget/Appropriations 22%, Defense 19%, Energy/Nuclear 10%, Trade 9%, Homeland Security 7%. Source: Legis1 summary, not the LD-2 itself. — [Legis1, Oct 20, 2025](https://legis1.com/news/aipac-lobbies-congress-middle-east-policy)
- REPORTED. AIPAC's amended Q4 2025 LD-2 reports $973,910 spent and 10 in-house lobbyists. Bills listed:
  - Enhanced Iran Sanctions Act of 2025 (H.R.1422/S.556)
  - H.R.4779, the FY2026 National Security, Department of State, and Related Programs Appropriations Act
  - defense authorization (no bill number given)
  - Its Q2 2025 filing listed H.R.4795, the Protect Economic and Academic Freedom Act.
  - Source: [Legis1, Sept 29, 2026](https://legis1.com/news/aipac-lobbying-iran-sanctions-lobbies-defense)
- REPORTED. Read Sludge described the IGO Anti-Boycott Act (H.R. 867) as part of AIPAC's "campaign to expand anti-BDS laws." The bill was pulled from the House calendar in May 2025. Read Sludge cites no LD-2 entry for it. — [Read Sludge, May 5, 2025](https://readsludge.com/2025/05/05/aipacs-anti-bds-bill-is-derailed-at-least-for-now/)
- NOT VERIFIED. AIPAC lobbying on the Antisemitism Awareness Act. The one source opened attributes a Feb 2024 lobbying day for that bill to the Jewish Federations of North America, not AIPAC. — [JNS, Feb 4, 2024](https://www.jns.org/u.s.-news/more-than-400-activists-lobby-for-antisemitism-awareness-act-aid-to-israel)
- NOT VERIFIED. AIPAC's statement on the 2016 $38B MOU. Only a headline was seen: "U.S., Israel Sign Historic Security Assistance Deal." The MOU's terms appear in [JTA, Sept 14, 2016](https://www.jta.org/2016/09/14/politics/obamas-38b-aid-package-to-israel-comes-with-caveats-its-generous-but-on-his-terms) and [Obama White House blog, Sept 14, 2016](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/blog/2016/09/14/us-making-historic-investment-protect-security-israel), neither of which was opened. — [Jewish Insider, Sept 2016](https://jewishinsider.com/2016/09/u-s-israel-sign-historic-security-assistance-deal/)

### Inferences
- Every bill identified in AIPAC's disclosures and press output concerns Israel, Iran or Middle East security. The "Trade" (9%) and "Homeland Security" (7%) categories may also be Israel-related, such as anti-boycott trade law or security grants for nonprofits, but that is unconfirmed.
- The April 2024 supplemental AIPAC praised also carried Ukraine and Indo-Pacific funding. AIPAC's public statement addresses only the Israel portion.

### Gaps
- The primary LD-2 PDFs at lda.senate.gov / lda.gov were not opened. Exact issue codes and full bill lists for 2023–2026 are unverified.
- AIPAC's 2023 supplemental lobbying (the Oct 2023 $14.3B House bill) was not separately sourced.

## 3. Positions on U.S. military action

### Takeaway
AIPAC publicly praised the Feb 28, 2026 U.S. strikes on Iran. Its role in the 2003 Iraq war is CONTESTED: AIPAC has said it took no position, while contemporaneous reporting, including a reported 2003 remark by its own director, says it quietly lobbied for the use-of-force authorization.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED. On Feb 28, 2026, AIPAC praised U.S. strikes on Iran that were coordinated with Israel. It called Iran a "terror-supporting regime" and commended the effort to ensure Iran "will never acquire a nuclear weapon." — [AIPAC press release, Feb 28, 2026](https://aipac.org/press-release/american-strikes-on-iran)
- REPORTED. In March 2026, Axios Chicago examined how AIPAC funding related to local lawmakers' support for the Iran war (headline only seen). — [Axios, Mar 2, 2026](https://www.axios.com/local/chicago/2026/03/02/how-aipac-funding-affects-local-iran-war-support)
- NOT VERIFIED. In June 2025, AIPAC reportedly called for U.S. aid to Israel during the Israel-Iran war. AIPAC also has pages on the June 2025 ceasefire and an "Israel-Iran war" memo. Only titles were seen. — [Daily Signal, June 18, 2025](https://www.dailysignal.com/2025/06/18/aipac-calls-trump-congress-aid-israel/); [AIPAC ceasefire release](https://aipac.org/press-release/iran-ceasefire-announcement); [AIPAC memo](https://aipac.org/memos/israel-iran-war)
- REPORTED. NPR reported that criticism of AIPAC during the 2026 cycle focused on its positions on the wars in Gaza and Iran. — [NPR via OPB, Oct 21, 2026](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/21/aipac-won-t-give-candidates-direct-contributions-anymore/)
- CONTESTED (Iraq 2003):
  - In 2015 AIPAC spokesman Wittmann told the NYT that AIPAC "took no position whatsoever on the Iraq war."
  - Against that, LobeLog cites:
    - Howard Kohr, in the New York Sun (Jan 2003), saying AIPAC "quietly" lobbied Congress for the use-of-force resolution
    - Washington Post (Dana Milbank, Apr 2003), reporting AIPAC's Steve Rosen saying "God willing, we're going to have a great victory in Iraq"
    - The New Yorker (Jeffrey Goldberg, 2005), reporting that AIPAC lobbied for the war but that Iraq was not a chief priority
    - The Forward (2015), reporting Barney Frank's account
  - Source: [LobeLog, Aug 6, 2015](https://lobelog.com/aipac-bristles-at-obamas-reminder-of-iraq-war-lobbying/)
  - Counter-view (headline only): [Truthout, "Don't blame the Iraq debacle on the Israel lobby"](https://truthout.org/articles/dont-blame-the-iraq-debacle-on-the-israel-lobby/). [Responsible Statecraft (Mar 20, 2023)](https://responsiblestatecraft.org/2023/03/20/aipac-fdd-websites-erase-all-evidence-of-their-iraq-war-cheerleading) reports that AIPAC removed Iraq-era material from its website; this was not opened.

### Inferences
- AIPAC's support for military action against Iran is documented in its own words. On Iraq, the evidence suggests informal lobbying for the authorization rather than a formal campaign. That is consistent with both Goldberg's "not a chief concern" and AIPAC's "no position" claim, if "position" means formal policy.

### Gaps
- No AIPAC statement was found on a 2026 War Powers resolution vote. Primary texts for the Kohr 2003 NY Sun quote and the Milbank WaPo piece were not opened; they are cited via LobeLog.

## 4. Election role: PACs, super PAC, conduits, totals, and the Oct 21, 2026 change

### Takeaway
AIPAC began direct electoral work in Dec 2021–Jan 2022. AIPAC PAC (C00797670) and UDP (C00799031) spent over $100M in both 2024 and 2026. UDP increasingly routes money through pop-up PACs with neutral names. On Oct 21, 2026, AIPAC said it would end endorsements, AIPAC PAC contributions and earmarking. UDP continues to operate, and AIPAC said it would push members' direct giving.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED. AIPAC PAC was created Dec 15, 2021, and UDP on Jan 3, 2022. The PAC's limit is $5,000 per candidate per election. UDP makes unlimited independent expenditures. — [FactCheck.org, Aug 2026](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/08/united-democracy-project-3/)
- CONFIRMED. FactCheck.org's UDP figures by cycle:
  - 2022: raised over $35.9M, spent over $32.9M, $26.1M in independent expenditures — [FactCheck.org, Sept 26, 2024](https://www.factcheck.org/2024/09/united-democracy-project-2/)
  - 2024 (full cycle): raised about $87.1M, spent about $61.4M, $34.8M in independent expenditures against or for Democratic House candidates — [FactCheck.org, Aug 2026](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/08/united-democracy-project-3/)
  - 2026 through June 30: raised about $104M, spent about $52.5M, more than $27.9M in independent expenditures — [FactCheck.org, Aug 2026](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/08/united-democracy-project-3/)
- REPORTED. Combined AIPAC PAC and UDP spending was $104.3M in the 2026 cycle by early Aug 2026, not counting allied groups such as DMFI. UDP had $80M cash on hand at end of June. — [Read Sludge, Aug 3, 2026](https://readsludge.com/2026/08/03/aipac-tops-100-million-in-election-spending-for-second-straight-cycle/)
- REPORTED. AIPAC spent over $100M in 2024 and claimed credit for 361 endorsed candidates. — [The Intercept, Dec 30, 2025](https://theintercept.com/2025/12/30/aipac-campaigns-elections-israel-congress/); also [Read Sludge, Aug 27, 2024](https://readsludge.com/2024/08/27/aipac-officially-surpasses-100-million-in-spending-on-2024-elections/) (headline)
- CONFIRMED. Through Sept 30, 2026 (per FEC filings reported by NPR), AIPAC gave nearly $25M to candidates and committees. UDP gave $13M and spent $53.5M on independent expenditures. — [NPR via OPB, Oct 21, 2026](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/21/aipac-won-t-give-candidates-direct-contributions-anymore/)
- CONFIRMED (AIPAC claim). AIPAC spokesperson Deryn Sousa said "AIPAC members gave more than $90 million directly to candidates this cycle." That is the conduit/earmark and member-giving channel. — [NPR via OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/21/aipac-won-t-give-candidates-direct-contributions-anymore/)
- CONFIRMED. The Oct 21, 2026 announcement, in a letter to members:
  - AIPAC will stop endorsements, direct PAC contributions and earmarking.
  - It will shift to "direct, organized contributions from individual Americans."
  - It warned that "the challenges we are confronting will continue to grow."
  - Sousa: "Our goal is to build support for the U.S.-Israel relationship."
  - UDP continues.
  - Source: [NPR via OPB, Oct 21, 2026](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/21/aipac-won-t-give-candidates-direct-contributions-anymore/); also [J. Weekly, Oct 22, 2026](https://jweekly.com/2026/10/22/aipac-to-halt-direct-campaign-contributions-and-candidate-endorsements/) (not opened)
- REPORTED. By Dec 2025, AIPAC had made no new public endorsements for 2026. The Intercept reported that it was working behind the scenes through members, DMFI, 314 Action and small PACs. Critics described the brand as an "electoral liability." — [The Intercept, Dec 30, 2025](https://theintercept.com/2025/12/30/aipac-campaigns-elections-israel-congress/)
- REPORTED. Pop-up and pass-through PACs:
  - Politico (via FactCheck) reported that UDP "shielded more than 40 percent" of its 2026 primary spending through pop-up PACs. — [FactCheck.org](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/08/united-democracy-project-3/)
  - The Intercept's money-flow chart (Oct 2026) shows UDP transfers to:
    - Elect Chicago Women: $4.01M. It spent about $9.8M backing Laura Fine (IL-9) and Melissa Bean (IL-8) and opposing Daniel Biss.
    - EDW Action Fund: $2.35M. It passed $5.57M to Pro-Choice Majority Action.
    - Affordable Chicago Now!: $1.32M, spent backing Donna Miller (IL-2).
    - Bold America: $2.8M. Races included NY-13, CA-14 and AZ-1.
    - Palmetto Action: $1M, spent in the SC Senate GOP primary.
    - Save Democracy: $1.65M.
    - Elect Chicago Women also funded Chicago Progressive Partnership ($1M), which opposed Kat Abughazaleh.
  - Source: [The Intercept chart, Oct 2026](https://theintercept.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/UDP_PER_MONEY_FLOW_FINAL.html); Elect Chicago Women's FEC record: [FEC C00936724](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00936724/)
- CONFIRMED / REPORTED. Notable races:
  - 2024: about $9.9M against Jamaal Bowman and $5.2M+ against Cori Bush; both lost their primaries — [FactCheck.org 2024](https://www.factcheck.org/2024/09/united-democracy-project-2/)
  - 2026, Thomas Massie (R, KY-4): over $3M against Massie, who lost the May 19 primary. Politico called it the most expensive House primary ever, at over $9M from pro-Israel groups. UDP's Patrick Dorton called Massie "the most anti-Israel Republican in the House." — [FactCheck.org 2026](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/08/united-democracy-project-3/)
  - 2026, Tom Malinowski (NJ-11): over $2M against him; he lost to Analilia Mejia — [FactCheck.org 2026](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/08/united-democracy-project-3/)
  - 2026, IL-7: about $5M backing Melissa Conyears-Ervin, who lost — [FactCheck.org 2026](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/08/united-democracy-project-3/)
  - 2026, Michigan Senate primary: UDP spent $30.6M backing Haley Stevens and opposing Abdul El-Sayed, AIPAC's largest single-race spend — [Read Sludge, Aug 3, 2026](https://readsludge.com/2026/08/03/aipac-tops-100-million-in-election-spending-for-second-straight-cycle/)
  - UDP continued spending against El-Sayed in the general election — [NPR via OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/21/aipac-won-t-give-candidates-direct-contributions-anymore/)
- CONFIRMED. Top UDP donors in 2026 include Paul Singer, Marc Rowan and Haim Saban. — [FactCheck.org 2026](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/08/united-democracy-project-3/)

### Inferences
- The Oct 21 change ends the endorsement and PAC channels, which carry AIPAC's brand. It leaves in place UDP, which raises unlimited sums, and AIPAC's organization of member giving, which AIPAC says came to $90M+ this cycle. The change looks like a shift in channel rather than an exit from elections. That is an inference, but it rests on AIPAC's own statement that the "mission and strategy remain the same."
- The most-cited spending totals ($100M+) mix PAC contributions, UDP independent expenditures and sometimes transfers. Totals from different sources are not directly comparable.

### Gaps
- FEC pages for AIPAC PAC (C00797670) and UDP (C00799031) were not opened directly. The figures come from FactCheck.org, NPR and Read Sludge reporting of FEC data.
- No independent figure was found for 2022 earmarked conduit totals, and none was found for the "$90M" member-giving figure.
- 2026 general-election outcomes (Nov 3) for UDP-targeted races, including El-Sayed, were not verified.

## 5. Does AIPAC work on issues unrelated to Israel?

### Takeaway
No evidence was found that AIPAC lobbies for or endorses candidates on any issue unrelated to Israel, Iran or Middle East security. AIPAC says it is single-issue. Critics point to its endorsement of election objectors in 2022 as a result of that single-issue approach; AIPAC defended those endorsements on the same single-issue grounds.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED. In 2022 AIPAC endorsed about 35+ Republicans who objected to certifying the 2020 election. Wittmann defended the choice: AIPAC is "a single-issue organization." — [Jerusalem Post, Mar 8, 2022](https://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-700701)
- CONFIRMED. AIPAC's FAQ describes its work only in terms of U.S.-Israel policy. — [AIPAC FAQs](https://www.aipac.org/memos/aipac-faqs)
- REPORTED. In the 2026 cycle, UDP-funded pop-up PACs ran races under non-Israel branding, such as "Pro-Choice Majority Action" and "Affordable Chicago Now!". The issue framing of their ads was not documented in the sources opened. — [The Intercept chart](https://theintercept.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/UDP_PER_MONEY_FLOW_FINAL.html)
- REPORTED. Lobbying issue categories include Trade (9%) and Homeland Security (7%), with no detail on content. — [Legis1, Oct 20, 2025](https://legis1.com/news/aipac-lobbies-congress-middle-east-policy)

### Inferences
- On the claim's "sole goal" wording: the record supports that AIPAC's agenda is exclusively Israel-focused U.S. policy across lobbying, elections and positions on military action. AIPAC agrees with that description of itself.
- The disputed part is "on behalf of a foreign nation." AIPAC says it is American-funded, independent, and acting on Americans' views about U.S. interests. The claim implies Israel directs it. Nothing reviewed proves direction or funding from Israel, and AIPAC is not registered under FARA. Characterization: accurate on single-issue scope, CONTESTED on agency.
- Using non-Israel branding in pop-up PACs is a messaging choice. It is not evidence that AIPAC has a non-Israel policy agenda.

### Gaps
- Bill-level detail for the "Trade" and "Homeland Security" LD-2 entries (likely anti-boycott and nonprofit security, unconfirmed).
- No content analysis of ads by AIPAC-funded PACs was reviewed. Prior reporting says they rarely mention Israel, but that was not verified in this pass.
