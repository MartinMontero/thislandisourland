## 1. Statutory text: 22 U.S.C. 611(b), 611(c), 613(h), and how they work for a domestic organization

### Takeaway
FARA turns on an agency relationship. A person must act "at the order, request, or under the direction or control" of a foreign principal, or of someone financed or supervised by one, and must engage in covered activity such as political activity. A U.S.-organized entity with its principal place of business in the U.S. is excluded from the "foreign principal" definition. Shared interests with a foreign government are not, on the statute's face, a trigger. The LDA exemption in § 613(h) covers agents of foreign private persons and entities only. DOJ says it does not apply where a foreign government is the principal beneficiary.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED (DOJ text of the Act): § 611(b)(1) defines a foreign principal as "a government of a foreign country and a foreign political party." — [DOJ, FARA Index and Act](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/fara-index-and-act)
- CONFIRMED: § 611(b)(2) covers "a person outside of the United States," except a U.S. citizen domiciled in the U.S., or an entity organized under U.S. or state law with its principal place of business in the U.S. — [DOJ, FARA Index and Act](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/fara-index-and-act)
- CONFIRMED: § 611(b)(3) covers an organization "organized under the laws of or having its principal place of business in a foreign country." — [DOJ, FARA Index and Act](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/fara-index-and-act)
- CONFIRMED: § 611(c)(1) defines an agent as anyone acting "at the order, request, or under the direction or control, of a foreign principal". — [DOJ, FARA Index and Act](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/fara-index-and-act)
- CONFIRMED: The same clause also reaches a person acting for someone whose activities are "directly or indirectly supervised, directed, controlled, financed, or subsidized in whole or in major part by a foreign principal." That intermediary route matters for any argument that relies on indirect control. — [DOJ, FARA Index and Act](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/fara-index-and-act)
- CONFIRMED: § 611(c)(1)(i) requires that the agent "engages within the United States in political activities for or in the interests of such foreign principal." Sub-clauses (ii) to (iv) cover public relations, fundraising and disbursement, and representation before U.S. officials. Only (i) was quoted in the fetched text; the others are summarized here from the general structure. — [DOJ, FARA Index and Act](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/fara-index-and-act)
- CONFIRMED: § 611(c)(2) also covers anyone who "holds himself out to be" an agent of a foreign principal, "whether or not pursuant to contractual relationship." — [DOJ, FARA Index and Act](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/fara-index-and-act)
- CONFIRMED: § 613(h) exempts an agent of a person described in § 611(b)(2) or an entity described in § 611(b)(3) who has engaged in lobbying and "has registered under the Lobbying Disclosure Act of 1995." By its text it does not cover agents of foreign governments or foreign political parties under § 611(b)(1). — [DOJ, FARA Index and Act](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/fara-index-and-act)
- CONFIRMED (DOJ guidance): DOJ's FAQ says "The exemption does not apply where a foreign government or a foreign political party is the principal beneficiary." — [DOJ FARA FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/frequently-asked-questions)
- CONFIRMED (DOJ guidance): The FAQ does not define "request" or "agency relationship." It frames coverage as acting at a foreign principal's order, request, or direction or control while engaging in a listed activity. — [DOJ FARA FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/frequently-asked-questions)
- REPORTED: The Congressional Research Service maintains a FARA background report (R46435), updated September 15, 2026. It was not read in full this pass. — [CRS R46435 via EveryCRSReport](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R46435.html)

### Inferences
- On the statute's face, an organization like AIPAC (U.S.-incorporated and U.S.-funded) is not itself a "foreign principal" under § 611(b)(2)–(3). Any FARA obligation would have to come from showing that it acts at the "order, request, or under the direction or control" of the Israeli government (§ 611(b)(1)), either directly or through a foreign-financed or foreign-supervised intermediary.
- Because the § 613(h) LDA exemption excludes foreign-government principals, AIPAC's LDA registration would not shield it if a foreign-government agency relationship were established. The exemption matters mainly for agents of foreign private entities.
- Whether "request" can be met by informal coordination, short of control, is the open interpretive question. This pass did not find DOJ guidance that resolves it.

### Gaps
- The codified text was not checked on uscode.house.gov. Full text of § 611(c)(1)(ii)–(iv) and § 611(o) ("political activities") was not quoted.
- The DOJ regulation behind the "principal beneficiary" language (28 C.F.R. 5.307) was not fetched directly.

## 2. DOJ practice: advisory opinions, guidance, historical precedent, and the Bondi memo

### Takeaway
DOJ's published guidance does not address AIPAC by name. Advisory opinions answer only real, prospective situations submitted by an actual party, so a third party cannot obtain one about AIPAC. The one widely cited precedent, a reported 1962 DOJ order directing the American Zionist Council (AZC) to register, comes mostly through advocacy sources in this pass. The February 5, 2025 Bondi memo limits criminal FARA charges to espionage-like conduct and keeps civil enforcement.

### Cited Findings
- CONFIRMED (DOJ guidance): Advisory-opinion requests must concern "an actual event, not a hypothetical situation". — [DOJ FARA FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/frequently-asked-questions)
- CONFIRMED: Requests "may only be submitted by an actual party to the proposed activities". Published opinions are anonymized. — [DOJ FARA FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/frequently-asked-questions)
- REPORTED (law-firm summary of the memo): On February 5, 2025, Attorney General Bondi directed that criminal FARA and 18 U.S.C. 951 charges be limited to conduct "similar to more traditional espionage by foreign government actors." — [Skadden, Feb. 2025](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/02/attorney-general-announces-shift-in-foreign-agents)
- REPORTED: The memo directs the section that houses the FARA Unit to focus on "civil enforcement, regulatory initiatives and public guidance." — [Skadden, Feb. 2025](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/02/attorney-general-announces-shift-in-foreign-agents)
- REPORTED: Former FARA Unit chief Brandon Van Grack and co-authors (Lawfare, January 28, 2026) say the memo kept civil enforcement in place. They also report that DOJ proposed revised FARA regulations in December 2024 and withdrew proposed rules in September 2025. — [Lawfare, "The State of FARA"](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-state-of-fara)
- REPORTED: The same Lawfare piece says a 2024 D.C. Circuit decision held that DOJ cannot compel retroactive FARA registration, at least in D.C. — [Lawfare, "The State of FARA"](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-state-of-fara)
- REPORTED (advocacy source, NOT VERIFIED against primary records): IRmep's account is that DOJ ordered the AZC to register in 1962. It says AZC's lobbying division incorporated as AIPAC about six weeks later without registering, and that DOJ in 1965 agreed to keep the AZC order secret. — [IRmep press release via PR Newswire, May 31, 2011](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/americans-demand-justice-department-regulate-aipac-as-israeli-foreign-agent-video-documents-122867839.html)
- REPORTED: On May 18, 2011, Heather Hunt, then chief of DOJ's FARA Registration Unit, answered the petitioners by asking for "additional information." There is no record in this source of DOJ action after that. — [IRmep press release via PR Newswire, May 31, 2011](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/americans-demand-justice-department-regulate-aipac-as-israeli-foreign-agent-video-documents-122867839.html)
- REPORTED: The Forward says critics have petitioned the government for years to make AIPAC register, without success. — [Forward, June 2025](https://forward.com/news/730423/tucker-carlson-ted-cruz-aipac-foreign-agent/)

### Inferences
- Under the Bondi memo, a criminal FARA case against a domestic lobby for ordinary advocacy appears outside stated DOJ priorities. Civil action (an injunction to compel registration) remains available in principle.
- The AZC episode is the main historical hook for advocates. Its details as presented here come from an advocacy organization (IRmep) that drew on documents it obtained. The site should cite primary archival documents, such as Senate Foreign Relations Committee 1963 hearing records or DOJ correspondence, before stating the details as fact.

## 3. Legal scholarship and expert commentary

### Takeaway
The most-cited practitioner view in mainstream outlets is that AIPAC does not have to register, because it receives no Israeli government funding, has no Israeli officials on its board, and shared interests do not create an agency relationship. Arguments that it should register rest mainly on the AZC history and on policy alignment and coordination. In this pass those arguments were found in advocacy, student, and non-peer-reviewed writing, not in a major law review or Lawfare/Just Security analysis specific to AIPAC. The question is CONTESTED in public debate. Practitioner commentary leans toward non-coverage under current law.

### Cited Findings
- REPORTED: Matthew Sanderson (Caplin & Drysdale, FARA practitioner, not AIPAC counsel) told the Forward that the law is clear that AIPAC does not need to register. — [Forward, June 2025](https://forward.com/news/730423/tucker-carlson-ted-cruz-aipac-foreign-agent/)
- REPORTED: Per the Forward, Sanderson says regulators look at foreign funding, contracts with foreign governments, and foreign control of the board. He says shared interests alone do not trigger registration. — [Forward, June 2025](https://forward.com/news/730423/tucker-carlson-ted-cruz-aipac-foreign-agent/)
- REPORTED: The Forward notes that other diaspora-linked lobbying groups also are not registered. It ran opposing op-eds on the question in 2018. — [Forward, June 2025](https://forward.com/news/730423/tucker-carlson-ted-cruz-aipac-foreign-agent/)
- REPORTED: Lawfare's January 2026 overview of FARA by a former FARA Unit chief does not mention AIPAC or diaspora advocacy groups. — [Lawfare, "The State of FARA"](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-state-of-fara)
- NOT VERIFIED (not read): A non-peer-reviewed paper on academia.edu argues that the AZC-to-AIPAC transition has FARA compliance implications. — [academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/145727596/From_Foreign_Agent_to_Domestic_Lobby_A_Historical_Analysis_of_the_American_Zionist_Councils_Transformation_to_AIPAC_and_the_Implications_for_Foreign_Agent_Registration_Act_Compliance)

## 4. Members of Congress, candidates, and bills (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
The clearest legislative step is Rep. Thomas Massie's "Americans Insist on Political Agent Clarity Act" (AIPAC Act), introduced May 2026 and reported as H.R. 8809. It would amend FARA's definitions so U.S.-organized groups whose lobbying principally advances a foreign state could be covered. Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene publicly said in August 2025 that AIPAC must register. Sen. Ted Cruz publicly rejected the premise in June 2025.

### Cited Findings
- REPORTED: Massie introduced the AIPAC Act, which would amend FARA to clarify "foreign principal." Wikipedia gives the introduction date as May 14, 2026, and says the bill was referred to House Judiciary. — [Wikipedia, AIPAC Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIPAC_Act); [Military.com, May 18, 2026](https://www.military.com/massie-bill-targets-aipac-with-new-fara-standards-israel)
- REPORTED: The bill number is H.R. 8809 (119th Congress), per a congress.gov link in Common Dreams. Not checked on congress.gov. — [Common Dreams, May 15, 2026](https://www.commondreams.org/news/thomas-massie-aipac-foreign-agents)
- REPORTED: Military.com says the bill would let U.S.-organized entities qualify as foreign principals even without direct foreign funding or instructions. It would look at whether lobbying "principally" advances a foreign state's interests. — [Military.com, May 18, 2026](https://www.military.com/massie-bill-targets-aipac-with-new-fara-standards-israel)
- REPORTED: Per Military.com, the bill lists factors DOJ could weigh: advocacy that tracks a foreign government's diplomatic goals, coordination with foreign officials, strategic guidance from abroad, and a name referencing a foreign nation. It also creates a citizen complaint process and cites *Meese v. Keene*. — [Military.com, May 18, 2026](https://www.military.com/massie-bill-targets-aipac-with-new-fara-standards-israel)
- REPORTED: Massie's press release: "Americans have a right to know when powerful lobbying organizations are advancing" foreign governments' interests. — [Military.com, May 18, 2026](https://www.military.com/massie-bill-targets-aipac-with-new-fara-standards-israel)

- REPORTED: Massie lost his May 2026 KY-04 primary to Ed Gallrein. - REPORTED: Greene made the statement after AIPAC criticized her remarks about Gaza. — [Just the News, Aug. 8, 2025](https://justthenews.com/government/congress/mtg-says-aipac-should-register-foreign-lobbyist-after-pushback-gaza-genocide)
- REPORTED (counter-view): In a June 18, 2025 interview with Tucker Carlson, Sen. Cruz said: "Does Israel direct AIPAC? No, they're not lobbying on behalf of them." — [Algemeiner, June 18, 2025](https://www.algemeiner.com/2025/06/18/ted-cruz-defends-aipac-foreign-influence-claims-accuses-tucker-carlson-antisemitism/)

## 6. The February 5, 2025 Bondi memo: relevance

### Takeaway
The memo narrows criminal FARA prosecution to espionage-like conduct by foreign government actors and points the FARA Unit toward civil enforcement and guidance. For a domestic lobby, this makes criminal charges for advocacy unlikely under current DOJ priorities. It does not change the statute's registration test or rule out civil action.

### Cited Findings
- REPORTED: Criminal FARA and § 951 charges are limited to conduct "similar to more traditional espionage by foreign government actors." — [Skadden, Feb. 2025](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/02/attorney-general-announces-shift-in-foreign-agents)
- REPORTED: The memo shifts focus to "civil enforcement, regulatory initiatives and public guidance." — [Skadden, Feb. 2025](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/02/attorney-general-announces-shift-in-foreign-agents)
- REPORTED: Lawfare (former FARA Unit chief Van Grack et al., Jan. 28, 2026) says civil enforcement remains in place. — [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-state-of-fara)
