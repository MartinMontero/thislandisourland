# Fact-check: block id="aipac-fara" (dist/index.html), checked Nov 14, 2026

Method: WebFetch/WebSearch only, original or best primary source. research_notes/ and reports/ not used. Quotes are 14 words or fewer. WebFetch returns model-written summaries, so "exact" figures and quotes are as relayed by the fetch tool, not read byte for byte.

## 1. FARA test, "foreign principal", LDA exemption
Block: FARA "requires registration when someone acts at the order, request, direction or control of a foreign government or foreign political party." And: the LDA exemption "does not cover agents of foreign governments, according to the Justice Department's own FAQ."
VERDICT: PARTLY
Opened: https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/frequently-asked-questions
- DOJ FAQ: agent acts "at the order, request, or under the direction or control of a foreign principal". Confirmed.
- DOJ FAQ on definition: "A 'foreign principal' can be a foreign government, a foreign political party, any person outside the United States". So a foreign principal is NOT only a government or party; it also includes persons outside the US and entities organized abroad. The block's sentence swaps "foreign principal" for "foreign government or party".
- LDA exemption: FAQ says "The exemption does not apply where a foreign government or a foreign political party is the principal beneficiary" of the activities. Confirmed in substance. Block's "agents of foreign governments" is a loose paraphrase.
- Not opened: uscode.house.gov (22 U.S.C. 611) failed (client error).
Correction wording: "FARA requires registration when someone acts as an agent at the order, request, or under the direction or control of a foreign principal. A foreign principal can be a foreign government or political party, or a person or entity outside the United States." And: "The Justice Department's FAQ says the Lobbying Disclosure Act exemption does not apply where a foreign government or foreign political party is the principal beneficiary of the activities."

## 4. Massie bill
Block: "introduced a bill on May 14, 2026 that would let a U.S.-organized group qualify as a foreign principal if its lobbying 'principally' advances a foreign state's interests, without proof of foreign funding or instructions. It was referred to the House Judiciary Committee; we found no later action."
VERDICT: PARTLY (the quoted word "principally" is WRONG)
Opened: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr8809ih/xml/BILLS-119hr8809ih.xml (introduced text); https://www.quiverquant.com/bills/119/hr-8809; https://policysoup.com/bills/hr8809-119/activities; https://modernaction.io/bills/hr8809-119/action; https://www.govinfo.gov/link/crec/172/h/3531
- congress.gov could not be opened (robots_disallowed). Bill number H.R. 8809 confirmed via govinfo text and Congressional Record page (record date May 14, 2026).
- Introduced May 14, 2026 by Rep. Massie; referred to House Judiciary. Confirmed (quiverquant: both on May 14, 2026).
- Short title: "Americans Insist on Political Agent Clarity Act" ("AIPAC Act").
- Text search of the introduced bill: zero occurrences of "principally" or "advance" as a test word. The operative test is that a foreign country or state-affiliated entity "is identified as a principal beneficiary" of the lobbying, shown "by a preponderance of objective indicia". The lobbying or stated mission must seek, "wholly or partly", to influence US policy to advance that foreign interest. Indicia include repeated advocacy aligned with the country's goals, coordination with foreign officials, and material support or strategic direction from people abroad.
- Entity must be U.S.-organized and must NOT directly receive funding or instruction from a foreign government. So "without proof of foreign funding or instructions" is accurate in effect (the test replaces proof of direct funding or instruction).
- Later action: trackers show no action after referral and no cosponsors. Caveat: I could not open congress.gov, and the tracker pages are undated crawls, so "no later action found as of Nov 14" rests on third-party trackers.
Correction wording: "...would let a U.S.-organized group that does not directly receive foreign-government funding or instruction qualify as a foreign principal if a foreign country is shown, by a preponderance of objective indicia, to be a principal beneficiary of its lobbying." Remove the quotation marks around "principally".

## 7. 1962 DOJ request to American Zionist Council
Block: "In 1962 the Justice Department asked AIPAC's predecessor, the American Zionist Council, to register after documented funding from the Jewish Agency. Foreign funding triggered that request, not shared goals. It did not involve AIPAC itself."

Opened: https://israellobby.org/AZCDOJ (IRmep, an advocacy group, hosting scanned DOJ records; not a government or neutral source) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Zionist_Council

Correction wording: "In November 1962 the Justice Department ordered the American Zionist Council, AIPAC's predecessor organization, to register, citing its receipt of Jewish Agency funds. The Council did not register." Attribute to records published by the Institute for Research: Middle Eastern Policy.
