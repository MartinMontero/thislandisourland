# Gulf payments hold up; causation does not

The uploaded file's money trail mostly survives checking, and its causal story does not. Foreign governments did pay Trump businesses in 2017-2021, Saudi money did flow to Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners, lobbyists, intermediaries and arms sales are real, and Trump has been back in office since January 2025. But the file's central thesis, that payments produced policy outcomes ("a clear causal relationship"), is not established by any source the researchers read. No court finding, inspector general report, indictment, plea or jury verdict says a decision was made because of a payment. Barrack was acquitted on every count, the State Department inspector general found the 2019 emergency arms sales lawful, the Qatar blockade began before the Oval Office meetings the file blames, and the file's own phrase "in return, or in parallel" concedes that it can show timing only. Most of the file's numbers are real but come from Democratic minority staff or senators, not from "the House Oversight Committee" or "Senate investigators" as a body, and the file omits the Republican counter-report and the acquittals. Several claims are wrong as written: the Qatar venue, "sanctions were vetoed", the Trump quote (a composite of separate statements), "dismissed as moot following the 2020 election", the committee-and-title attributions on the Affinity inquiry, the 0% return figure, "payoff", Qorvis "media buys", and a "Chairman Wyden and Representative Garcia" Senate Finance inquiry. The file is also stale (it treats a second Trump term as hypothetical), and its works-cited list has many mismatches: 29 of 64 entries are never cited, and several markers point to the wrong source or to a source that predates the event. Many primary documents were read through summarising fetch tools, and some sources could not be opened at all; every stated limit is carried forward below. A second pass in Chrome on Nov. 13, 2026 read a number of the blocked sources directly; see "Second pass in Chrome".

## Where the evidence stops short of the thesis

The documented layer is payments, requests and proposals. By value, China, not the Gulf, dominates the $7.8 million: $5,572,548 (about 71 percent), of which at least $5,357,495 is an estimate of ICBC's rent for Trump Tower space, while the Saudi, Qatar and UAE rows total $1,146,391 (about 15 percent) ([Oversight Democrats](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/oversight-democrats-release-report-proving-trump-pocketed-millions-from-at-least)). The Saudi figure ($615,422) is itself an estimate: $537,080 for Trump World Tower, extrapolated from $134,270 shown for 2018, plus $78,342 for a Saudi defense-ministry delegation at the Washington hotel in March 2018 ([report copy](https://docs.house.gov/meetings/JU/JU00/20240110/116733/HMKP-118-JU00-20240110-SD004.pdf)). The hotel money in the March 2018 episode largely preceded the Crown Prince's March 20 visit, and the $1.3 billion sale the file says was "finalized" was the April 5 Paladin howitzer notification; about $1 billion in anti-tank missiles was notified March 22-23 ([Military.com](https://military.com/daily-news/2018/03/23/trump-administration-approves-1-billion-arms-sale-saudi-arabia.html); [PBS/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/trump-administration-approves-sale-of-1-3-billion-in-artillery-to-saudi-arabia)). The Democratic report itself (read directly in the second pass) does not assert that the UAE stays caused any policy outcome (it excludes Broidy's stays as unclear and ends by raising the "persistent question of whether" Trump's foreign policy furthered his business interests; the quoted words above were the first-pass summary's, not the report's), and for China says only that the payments "created conflicts of interest" ([report copy](https://docs.house.gov/meetings/JU/JU00/20240110/116733/HMKP-118-JU00-20240110-SD004.pdf)). The file's "Notes" column causal statements are therefore mostly the file's own; the exception is the China bank-sanctions line, which the Democrats' press release asserts (see E5).

The intermediaries section fails on chronology. The blockade started June 5, 2017; the Broidy and Nader Oval Office contacts the sources document are from October to December 2017; Broidy reportedly urged Trump to stay out of the dispute, and at Barrack's trial Treasury Secretary Mnuchin testified that Barrack's "position was clearly in support of Qatar" ([AP via Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2018/5/23/the-princes-the-president-and-the-fortune-seekers); [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/trump-ally-roundly-acquitted-in-foreign-lobbying-trial/)). The firing of Tillerson on March 13, 2018 is documented as something Nader urged, "but only at a good time, politically" ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90233994/how-a-secret-seychelles-meeting-signaled-the-uae-pull-in-trump-d-c)); Trump cited a "different mind-set" and the Iran deal ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/after-months-of-tension-tillersons-state-department-tenure-comes-to-abrupt-end)). No court record in the notes alleges that Nader, Broidy or Barrack caused the blockade or the firing. The file's conclusion that they "orchestrat[ed]" both is not verified, and as to the blockade is contradicted by the dates.

On arms and Khashoggi, the file's strongest documented points survive but with different wording. The $110 billion figure had no contracts behind it by one named analyst's account (Bruce Riedel of Brookings: "letters of interest or intent, but not contracts") ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-110-billion-arms-deal-to-saudi-arabia-is-fake-news/)). The Pentagon had Saudi Letters of Offer and Acceptance of about $14.5 billion by November 2018 per ABC ([ABC](https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/news/story/kushner-pushed-inflate-saudi-arms-deal-110-billion-59418244)). The emergency declaration's legality was called "open to question" by CRS ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/LSB10304.html)), but the State Department's own inspector general later found the sales "executed in accordance with the requirements" of the Arms Export Control Act while faulting the assessment of civilian-casualty risk ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2020/08/11/pompeo-saudi-arms-sales-inspector-general)). The file omits the Treasury sanctions on 17 individuals and Trump's November 20, 2018 statement calling the killing a "terrible" crime ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2018/11/20/donald-trumps-full-statement-on-jamal-khashoggi-killing)). "Actively shielded" is a characterisation the record partly supports: no sanctions on the Crown Prince, no halt to arms sales, Trump saying "maybe he did and maybe he didn't" about MBS's knowledge.

On nuclear policy, the first-term effort did not transfer a reactor or material. Energy Department Part 810 authorizations allowed preliminary work; the count is reported as two, six or seven depending on source ([Japan Times/Reuters](https://japantimes.co.jp/news/2019/03/28/world/trump-camp-oks-secret-nuclear-power-work-saudi-arabia-gives-nonproliferation-assurances); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/6/4/us-oked-nuclear-power-sharing-with-saudi-after-khashoggi-murder)). Barrack was not appointed envoy and was given no nuclear role ([Republican report](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-Y4_OV2-PURL-gpo123686/pdf/GOVPUB-Y4_OV2-PURL-gpo123686.pdf)). The sentence that safety was "seemingly subordinated to the profit motives of administration allies" is, in the file's own hedge, inference, and the notes call it a Democratic staff conclusion disputed by the Republican report.

Affinity is the file's strongest material, and it sits at the allegation tier. The $2 billion is a commitment, reported in April 2022 on the basis of PIF minutes in which the panel cited the "inexperience" of Affinity's management and the PIF board, led by the Crown Prince, overruled it days later ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2022/04/11/trump-saudi-kushner-mnuchin); [AlterNet summary of the NYT](https://www.alternet.org/2022/04/kushner)). The fee figures are Senate Democrats' estimates: "as much as $157 million" from foreign clients, $87 million from the Saudi government, through about mid-2024 ([Senate Finance](https://www.finance.senate.gov/ranking-members-news/wyden-investigation-of-kushner-firm-continues-new-letter-outlines-affinity-partners-fee-structure-lack-of-return-to-investors-questionable-deals-with-foreign-governments/)); the March 2026 letter relabels this 2021-2024 and adds an estimated $39 million from the Saudi government in 2025 and "more than $110 million" since 2021 ([Garcia-Wyden letter](https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/imo/media/doc/2026-03-19garciawydenlettertoaffinitypartnersrekushnerfundraisingfinal.pdf)). No DOJ or court finding of a FARA violation was found, and Affinity's and Kushner's FARA position was not retrieved.

## Conclusion

The file's documentary layer and its argument are separate things, and only the first survives. The payments, the lobbying contracts, the intermediaries' cases, the arms sales and vetoes, the Affinity commitment and the 2025-26 deals are real, and in the second term they are larger and more current than the file knows. But none of the sources read ties a specific payment to a specific decision: the closest candidates (Tillerson's firing, the arms emergency, the nuclear push, the blockade) each have a documented request or proposal, a documented decision, and either a different stated reason, a contrary official finding or a date order that rules the link out. The file's own concession, "in return, or in parallel," is where the evidence actually sits.

Two things would change this assessment. The first is a primary document that links a payment to a decision, which none of the sources read contains; the second is the unread material the researchers flagged (the original New York Times articles, the Barrack indictment and verdict sheet, the full Democratic nuclear reports, the Affinity filings). The Saudi 123 agreement now before Congress, the Wyden-Garcia fee estimates, the CREW suit over Kushner's envoy role and the Cole book are the live items; each is an allegation or a pending process, not a finding, and a careful writer should present them that way.
